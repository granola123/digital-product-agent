# Taste of Argentina — 7-Day Meal Plan & Recipe Card Bundle

One of a growing set of cuisine packs in the International Food Recipe Collection launch bundle. Digital download bundle (PDF + Notion). English content for the Etsy/global market.

**A note on ingredients:** Argentine home cooking runs on beef, and most of this week's shopping list lives in a regular supermarket. A few items are worth flagging honestly: **dulce de leche** (the caramel-like milk spread for the alfajores) and pre-made **empanada dough discs** ("tapas para empanadas," usually sold frozen) are easiest to find at a Latin American grocery store, though dulce de leche is now common in the international aisle of many regular supermarkets too. **Canned/jarred white hominy** (sometimes labeled "mote" or "pozole blanco") stands in for the dried white corn traditional Locro uses, since dried corn needs an overnight soak that doesn't fit a weeknight — swap it in if you can only find dried.

## Recipe Cards

All ingredient tables below are scaled for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 servings.

### 1. Bife de Chorizo with Chimichurri
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bife de chorizo or sirloin strip steak | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Coarse salt | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |
| Black pepper | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |
| Olive oil (for searing) | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Flat-leaf parsley, finely chopped | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Garlic clove, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Dried oregano | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Red pepper flakes | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Red wine vinegar | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Olive oil (for chimichurri) | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Water | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |

**Steps:**
1. Take the steak out of the fridge 20 minutes before cooking. Pat dry and season generously with salt and pepper on both sides.
2. Make the chimichurri: combine the parsley, garlic, oregano, red pepper flakes, vinegar, and water in a bowl. Whisk in the olive oil. Season with salt and let sit at room temperature at least 15 minutes so the flavors meld (it keeps well in the fridge up to a week).
3. Heat a heavy skillet or grill pan over high heat with the searing oil until just smoking.
4. Sear the steak 3–4 minutes per side for medium-rare, adjusting time for thickness and desired doneness, pressing gently for an even crust.
5. Rest the steak 5 minutes before slicing against the grain.
6. Spoon chimichurri generously over the steak and serve extra on the side.

### 2. Milanesa a la Napolitana
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beef top round, pounded thin | 180g | 360g | 540g | 720g |
| All-purpose flour | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Egg, beaten | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Breadcrumbs | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Garlic powder | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Salt &amp; pepper | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |
| Neutral oil (for frying) | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Tomato sauce | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Cooked ham slice | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Mozzarella, sliced or shredded | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Dried oregano | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Pound the beef cutlets to an even ½cm thickness between plastic wrap.
2. Set up a dredging station: flour seasoned with garlic powder, salt, and pepper; beaten egg; breadcrumbs.
3. Coat each cutlet in flour, then egg, then breadcrumbs, pressing to adhere.
4. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Fry the cutlets 2–3 minutes per side until golden and just cooked through. Drain on paper towels.
5. Preheat the oven broiler (or oven to 220°C/425°F). Arrange the fried cutlets on a baking sheet.
6. Spread tomato sauce over each cutlet, top with a slice of ham, then mozzarella, then a pinch of oregano.
7. Broil or bake 5–8 minutes until the cheese is melted and bubbling.
8. Serve hot — classically with french fries or a simple salad.

### 3. Empanadas de Carne
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Empanada dough discs (tapas) | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 |
| Ground beef | 120g | 240g | 360g | 480g |
| Onion, finely diced | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Neutral oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Beef stock or water | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Sweet paprika | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Ground cumin | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Dried oregano | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Red pepper flakes (optional) | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Hard-boiled egg, chopped | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Green olives, chopped | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Egg, beaten (for sealing/egg wash) | as needed | as needed | as needed | as needed |
| Salt | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |

**Steps:**
1. Heat a splash of oil in a skillet over medium heat. Cook the onion 5–7 minutes until soft and translucent.
2. Add the ground beef, breaking it up, and cook until browned.
3. Stir in the paprika, cumin, oregano, and chili flakes (if using), cooking 1 minute until fragrant.
4. Add the stock and simmer 5 minutes until moist but not soupy. Season with salt. Spread on a tray and let cool completely — hot filling will melt the dough.
5. Once cool, stir in the chopped hard-boiled egg and olives.
6. Lay out the empanada discs. Place a spoonful of filling just off-center on each.
7. Moisten the edge with a little water or egg wash, fold into a half-moon, and press to seal. Crimp in the traditional repulgue braid, or press with a fork.
8. Arrange on a lined baking sheet, brush with egg wash, and bake at 200°C/400°F for 18–22 minutes until golden.
9. Serve warm, with lime wedges if you like.

### 4. Locro (Weeknight-Shortcut Version)
*A note on this one:* traditional Locro starts with dried white corn soaked overnight. This version uses canned/jarred white hominy so it's actually doable on a weeknight — still simmered long enough to soak up a rich, meaty broth, just without the overnight lead time.

**Stew**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Neutral oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Canned white hominy (mote), drained | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Canned white beans, drained | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Beef stew meat, cubed | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Pork shoulder or bacon, cubed | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Fresh chorizo criollo, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Butternut squash, cubed | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Onion, diced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Sweet paprika | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Ground cumin | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Bay leaf | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Beef or chicken stock | 250ml | 500ml | 750ml | 1000ml |
| Salt | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |

**Salsa Criolla (topping)**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Onion, finely diced | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Red bell pepper, finely diced | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Red wine vinegar | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Olive oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Sweet paprika | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. In a large heavy pot, brown the beef and pork over medium-high heat in a little oil, in batches if needed. Remove and set aside.
2. Add the chorizo and onion to the same pot, cooking 5 minutes until the onion softens and the chorizo releases some fat.
3. Stir in the paprika and cumin, cooking 30 seconds until fragrant.
4. Return the beef and pork to the pot. Add the hominy, beans, squash, bay leaf, and stock. Bring to a boil.
5. Reduce heat and simmer, partially covered, 45–60 minutes, stirring occasionally and mashing some of the squash against the side of the pot, until thick and stew-like and the meat is tender.
6. Meanwhile, make the salsa criolla: combine the onion, bell pepper, vinegar, oil, and paprika in a small bowl. Let sit at room temperature to marinate.
7. Season the locro with salt. Serve in deep bowls topped with a spoonful of salsa criolla.

### 5. Sorrentinos de Jamón y Queso
**Dough**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All-purpose flour | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Egg | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Olive oil | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Salt | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |

**Filling**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ricotta cheese | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Cooked ham, finely chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Mozzarella, grated | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Parmesan, grated | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Egg yolk | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Ground nutmeg | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Salt &amp; pepper | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |

**To serve**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tomato sauce | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Butter (optional finish) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Grated parmesan | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Steps:**
1. Make the dough: mound the flour, make a well, and add the egg(s), olive oil, and salt. Gradually work the flour into the wet ingredients until a shaggy dough forms.
2. Knead 8–10 minutes until smooth and elastic. Wrap in plastic and rest at room temperature 30 minutes.
3. Meanwhile, make the filling: combine the ricotta, ham, mozzarella, parmesan, egg yolk, nutmeg, salt, and pepper in a bowl. Mix well and refrigerate until ready to use.
4. Roll the dough out thin (about 2mm), working in batches and keeping the rest covered.
5. Using a round cutter or a sorrentinos press (about 7cm), cut circles. Place a small spoonful of filling in the center of half the circles.
6. Top with a second circle, pressing out any air and sealing the edges firmly. Crimp with a fork.
7. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Cook the sorrentinos in batches 3–5 minutes, until they float and the pasta is tender.
8. Meanwhile, warm the tomato sauce with the butter in a pan.
9. Drain the sorrentinos, toss gently with the sauce, and serve with grated parmesan.

### 6. Pastel de Papa
**Mashed Potato Topping**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Potato, peeled and cubed | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Butter | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Milk | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Salt | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |

**Beef Filling**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ground beef | 120g | 240g | 360g | 480g |
| Onion, diced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Neutral oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Red bell pepper, diced (optional) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Sweet paprika | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Ground cumin | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Raisins | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Green olives, chopped | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Hard-boiled egg, sliced | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Beef stock or water | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Grated cheese (optional, for the top) | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |

**Steps:**
1. Boil the potato in salted water until fork-tender, about 15 minutes. Drain and mash with the butter and milk until smooth. Season with salt.
2. Meanwhile, heat a little oil in a skillet over medium heat. Cook the onion and bell pepper (if using) 5 minutes until soft.
3. Add the ground beef, breaking it up, and cook until browned.
4. Stir in the paprika, cumin, and stock, simmering 3–4 minutes until slightly thickened. Season with salt.
5. Off the heat, stir in the raisins and olives.
6. Preheat the oven to 200°C/400°F. Spread half the mashed potato in the bottom of a baking dish. Layer the beef filling over it, then arrange the sliced hard-boiled egg on top.
7. Cover with the remaining mashed potato, smoothing the top with a fork to create ridges (this helps it brown). Sprinkle with cheese if using.
8. Bake 20–25 minutes until the top is golden brown, finishing under the broiler for the last 2–3 minutes for more color.
9. Let rest 5 minutes before serving.

### 7. Guiso de Lentejas
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Neutral oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Brown or green lentils | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Cured Spanish-style chorizo, sliced | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Onion, diced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Red bell pepper, diced | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Carrot, diced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Potato, cubed | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Garlic clove, minced | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Sweet paprika | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Bay leaf | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Tomato purée | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Beef or vegetable stock | 300ml | 600ml | 900ml | 1200ml |
| Salt | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |

**Steps:**
1. Heat a little oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the chorizo and cook 2–3 minutes until it releases its fat and starts to brown.
2. Add the onion, bell pepper, and carrot, cooking 6–8 minutes until softened.
3. Add the garlic and paprika, cooking 30 seconds until fragrant.
4. Stir in the tomato purée and cook 1–2 minutes.
5. Add the lentils, potato, bay leaf, and stock. Bring to a boil.
6. Reduce heat and simmer, partially covered, 30–35 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the lentils are tender and the stew has thickened.
7. Season with salt and pepper. Remove the bay leaf.
8. Serve hot with crusty bread.

### 8. Alfajores de Maicena (Bonus)
*A note on this one:* these are the classic cornstarch-based alfajor — a shortbread that stays impossibly tender because of the cornstarch, sandwiched with dulce de leche, and rolled in coconut. Don't let them brown in the oven, or you'll lose the melt-in-the-mouth texture that makes them worth making.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Butter, softened | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Sugar | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Egg yolk | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Vanilla extract | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Cornstarch | 45g | 90g | 135g | 180g |
| All-purpose flour | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Baking powder | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Dulce de leche | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Shredded coconut, for coating | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |

**Steps:**
1. Beat the butter and sugar together until light and creamy. Add the egg yolk(s) and vanilla, beating until smooth.
2. Sift in the cornstarch, flour, and baking powder. Fold in with a spatula, then bring together into a smooth, soft dough with your hands.
3. Wrap and chill 30–60 minutes until firm enough to roll.
4. Preheat the oven to 180°C/350°F and line a baking sheet with parchment.
5. Roll the dough to about 6mm thick and cut into small rounds (about 4cm). Place on the baking sheet.
6. Bake 8–10 minutes — they should stay pale; don't let them brown. Cool completely on the tray (they're fragile while warm).
7. Sandwich pairs of cookies together with a generous spoonful of dulce de leche.
8. Roll the exposed dulce de leche edge in shredded coconut. Serve at room temperature; keeps in an airtight container for several days.
