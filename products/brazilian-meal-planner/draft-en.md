## Recipe Cards

All ingredient tables below are scaled for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 servings.

### 1. Feijoada (Weeknight-Simplified Black Bean & Pork Stew)
*A note on this one:* traditional feijoada simmers dried beans with several cuts of salted, smoked, and fresh pork for hours, sometimes days. This version swaps in canned black beans and two easy proteins (bacon + smoked sausage) to get the same smoky, hearty flavor onto the table on a weeknight — it's a real shortcut, not a full substitute for the weekend version, and we say so plainly.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Canned black beans, drained and rinsed | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Bacon, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Smoked sausage (linguiça or kielbasa), sliced | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Onion, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic clove, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Bay leaf | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Smoked paprika | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Chicken or beef stock | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Olive oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Salt and black pepper | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Orange, sliced (for serving, traditional) | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |

**Steps:**
1. Heat the olive oil in a pot over medium heat. Add the bacon and cook 3–4 minutes until it starts to render and crisp.
2. Add the sliced sausage and cook 3–4 minutes until browned.
3. Add the onion and garlic, cooking 4–5 minutes until softened.
4. Stir in the smoked paprika and bay leaf, cooking 30 seconds until fragrant.
5. Add the black beans and stock. Bring to a simmer.
6. Reduce heat and simmer, uncovered, 20–25 minutes, stirring occasionally and mashing a few beans against the side of the pot to thicken the broth.
7. Season with salt and pepper to taste. Discard the bay leaf.
8. Serve hot over rice, with orange slices alongside — the citrus cuts through the richness, the traditional way.

### 2. Moqueca de Peixe (Brazilian Coconut Fish Stew)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Firm white fish fillets (tilapia, cod, or snapper), cut into chunks | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Lime juice | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 tbsp | 4 tsp |
| Garlic clove, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Onion, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Red or green bell pepper, sliced | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Tomato, sliced | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Cilantro, chopped | 1 tbsp | 2 tbsp | 3 tbsp | 4 tbsp |
| Coconut milk | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Dendê (palm) oil or olive oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Salt | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Toss the fish chunks with the lime juice and a pinch of salt. Let sit 10 minutes while you prep the vegetables.
2. Heat the oil in a wide pot or skillet over medium heat. Add the onion, garlic, and bell pepper, cooking 4–5 minutes until softened.
3. Add the tomato and cook 2–3 minutes until it starts to break down.
4. Pour in the coconut milk and bring to a gentle simmer.
5. Nestle the fish into the sauce in a single layer. Cover and simmer 8–10 minutes, without stirring too much, until the fish is just cooked through and flakes easily.
6. Stir in half the cilantro and season with salt to taste.
7. Sprinkle with the remaining cilantro and serve hot over white rice, with farofa on the side if you have it.

### 3. Picanha na Chapa with Farofa (Pan-Seared Steak & Toasted Cassava Flour)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Picanha (top sirloin cap) or sirloin steak, sliced into 1-inch pieces | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Coarse salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Cassava flour (farinha de mandioca) | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Butter or bacon fat | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Onion, finely chopped (optional, for farofa) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Garlic clove, minced | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |

**Steps:**
1. Pat the steak dry and season generously with the coarse salt and black pepper. Let sit at room temperature 10 minutes.
2. Heat a heavy skillet or griddle (chapa) over high heat until very hot.
3. Sear the steak pieces 2–3 minutes per side for medium-rare, or to your liking. Remove to a plate and let rest 5 minutes.
4. Meanwhile, melt the butter (or bacon fat) in a separate skillet over medium heat. Add the onion (if using) and garlic, cooking 2–3 minutes until fragrant and golden.
5. Add the cassava flour and toast, stirring constantly, 4–5 minutes until golden and toasted, not raw-tasting.
6. Season the farofa with a pinch of salt.
7. Slice the rested steak against the grain and serve immediately alongside the warm farofa.

### 4. Frango à Passarinho (Garlic Fried Chicken Bites)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, boneless, cut into small bite-sized pieces | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Lime juice | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 tbsp | 4 tsp |
| Garlic clove, minced (for marinade) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| All-purpose flour, for dredging | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Vegetable oil, for frying | 200ml | 400ml | 600ml | 800ml |
| Garlic clove, minced (for finishing oil) | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Olive oil, for finishing | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 tbsp | 4 tsp |
| Parsley, chopped, for garnish | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 tbsp | 4 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. In a bowl, toss the chicken pieces with the lime juice, marinade garlic, salt, and pepper. Cover and marinate in the fridge at least 1 hour (or up to overnight).
2. Heat about 2cm of vegetable oil in a deep skillet or pot to 350°F (175°C).
3. Toss the marinated chicken pieces in the flour, shaking off excess.
4. Fry the chicken in batches, 4–5 minutes per batch, until deeply golden and cooked through. Drain on paper towels.
5. While the chicken fries, warm the finishing olive oil in a small pan over low heat. Add the finishing garlic and cook 1–2 minutes until just golden — watch closely, it burns fast.
6. Toss the hot fried chicken bites with the garlic oil and parsley.
7. Serve immediately, with lime wedges and a cold drink on the side — classic Brazilian bar food.

### 5. Bobó de Camarão (Shrimp in Cassava-Coconut Puree)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shrimp, peeled and deveined | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Fresh cassava/yuca root, peeled and cubed | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Onion, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic clove, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Tomato, chopped | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Cilantro, chopped | 1 tbsp | 2 tbsp | 3 tbsp | 4 tbsp |
| Coconut milk | 80ml | 160ml | 240ml | 320ml |
| Dendê (palm) oil or olive oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Salt | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Boil the cassava in salted water 20–25 minutes until very soft. Drain, reserving a little of the cooking water.
2. Mash or blend the cassava with a splash of the reserved cooking water until smooth and thick, like mashed potatoes.
3. Meanwhile, heat the oil in a pot over medium heat. Add the onion and garlic, cooking 4–5 minutes until softened.
4. Add the tomato and cook 2–3 minutes until it breaks down.
5. Season the shrimp with a pinch of salt, then add to the pot. Cook 2–3 minutes per side until just pink.
6. Stir in the cassava puree and coconut milk, mixing until smooth and creamy. Simmer 5 minutes.
7. Stir in the cilantro and season with salt to taste.
8. Serve hot over white rice.

### 6. Strogonoff de Frango (Brazilian Chicken Stroganoff)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken breast, cut into 1-inch cubes | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Red wine vinegar | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Garlic clove, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Salt and black pepper | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Butter or oil | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Onion, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Tomato sauce (passata) | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Ketchup | 1 tbsp | 2 tbsp | 3 tbsp | 4 tbsp |
| Dijon mustard | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Heavy cream | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Shoestring potato sticks (batata palha), for serving | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |

**Steps:**
1. Toss the chicken cubes with the red wine vinegar, salt, and pepper. Let sit 5–10 minutes while you prep the other ingredients.
2. Heat the butter in a large skillet over medium heat. Add the onion and cook 4–5 minutes until soft.
3. Add the garlic and cook 1 minute until fragrant.
4. Add the chicken and cook 5–6 minutes, stirring occasionally, until golden and cooked through.
5. Stir in the tomato sauce, ketchup, and mustard. Simmer 3–4 minutes until slightly thickened.
6. Lower the heat and stir in the cream, mixing gently. Warm through 2–3 minutes without letting it boil (boiling can break the sauce).
7. Season with salt to taste.
8. Serve hot over white rice, topped generously with shoestring potato sticks for crunch.

### 7. Galinhada (Brazilian Chicken and Rice)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bone-in or boneless, cut into pieces | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Long-grain white rice | 65g | 130g | 195g | 260g |
| Onion, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic clove, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Red bell pepper, chopped | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Tomato, chopped | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Ground turmeric | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Bay leaf | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Chicken stock or water | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Olive oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Salt and black pepper | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Season the chicken with salt and pepper. Heat the oil in a large pot or Dutch oven over medium-high heat and brown the chicken 3–4 minutes per side. Remove to a plate.
2. In the same pot, add the onion, garlic, and bell pepper, cooking 4–5 minutes until softened.
3. Stir in the tomato and turmeric, cooking 1–2 minutes until fragrant and the mixture is stained gold.
4. Add the rice and stir to coat every grain in the spiced oil, toasting 1 minute.
5. Return the chicken to the pot, add the bay leaf and stock, and bring to a boil.
6. Reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer 18–20 minutes, without lifting the lid, until the rice is tender and the liquid is absorbed.
7. Remove from heat and let rest, covered, 5 minutes. Discard the bay leaf, fluff with a fork, and season with salt to taste.
8. Serve hot, straight from the pot.

### 8. Brigadeiro (Brazilian Chocolate Truffles) (Bonus)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sweetened condensed milk | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Unsalted butter | 8g | 15g | 23g | 30g |
| Unsweetened cocoa powder | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Salt | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Chocolate sprinkles (granulado), for rolling | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |

**Steps:**
1. Combine the condensed milk, butter, cocoa powder, and salt in a saucepan over medium-low heat.
2. Cook, stirring constantly and scraping the bottom and sides, for 10–15 minutes, until the mixture thickens enough that a spatula dragged through it leaves the pan bottom visible for a second before the mixture flows back together.
3. Transfer to a buttered plate or shallow dish and let cool at room temperature 30 minutes, until firm enough to handle.
4. Lightly grease your hands with butter. Scoop about 1 teaspoon of the mixture and roll between your palms into a smooth ball.
5. Roll each ball in the chocolate sprinkles until fully coated.
6. Place in mini paper cups and serve at room temperature, or chill for a firmer bite.
