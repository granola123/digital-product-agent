## Recipe Cards

### 1. Fish and Chips
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cod fillet (skinless) | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Potatoes (for chips) | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Plain flour (for batter) | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Cornflour | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Baking powder | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Ground turmeric | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |
| Cold beer (or sparkling water) | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |
| Vegetable oil (for frying) | as needed | as needed | as needed | as needed |
| Dried marrowfat peas (for mushy peas) | 75g | 150g | 225g | 300g |
| Butter (for mushy peas) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Mint (chopped) | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |
| Sugar (for mushy peas) | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Malt vinegar (to serve) | as needed | as needed | as needed | as needed |
| Lemon wedge | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |

**Steps:**
1. Soak the dried marrowfat peas in cold water overnight (or use a quick-soak method following the packet). Drain before cooking.
2. Peel the potatoes and cut them into thick chip shapes. Rinse under cold water to wash off extra starch, then pat dry with a clean towel.
3. Ask an adult to help heat the vegetable oil in a deep pan or fryer to about 130°C (a low, gentle heat) — this first fry cooks the chips through without browning them yet. Fry the chips for 5–6 minutes until soft but pale, then lift them out and let them drain and cool.
4. In a bowl, whisk together the plain flour, cornflour, baking powder, turmeric, salt, and black pepper. Slowly pour in the cold beer, whisking as you go, until you have a smooth, lump-free batter about as thick as double cream. Turmeric is what gives fish and chip shop batter its golden color.
5. Turn the oil up to about 180°C (hot enough that a drop of batter sizzles and floats immediately). Dip each piece of cod into the batter, letting the extra drip off, then carefully lower it into the hot oil.
6. Fry the fish for 5–6 minutes, turning once, until the batter is deep golden and crisp and the fish is opaque and flakes easily with a fork. Lift out and drain on paper towels.
7. Turn the oil back up to 190°C and fry the part-cooked chips a second time for 2–3 minutes, until golden and crunchy on the outside. Drain and sprinkle with a little extra salt.
8. While the chips fry, warm the drained marrowfat peas in a small pot with a splash of water, the butter, chopped mint, and sugar. Mash roughly with a fork or potato masher until thick and a little chunky — this is "mushy peas."
9. Serve the fish and chips together with a scoop of mushy peas, a lemon wedge, and malt vinegar on the side for splashing over everything.

### 2. Shepherd's Pie
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lamb mince | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Onion (diced) | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Carrot (diced) | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Celery stalk (diced) | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Garlic (cloves) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Tomato purée | ½ tbsp | 1 tbsp | 1½ tbsp | 2 tbsp |
| Worcestershire sauce | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Lamb or beef stock | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Plain flour (to thicken) | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Frozen peas | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Dried thyme | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Oil (for browning) | as needed | as needed | as needed | as needed |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |
| Potatoes (for mash) | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Butter (for mash) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Milk (for mash) | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |

**Steps:**
1. Peel the potatoes, cut into even chunks, and put them in a pot of cold salted water. Ask an adult to help bring it to a boil, then simmer 15–18 minutes until a fork slides in easily.
2. While the potatoes cook, heat a splash of oil in a large pan over medium-high heat. Add the onion, carrot, and celery and cook 5 minutes, stirring, until softened.
3. Add the garlic and cook 30 seconds until you can smell it. Add the lamb mince, breaking it up with a spatula, and cook 5–6 minutes until no pink remains.
4. Sprinkle the flour over the mince and stir for 1 minute so it coats everything and loses its raw taste.
5. Stir in the tomato purée, Worcestershire sauce, stock, and thyme. Bring to a gentle simmer, then turn the heat down low and cook uncovered for 15 minutes, stirring occasionally, until thickened like a rich gravy.
6. Stir in the frozen peas and season with the salt and black pepper. Taste carefully and adjust. Spoon the mince into a baking dish and spread it level.
7. Drain the cooked potatoes and mash them with the butter and milk until smooth and fluffy. Season with a little more salt if needed.
8. Spoon the mash over the mince in an even layer, then run a fork over the top in swirls — this helps the peaks turn golden and crisp in the oven.
9. Ask an adult to help bake at 200°C (400°F) for 20–25 minutes until the top is golden brown and the filling is bubbling at the edges.

### 3. Bangers and Mash with Onion Gravy
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pork sausages | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Potatoes (for mash) | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Butter (for mash) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Milk (for mash) | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Onion (sliced) | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Plain flour (for gravy) | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Beef stock | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Worcestershire sauce | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Oil (for frying) | as needed | as needed | as needed | as needed |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Peel the potatoes, cut into chunks, and boil in salted water 15–18 minutes until fork-tender. Drain and mash with the butter and milk until smooth. Season with a little salt and keep warm.
2. While the potatoes cook, heat a little oil in a frying pan over medium heat. Add the sausages and cook, turning every couple of minutes, for 12–15 minutes total until browned all over and cooked through with no pink in the middle. Lift out and set aside, keeping warm.
3. Using the same pan (with the sausage fat still in it), add the sliced onion and a pinch of salt. Cook over medium-low heat for 10 minutes, stirring now and then, until soft and golden — don't rush this, slow cooking is what makes the onions sweet.
4. Sprinkle the flour over the onions and stir for 1 minute so it soaks up the fat and loses its raw, powdery taste.
5. Slowly pour in the beef stock, stirring constantly to smooth out any lumps. Add the Worcestershire sauce and season with black pepper.
6. Simmer the gravy 4–5 minutes, stirring, until it thickens enough to coat the back of a spoon.
7. Pile the mash onto plates, lean the sausages against it, and pour the onion gravy generously over the top.

### 4. Chicken Tikka Masala
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thigh (boneless) | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Plain yogurt (for marinade) | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Lemon juice | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Garlic (cloves, grated) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Fresh ginger (grated) | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Garam masala | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Ground turmeric | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Chili powder | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |
| Butter or ghee | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Onion (diced) | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Ground cumin | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Ground coriander | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Tomato passata | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Double cream | 40ml | 80ml | 120ml | 160ml |
| Oil (for cooking chicken) | as needed | as needed | as needed | as needed |
| Fresh cilantro (to garnish) | small handful | small handful | handful | handful |
| Basmati rice (dry, to serve) | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |

**Steps:**
1. In a bowl, mix the yogurt, lemon juice, half the garlic and ginger, half the garam masala, the turmeric, chili powder, and a pinch of salt. Add the chicken pieces and turn to coat evenly. Cover and marinate in the fridge at least 30 minutes (longer, even overnight, gives more flavor).
2. Ask an adult to help heat a splash of oil in a large pan over high heat. Add the marinated chicken pieces in a single layer and cook 3–4 minutes per side until charred at the edges and cooked through with no pink inside. Lift out and set aside.
3. In the same pan, melt the butter or ghee over medium heat. Add the onion and cook 5 minutes until soft and golden.
4. Add the remaining garlic and ginger, the cumin, coriander, and remaining garam masala. Stir for 30 seconds until fragrant — don't let the spices burn or they'll taste bitter.
5. Pour in the tomato passata and simmer 8–10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens and turns a deep red-orange.
6. Stir in the double cream and return the cooked chicken to the pan. Simmer 3–4 minutes so the chicken warms through and soaks up the sauce.
7. Taste and add a little more salt if needed. Scatter with fresh cilantro and serve over basmati rice.

### 5. Steak and Ale Pie
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Braising beef (chuck), cubed | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Plain flour (to coat beef) | 1 tbsp | 2 tbsp | 3 tbsp | 4 tbsp |
| Onion (diced) | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Carrot (diced) | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic (cloves) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Brown ale or stout | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Beef stock | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Tomato purée | ½ tbsp | 1 tbsp | 1½ tbsp | 2 tbsp |
| Worcestershire sauce | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Bay leaf | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Fresh thyme | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Oil (for browning) | as needed | as needed | as needed | as needed |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |
| Ready-rolled puff pastry | ¼ sheet | ½ sheet | ¾ sheet | 1 sheet |
| Egg (beaten, for egg wash) | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |

**Steps:**
1. Toss the beef cubes in the flour with a pinch of salt and pepper until lightly coated. Ask an adult to help heat a splash of oil in a large, heavy pot over high heat.
2. Brown the beef in batches (don't crowd the pot, or it will steam instead of browning), 2–3 minutes per batch, until deep brown on the outside. Set the beef aside.
3. Add a little more oil if needed, then add the onion and carrot. Cook 5 minutes until softened. Add the garlic and cook 30 seconds more.
4. Return the beef to the pot. Pour in the ale, scraping up any browned bits stuck to the bottom of the pot with a wooden spoon — that's flavor you don't want to lose.
5. Add the beef stock, tomato purée, Worcestershire sauce, bay leaf, and thyme. Bring to a gentle simmer.
6. Turn the heat down low, cover with a lid slightly ajar, and let it simmer gently for 1½ hours, stirring occasionally, until the beef is fork-tender and the sauce has thickened. Remove the bay leaf and taste, adjusting salt and pepper.
7. Spoon the cooled filling into a pie dish. Roll the puff pastry out to a size a little bigger than the dish and lay it over the top, trimming the excess and crimping the edges with a fork to seal.
8. Cut a small slit in the center of the pastry to let steam escape, then brush the top all over with the beaten egg.
9. Ask an adult to help bake at 200°C (400°F) for 25–30 minutes until the pastry is puffed and deep golden brown.

### 6. Toad in the Hole
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pork sausages | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Plain flour | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Eggs | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Milk | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |
| Vegetable oil or beef dripping (for the tin) | 1 tbsp | 2 tbsp | 3 tbsp | 4 tbsp |
| Onion (sliced, for gravy) | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Plain flour (for gravy) | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Beef stock (for gravy) | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Worcestershire sauce (for gravy) | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Butter or oil (for gravy) | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |

**Steps:**
1. Whisk the flour, eggs, milk, and a pinch of salt together in a bowl until you have a smooth, lump-free batter about the thickness of pouring cream. Let it rest at room temperature for 20–30 minutes — resting makes the pudding puff up better.
2. Ask an adult to help preheat the oven to 220°C (425°F). Pour the oil or dripping into a metal roasting tin and put it in the oven for 8–10 minutes until it's smoking hot — this is the secret to a crisp, well-risen pudding.
3. While the tin heats, brown the sausages in a separate frying pan over medium heat for about 5 minutes, turning them, just until golden on the outside (they'll finish cooking in the oven).
4. Carefully take the very hot tin out of the oven — the oil will be shimmering and hot, so be extra careful. Quickly place the browned sausages in the tin, then immediately pour the batter around them and slide it straight back into the oven.
5. Bake 25–30 minutes without opening the oven door (peeking lets the heat out and the batter can collapse) until the batter is puffed up dramatically and deep golden brown.
6. While it bakes, make the gravy: melt the butter or oil in a small pan over medium-low heat, add the sliced onion, and cook 8–10 minutes until soft and golden.
7. Stir in the flour and cook 1 minute, then slowly pour in the beef stock, stirring to smooth out lumps. Add the Worcestershire sauce and simmer 4–5 minutes until thickened.
8. Cut the toad in the hole into portions right away (it starts to sink as it cools) and serve immediately with the onion gravy poured over.

### 7. Sunday Roast Chicken with Roast Vegetables
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken (bone-in, skin-on pieces) | 400g | 800g | 1.2kg | 1.6kg |
| Butter (for the chicken) | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Lemon | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Garlic (cloves) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Fresh thyme | few sprigs | few sprigs | small bunch | small bunch |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |
| Potatoes (for roasting) | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Carrots | 75g | 150g | 225g | 300g |
| Parsnips | 75g | 150g | 225g | 300g |
| Oil (for the vegetables) | as needed | as needed | as needed | as needed |
| Plain flour (for gravy) | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Chicken stock (for gravy) | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help preheat the oven to 200°C (400°F). Pat the chicken pieces dry with a paper towel — dry skin crisps up better than damp skin.
2. Rub the chicken all over with the butter, then season generously with salt and pepper. Tuck the garlic cloves, lemon quarters, and thyme sprigs around the chicken in a roasting tin.
3. Peel the potatoes, carrots, and parsnips and cut into even chunks. Toss them in a separate roasting tin with a splash of oil and a pinch of salt.
4. Put both tins in the oven. Roast the chicken for about 45–55 minutes (a bit longer for larger amounts), until the skin is golden and the juices run clear with no pink when you cut into the thickest part.
5. Add the vegetable tin to the oven partway through (about 35–40 minutes before the chicken finishes), turning the vegetables once halfway, until they're golden and a fork slides in easily.
6. Once the chicken is done, lift it onto a plate to rest for 10 minutes — this keeps it juicy when you carve it. Keep the roasting tin with all the flavorful juices and bits.
7. Place the roasting tin over medium heat on the stove (ask an adult to help). Sprinkle in the flour and stir into the juices for 1 minute to make a paste.
8. Slowly pour in the chicken stock, stirring and scraping up the browned bits from the bottom of the tin, and simmer 3–4 minutes until it thickens into a gravy. Taste and season if needed.
9. Carve the rested chicken and serve with the roast vegetables and gravy poured over the top.

### 8. Sticky Toffee Pudding (Bonus)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pitted dates (chopped) | 75g | 150g | 225g | 300g |
| Bicarbonate of soda | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Boiling water | 90ml | 180ml | 270ml | 360ml |
| Butter (softened, for sponge) | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Dark brown sugar (for sponge) | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Eggs | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Self-raising flour | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Vanilla extract (for sponge) | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Butter (for toffee sauce) | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Dark brown sugar (for toffee sauce) | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Double cream | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Salt (for toffee sauce) | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Put the chopped dates and bicarbonate of soda in a heatproof bowl. Ask an adult to help pour the boiling water over them, then let the mixture sit for 10 minutes — it will look a little foamy, that's normal. This softens the dates so they melt into the sponge.
2. Ask an adult to help preheat the oven to 180°C (350°F) and grease a baking dish with a little butter.
3. In a separate bowl, beat the softened butter and dark brown sugar together until light and fluffy. Beat in the eggs one at a time, then stir in the vanilla extract.
4. Fold in the self-raising flour gently until just combined — don't overmix, or the sponge can turn tough.
5. Pour in the date mixture (including all the soaking liquid) and fold together gently until you have a smooth, fairly runny batter.
6. Pour the batter into the prepared dish and bake 30–35 minutes until risen and a skewer poked into the center comes out clean.
7. While it bakes, make the toffee sauce: melt the butter and dark brown sugar together in a small pot over medium-low heat, stirring until the sugar dissolves.
8. Pour in the double cream and a pinch of salt, and simmer gently for 3–4 minutes, stirring, until it turns a rich, glossy toffee color and coats the back of a spoon.
9. Poke a few holes in the warm sponge with a skewer and pour about a third of the warm toffee sauce over the top so it soaks in. Serve slices with the extra sauce poured over.
