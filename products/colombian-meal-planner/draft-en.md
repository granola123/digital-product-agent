## Grocery List (by category)

**Produce & Herbs:** onion (needed for nearly every dinner — buy in bulk), garlic, scallions (green onion), tomatoes, red bell pepper, green bell pepper, carrots, potatoes (a mix of yellow/Yukon gold, red, and russet if you can find them, for the ajiaco), yuca (cassava), green plantain, ripe (sweet) plantain, corn on the cob, avocado, fresh cilantro, dried guascas (a Colombian herb sold at Latin grocers or online — a mix of chopped cilantro and a little parsley is the closest stand-in if you can't find it)

**Protein:** ground beef (for bandeja paisa), chorizo (Colombian-style, or any fresh pork sausage), chicken thighs and/or drumsticks — buy in bulk (for ajiaco, sancocho, arroz con pollo, pollo sudado), flank steak (for sobrebarriga), eggs

**Grains, Legumes & Starch:** long-grain white rice (needed almost every night — buy a large bag), dried lentils, canned red kidney beans, arepas (small store-bought white-corn arepas, or masarepa if making your own), all-purpose flour

**Dairy & Pantry Staples:** milk, heavy/whipping cream, sweetened condensed milk, evaporated milk, butter (optional, for greasing), capers, ground cumin, achiote powder (or sweet paprika as a substitute), bay leaves, tomato paste, baking powder, granulated sugar, vanilla extract, ground cinnamon (optional), chicken stock or bouillon, cooking oil, salt, black pepper

## Recipe Cards

All ingredient tables below are scaled for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 servings.

### 1. Bandeja Paisa
Antioquia's legendary loaded platter — seasoned ground beef and chorizo, red beans simmered in hogao, rice, sweet plantain, a fried egg, and avocado, all served alongside a warm arepa. The definitive Colombian weekend feast, streamlined here with canned beans for a weeknight-friendly version.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ground beef | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Chorizo, sliced | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Canned red kidney beans, drained | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Onion, finely chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Tomato, chopped | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ground cumin | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Achiote powder (or paprika) | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| White rice | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Water, for rice | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Ripe (sweet) plantain, sliced | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Egg, fried | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Avocado, sliced | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Arepa (store-bought or premade), small | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Cilantro, chopped, for garnish | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Make the hogao: heat half the oil in a pan and sauté half the onion, half the garlic, and the tomato with the cumin and achiote until it breaks down into a thick sauce, about 8 minutes. Season with a little salt.
2. Stir the drained beans into half the hogao and simmer 10 minutes, mashing a few beans against the side of the pan to thicken the sauce. Keep warm.
3. In a separate pan, heat the remaining oil and sauté the remaining onion and garlic, then add the ground beef, breaking it up with a spoon, and cook until browned. Stir in the remaining hogao and simmer 5 minutes. Season with salt and pepper.
4. Pan-fry the chorizo slices in a dry skillet until browned on both sides, about 5 minutes.
5. Meanwhile, cook the rice: bring the water to a boil with a pinch of salt, add the rice, cover, and simmer 15 minutes until tender and the water is absorbed.
6. Pan-fry the plantain slices in a little oil over medium heat, 3–4 minutes per side, until caramelized and golden.
7. Fry the eggs in a lightly oiled pan to your liking (a soft yolk is traditional).
8. Warm the arepa in a dry pan or toaster.
9. Plate the rice, beans, ground beef, chorizo, plantain, fried egg, avocado, and arepa together on one platter. Scatter the cilantro over the top and serve immediately.

### 2. Ajiaco Bogotano
Bogotá's signature chicken and potato soup, thickened naturally as starchy potatoes break down while waxy ones hold their shape, and seasoned with guascas, the citrusy herb that gives ajiaco its unmistakable flavor. Ladled into bowls with rice, avocado, capers, and a swirl of cream.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs or breast, bone-in | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Chicken stock or water | 250ml | 500ml | 750ml | 1000ml |
| Yellow potato (or Yukon gold), diced | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Red potato, chunked | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Russet potato, chunked | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Corn on the cob, cut into pieces | ½ ear | 1 ear | 1½ ears | 2 ears |
| Scallions, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Dried guascas | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Heavy cream, to serve | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Capers, to serve | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 tbsp | 4 tsp |
| Avocado, sliced, to serve | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| White rice, to serve | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Put the chicken, stock, garlic, and half the scallions in a pot. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat and simmer 20 minutes until the chicken is cooked through.
2. Lift out the chicken, let it cool slightly, then shred the meat and set aside. Return any bones to the pot to keep simmering for extra flavor, if you like, then discard before serving.
3. Add the russet potato (the starchy one) to the pot first and simmer 10 minutes so it starts to break down.
4. Add the yellow and red potatoes and the corn pieces, and simmer 15–20 minutes more, stirring occasionally, until the broth is thickened and all the potatoes are tender.
5. Stir in the guascas and the remaining scallions and simmer 5 minutes.
6. Return the shredded chicken to the pot to heat through. Season with salt and pepper.
7. Ladle into bowls and serve with a spoonful of cream, a scatter of capers, sliced avocado, and rice alongside.

### 3. Sancocho de Pollo
A hearty one-pot chicken stew loaded with yuca, green plantain, corn, and potato — the dish that turns any Sunday into a family gathering, and just as welcome on a weeknight. The starches slowly thicken the broth into something halfway between a soup and a stew.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs or drumsticks, bone-in | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Water or chicken stock | 300ml | 600ml | 900ml | 1200ml |
| Yuca (cassava), peeled and chunked | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Green plantain, chunked | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Corn on the cob, cut into pieces | ½ ear | 1 ear | 1½ ears | 2 ears |
| Potato, chunked | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Scallions, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Tomato, chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ground cumin | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Cilantro, chopped, for garnish | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| White rice, to serve | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Avocado, to serve | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. In a large pot, combine the chicken, water, scallions, tomato, garlic, and cumin. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat and simmer 15 minutes.
2. Add the yuca and simmer 10 minutes, since it takes the longest to soften.
3. Add the green plantain, potato, and corn pieces. Cover and simmer 20–25 minutes more, until the chicken is falling-tender and the yuca and potato are soft.
4. Season with salt and pepper.
5. Ladle into deep bowls, scatter with cilantro, and serve with rice and avocado alongside.

### 4. Arroz con Pollo
Colombian-style chicken and rice, the sauce colored a warm gold with achiote and simmered with carrot and peas until the rice soaks up every bit of the seasoned broth — a true one-pot weeknight staple in Colombian homes.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bone-in or boneless, cut into pieces | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Onion, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Red bell pepper, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Green bell pepper, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Tomato paste | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 tbsp | 4 tsp |
| Ground cumin | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Achiote powder (or paprika) | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Bay leaf | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Long-grain white rice | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Chicken stock | 120ml | 240ml | 360ml | 480ml |
| Carrot, diced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Peas | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Heat the oil in a wide pot over medium-high heat. Season the chicken with salt and pepper and brown it on all sides, about 6 minutes. Remove and set aside.
2. In the same pot, sauté the onion, garlic, red pepper, and green pepper until softened, about 5 minutes.
3. Stir in the tomato paste, cumin, achiote, and bay leaf, and cook 1 minute until fragrant.
4. Add the rice and stir to coat it in the paste, toasting it for 1–2 minutes.
5. Return the chicken to the pot, add the stock and carrot, and bring to a boil. Reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer 18–20 minutes, until the rice is tender and the liquid is absorbed.
6. Stir in the peas, cover, and let sit off the heat for 5 minutes to steam through.
7. Fluff with a fork, remove the bay leaf, and serve hot.

### 5. Sobrebarriga en Salsa Criolla
Flank steak simmered low and slow until fall-apart tender, then sliced and folded through a bright Creole sauce of tomato, onion, and achiote — a Bogotá Sunday-table classic that's mostly hands-off simmering time.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flank steak (sobrebarriga), whole piece | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Water or beef stock, for braising | 300ml | 600ml | 900ml | 1200ml |
| Onion, chopped, divided | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Garlic cloves, minced, divided | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Bay leaf | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Scallions, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Tomato, chopped | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Achiote powder (or paprika) | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Ground cumin | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| White rice, to serve | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Potatoes, boiled, to serve | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Put the flank steak in a pot with the water, half the onion, half the garlic, and the bay leaf. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat, cover, and simmer 1½ hours, until fork-tender (or 45 minutes in a pressure cooker).
2. Lift out the meat and let it cool slightly, then slice it against the grain or shred it into bite-sized pieces. Strain and reserve the broth.
3. Heat the oil in a pan and sauté the remaining onion, garlic, and the scallions until soft.
4. Add the tomato, achiote, and cumin, and cook 8–10 minutes, mashing the tomato down, until you have a thick sauce.
5. Add the sliced meat and a splash of the reserved broth to the sauce and simmer 10 minutes so the meat absorbs the flavor. Season with salt and pepper.
6. Serve hot over rice with boiled potatoes on the side.

### 6. Lentejas Guisadas
Colombian stewed lentils, built on the same tomato-onion hogao base that anchors so much of the country's cooking, simmered with carrot and potato until thick and comforting — a lighter, meat-free night that's on the table in half an hour.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dried lentils, rinsed | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Water or vegetable stock | 250ml | 500ml | 750ml | 1000ml |
| Onion, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Scallions, chopped | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Tomato, chopped | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Carrot, diced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Potato, diced | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Ground cumin | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Cilantro, chopped, for garnish | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| White rice, to serve | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Heat the oil in a pot and sauté the onion, garlic, and scallions until soft, about 4 minutes.
2. Add the tomato and cumin and cook 5 minutes, mashing the tomato down, until it forms a thick hogao base.
3. Stir in the lentils, water, carrot, and potato. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat, cover, and simmer 25–30 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the lentils are tender and the stew has thickened.
4. Season with salt and pepper.
5. Scatter with cilantro and serve hot over rice.

### 7. Pollo Sudado
"Sweated chicken" — bone-in chicken braised low and slow in a tomato-pepper sauce until it's fall-off-the-bone tender, with potato simmered right in the same pot. Simple, homey, and the dish most Colombian families know by heart.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs or drumsticks, bone-in | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Onion, chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Red bell pepper, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Tomato, chopped | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Ground cumin | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Achiote powder (or paprika) | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Potato, chunked | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Scallions, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Chicken stock or water | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Cilantro, chopped, for garnish | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| White rice, to serve | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Heat the oil in a wide pan and sauté the onion, red pepper, garlic, and scallions until softened, about 5 minutes.
2. Add the tomato, cumin, and achiote, and cook 5 minutes, mashing the tomato down, until it forms a sauce.
3. Season the chicken with salt and pepper and nestle it into the sauce, turning to coat and browning lightly on both sides, about 5 minutes.
4. Add the potato and stock. Cover and simmer 25–30 minutes, until the chicken is very tender and the potato is cooked through.
5. Taste and adjust the seasoning.
6. Scatter with cilantro and serve hot over rice.

### 8. Tres Leches Cake (Bonus)
A light sponge cake soaked through with a mixture of three milks until every bite is soft and milky-sweet, finished with a billow of whipped cream — the cake on every Colombian birthday table, and the natural sweet finish to the week.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All-purpose flour | 35g | 70g | 105g | 140g |
| Baking powder | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Salt | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Egg, separated | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Granulated sugar | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Milk, for batter | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Vanilla extract | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Sweetened condensed milk, for soak | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Evaporated milk, for soak | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Heavy cream, for soak | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Heavy cream, whipped, for topping | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Granulated sugar, for topping | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Ground cinnamon, for garnish, optional | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Preheat the oven to 175°C (350°F). Grease a small baking dish.
2. Whisk the egg yolks and sugar together until pale and thick, then whisk in the milk and vanilla.
3. Sift the flour, baking powder, and salt over the yolk mixture and fold in gently until just combined.
4. In a clean bowl, whisk the egg whites to soft peaks, then fold them into the batter in two additions, keeping it light.
5. Pour the batter into the prepared dish and bake 22–25 minutes, until golden and a skewer inserted in the center comes out clean. Let cool completely.
6. Whisk together the condensed milk, evaporated milk, and heavy cream for the soak.
7. Poke holes all over the cooled cake with a skewer and slowly pour the three-milk mixture over so it soaks all the way through. Refrigerate at least 1 hour (overnight is best).
8. Whip the topping cream with the sugar to soft peaks and spread it over the soaked cake.
9. Dust with a little cinnamon if you like, and serve chilled.
