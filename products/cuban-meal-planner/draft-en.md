## Grocery List (by category)

**Produce & Herbs:** onion (needed for nearly every dinner — buy in bulk), garlic, green bell pepper, red bell pepper, fresh tomatoes, lime, sweet ripe plantains (maduros), fresh or frozen corn kernels (for tamales masa), corn on the cob (for caldosa), malanga (taro), yuca (cassava), boniato (Cuban sweet potato, or regular sweet potato)

**Protein:** ground beef (for picadillo), flank or skirt steak (for ropa vieja and vaca frita — buy in bulk), chicken thighs — a mix of bone-in skin-on (for arroz con pollo and caldosa), pork stew meat or smoked ham, ground pork (for tamale filling), chorizo or Spanish-style sausage, bacon or salt pork, eggs

**Grains, Legumes & Starch:** white or long-grain rice (needed several times — buy in bulk), canned black beans, cornmeal (masa harina or fine cornmeal, for tamales), dried or fresh corn husks, thin soup noodles (fideos)

**Dairy & Pantry Staples:** sweetened condensed milk, evaporated milk, milk, butter or lard, tomato sauce, canned crushed tomatoes, tomato paste, pimiento-stuffed green olives, capers, raisins, dry white wine (optional), sour orange juice (or extra lime + a splash of orange juice), ground cumin, dried oregano, sweet paprika, ground annatto/achiote (or substitute paprika), bay leaves, granulated sugar, vanilla extract, chicken stock (or bouillon), beef stock (or bouillon), cooking oil, salt, black pepper

## Recipe Cards

All ingredient tables below are scaled for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 servings.

### 1. Picadillo
Cuba's everyday beef hash — ground beef simmered down with tomatoes, briny green olives, sweet raisins, and a spoonful of capers, spooned over white rice. Sweet, salty, and savory all in one skillet, on the table in half an hour.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ground beef | 120g | 240g | 360g | 480g |
| Onion, finely chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Green bell pepper, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Oil, for sautéing | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Canned crushed tomatoes | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Tomato paste | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Dry white wine or beef stock | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Green olives, sliced | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Raisins | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Capers | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Ground cumin | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Dried oregano | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Bay leaf | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| White rice, to serve | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Ripe plantain, sliced, for frying (optional) | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Oil, for frying plantains | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Heat the oil in a skillet and sauté the onion and green pepper until soft, about 5 minutes.
2. Add the garlic and cook 30 seconds until fragrant.
3. Add the ground beef, breaking it up with a spoon, and cook until browned.
4. Stir in the crushed tomatoes, tomato paste, wine (or stock), olives, raisins, capers, cumin, oregano, and bay leaf.
5. Simmer uncovered 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until thickened.
6. Season with salt and pepper, and remove the bay leaf.
7. Meanwhile, cook the rice according to package directions.
8. If using, fry the plantain slices in the oil in a separate pan, 2–3 minutes per side, until golden.
9. Serve the picadillo over rice with the fried plantains alongside.

### 2. Moros y Cristianos with Maduros
Cuba's classic black beans and rice — "Moors and Christians" — cooked together in one pot with a little bacon and sofrito until the rice turns a deep purple-brown, finished with sweet caramelized fried plantains on top. (Note: further east in Cuba this same style of dish is often called congrí instead, usually made with red beans rather than black — the name and the bean color travel together depending on which end of the island you're in.)

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Canned black beans, undrained | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Bacon or salt pork, diced | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Onion, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Green bell pepper, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ground cumin | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Dried oregano | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Bay leaf | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Long-grain white rice | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Chicken stock or water | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Oil, for the pot | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Ripe plantain (maduro), sliced | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Oil, for frying plantains | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Heat the oil in a pot and cook the bacon until it renders its fat and starts to crisp.
2. Add the onion and green pepper and sauté until soft, then add the garlic and cook 30 seconds.
3. Stir in the cumin, oregano, and bay leaf.
4. Add the black beans (with their liquid), rice, and stock. Stir to combine.
5. Bring to a boil, cover, reduce the heat to low, and simmer 18–20 minutes until the rice is tender and the liquid is absorbed. Let it rest, covered, 5 minutes.
6. Season with salt and pepper, remove the bay leaf, and fluff with a fork.
7. Meanwhile, fry the plantain slices in the oil in a separate pan, 2–3 minutes per side, until golden and caramelized.
8. Serve the congrí topped with the fried plantains.

### 3. Arroz con Pollo
Chicken and rice cooked together in one pot the Cuban way — a golden, annatto-tinted sofrito base, a splash of wine, and a scatter of peas and pimientos stirred in at the end.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bone-in, skin-on | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Salt & black pepper, for chicken | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Onion, chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Green bell pepper, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ground annatto (achiote) or paprika | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Ground cumin | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Dried oregano | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Tomato sauce | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Dry white wine, optional | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Long-grain rice | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Chicken stock | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Frozen peas | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Jarred pimientos, sliced, for garnish | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |

**Steps:**
1. Season the chicken with salt and pepper. Heat the oil in a large pot or deep skillet, brown the chicken on both sides, then remove and set aside.
2. In the same pot, sauté the onion and green pepper until soft, then add the garlic and cook 30 seconds.
3. Stir in the annatto (or paprika), cumin, and oregano, and cook 30 seconds until fragrant.
4. Add the tomato sauce and wine, if using, and scrape up any browned bits from the bottom of the pot.
5. Add the rice and stir to coat it in the sofrito.
6. Return the chicken to the pot and pour in the stock. Bring to a boil.
7. Cover, reduce the heat, and simmer 20–25 minutes until the rice is tender and the chicken is cooked through.
8. Stir in the peas during the last 5 minutes of cooking.
9. Season to taste, garnish with the sliced pimientos, and serve.

### 4. Ropa Vieja
Cuba's national dish: flank steak simmered until fall-apart tender, shredded, then finished in a sofrito of onions, peppers, and tomato until every strand is coated in a rich, savory sauce. Named "old clothes" for the way the shredded meat looks in the pot.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flank or skirt steak | 180g | 360g | 540g | 720g |
| Beef stock or water, for boiling | 300ml | 600ml | 900ml | 1200ml |
| Bay leaf, for boiling | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Onion, halved, for boiling | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Onion, sliced, for sofrito | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Green bell pepper, sliced | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Red bell pepper, sliced | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Canned crushed tomatoes | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Tomato paste | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Dry white wine, optional | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Ground cumin | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Dried oregano | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Sweet paprika | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Pimiento-stuffed green olives | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| White rice, to serve | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |

**Steps:**
1. Place the steak, stock (or water), bay leaf, and halved onion in a pot. Bring to a boil, then reduce the heat, cover, and simmer 1–1.5 hours (mostly hands-off) until the meat is fork-tender.
2. Remove the steak and let it cool slightly, then shred it with two forks along the grain. Reserve 120ml of the cooking liquid; discard the bay leaf and boiled onion.
3. Heat the oil in a large skillet and sauté the sliced onion and both peppers until soft, about 8 minutes.
4. Add the garlic and cook 30 seconds until fragrant.
5. Stir in the cumin, oregano, and paprika, and cook 30 seconds more.
6. Add the crushed tomatoes, tomato paste, wine (if using), and the reserved cooking liquid.
7. Add the shredded beef and olives, and stir to combine.
8. Simmer uncovered 15–20 minutes until the sauce thickens and coats the meat.
9. Season with salt and pepper.
10. Serve hot over white rice.

### 5. Vaca Frita
"Fried cow" — beef boiled until tender, shredded, marinated in garlic and citrus, then seared hard in a hot skillet until the edges turn crisp and caramelized, topped with a tangle of onions.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flank or skirt steak | 180g | 360g | 540g | 720g |
| Beef stock or water, for boiling | 300ml | 600ml | 900ml | 1200ml |
| Bay leaf, for boiling | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Onion, halved, for boiling | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Garlic cloves, minced, for marinade | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Lime juice | 1 tbsp | 2 tbsp | 3 tbsp | 4 tbsp |
| Sour orange juice, or extra lime juice | 1 tbsp | 2 tbsp | 3 tbsp | 4 tbsp |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Oil, for frying | 1 tbsp | 2 tbsp | 3 tbsp | 4 tbsp |
| Onion, thinly sliced, for topping | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Lime wedges, to serve | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |

**Steps:**
1. Place the steak, stock (or water), bay leaf, and halved onion in a pot. Bring to a boil, then reduce the heat, cover, and simmer 1–1.5 hours (mostly hands-off) until fork-tender.
2. Remove the steak, let it cool slightly, then shred it with two forks. Discard the bay leaf and boiled onion.
3. Toss the shredded beef with the minced garlic, lime juice, sour orange juice, salt, and pepper. Let it marinate 15–20 minutes.
4. Heat the oil in a large skillet until shimmering. Add the beef in a single layer, pressing it down, and fry undisturbed 4–5 minutes until crisp and browned at the edges.
5. Toss and fry 3–4 minutes more, until well-crisped throughout.
6. In the same pan (or a separate one), quickly sauté the sliced onion 2–3 minutes until just softened but still with some bite.
7. Serve the beef topped with the onions and lime wedges.

### 6. Caldosa Cubana
A big, hearty one-pot Cuban stew-soup loaded with chicken, pork, chorizo, root vegetables, and thin noodles in a rich, deeply savory broth — the kind of dish Cuban families make in an enormous pot to feed a crowd on a weekend.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bone-in | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Pork stew meat, cubed | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Chorizo or Spanish-style sausage, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Water or stock | 500ml | 1000ml | 1500ml | 2000ml |
| Onion, chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Green bell pepper, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Tomato, chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Malanga (taro), peeled & cubed | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Yuca (cassava), peeled & cubed | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Boniato or sweet potato, cubed | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Calabaza or butternut squash, cubed | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Corn on the cob, cut into chunks | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Green plantain, sliced | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Thin soup noodles (fideos) | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Ground cumin | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Dried oregano | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Lime juice, to finish | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 tbsp | 4 tsp |
| Oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Heat the oil in a large pot and brown the chicken and pork in batches; remove and set aside.
2. Add the chorizo, onion, and green pepper to the pot and sauté until softened, then add the garlic and cook 30 seconds.
3. Return the chicken and pork to the pot, add the water (or stock), tomato, cumin, and oregano. Bring to a boil, skim off any foam, then reduce the heat, cover, and simmer 40 minutes.
4. Add the malanga, yuca, and boniato, and simmer 15 minutes more.
5. Add the calabaza, corn, and plantain, and simmer another 15 minutes until all the vegetables are tender.
6. Stir in the fideos and cook 5–8 minutes until just tender.
7. Season with salt and pepper, and finish with a squeeze of lime juice.
8. Ladle into bowls and serve hot with crusty bread.

### 7. Tamales Cubanos
Fresh corn masa studded with a savory pork filling, wrapped in softened corn husks and tied into neat parcels, then steamed until firm — a Sunday project worth the wait.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dried corn husks (or extra strips for tying) | 8 | 16 | 24 | 32 |
| Fresh or frozen corn kernels, pureed | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Cornmeal (masa harina or fine cornmeal) | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Milk | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Butter or lard, melted | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Sugar | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Salt, for masa | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Ground pork, for filling | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Onion, finely chopped, for filling | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Garlic cloves, minced, for filling | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Green bell pepper, finely chopped, for filling | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Tomato sauce, for filling | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Ground cumin, for filling | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Dried oregano, for filling | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Oil, for filling | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Salt & black pepper, for filling | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Soak the corn husks in warm water until pliable, about 30 minutes.
2. Make the filling: heat the oil in a skillet, sauté the onion and green pepper until soft, then add the garlic and cook 30 seconds.
3. Add the ground pork, breaking it up, and cook until browned. Stir in the tomato sauce, cumin, oregano, salt, and pepper. Simmer 5 minutes, then remove from the heat and let cool slightly.
4. Make the masa: blend the pureed corn, cornmeal, milk, melted butter, sugar, and salt into a thick batter.
5. Lay a softened corn husk flat, spoon a portion of the masa into the center, and spread it into a rectangle. Add a spoonful of the pork filling down the middle.
6. Fold the sides of the husk over the filling, then fold the ends up to enclose it, and tie with a strip of husk (or twine).
7. Repeat with the remaining husks, masa, and filling.
8. Stand the tamales upright (or lay flat) in a steamer basket over simmering water. Cover and steam 50–75 minutes, until the masa is firm and pulls cleanly from the husk.
9. Let rest 5 minutes before unwrapping and serving hot.

### 8. Flan de Leche (Bonus)
A silky caramel custard, Cuba's most beloved dessert — sweetened condensed and evaporated milk baked low and slow in a water bath, then unmolded to let the deep amber caramel pool over the top.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Granulated sugar, for caramel | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Water, for caramel | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 tbsp | 4 tsp |
| Eggs | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Sweetened condensed milk | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Evaporated milk | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Vanilla extract | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Salt | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Preheat the oven to 160°C (325°F).
2. Make the caramel: in a small saucepan, heat the sugar and water over medium heat without stirring until it turns a deep amber, 5–8 minutes. Immediately pour into ramekins (or one baking dish), swirling to coat the bottom.
3. Whisk the eggs, condensed milk, evaporated milk, vanilla, and salt together until smooth.
4. Pour the custard over the caramel in the ramekins.
5. Place the ramekins in a larger baking dish and add hot water to come halfway up their sides (a water bath).
6. Bake 35–45 minutes, until the custard is just set but still slightly wobbly in the center.
7. Let cool, then refrigerate at least 3 hours (overnight is best).
8. Run a knife around the edge and invert onto a plate to unmold, letting the caramel sauce pour over the top.
