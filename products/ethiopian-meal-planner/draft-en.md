# Taste of Ethiopia — 7-Day Meal Plan & Recipe Card Bundle

One of a growing set of cuisine packs in the International Food Recipe Collection launch bundle. Digital download bundle (PDF + Notion). English content for the Etsy/global market.

**A note on ingredients:** Ethiopian cooking leans on a few ingredients that most regular supermarkets don't stock — **berbere** (the fiery red spice blend that flavors nearly every wat/stew here), **niter kibbeh** (Ethiopian spiced clarified butter), and **teff flour** (for injera). All three are usually easy to find at an African/international grocery store or online, and berbere + niter kibbeh can be made at home from common pantry spices (both linked in the grocery list below) if you'd rather not special-order. We're flagging this honestly rather than pretending every ingredient here lives on a regular supermarket shelf — it doesn't, and pretending otherwise sets buyers up to be annoyed mid-shop.

## Grocery List (by category)

**Produce & Aromatics:** red onions (a lot — Ethiopian wats build their whole flavor base on slow-cooked onion), garlic, fresh ginger, green bell pepper or jalapeño, tomatoes, green cabbage, carrots, potatoes, collard greens, lemon (optional finishing squeeze)

**Protein:** chicken thighs (bone-in or boneless, skin removed — for Doro Wat), beef sirloin or flank (for Tibs), beef stew meat/chuck (for Key Wat), eggs (hard-boiled, for Doro Wat)

**Grains, Legumes & Flours:** red split lentils (Misir Wat), chickpea flour — sometimes sold pre-spiced as "shiro powder" (Shiro Wat), teff flour (Injera — specialty/international grocery item), all-purpose flour (Injera shortcut batter)

**Dairy & Fats:** niter kibbeh (Ethiopian spiced clarified butter — specialty item, or make your own from butter + whole spices, see recipe note) or substitute plain unsalted butter, neutral vegetable oil

**Spices, Pastes & Pantry:** berbere spice blend (specialty item — or blend your own from paprika, chili powder, fenugreek, cardamom, coriander, cumin, cloves, cinnamon, and ginger), ground cumin, ground turmeric, tomato paste, instant yeast, baking soda, apple cider vinegar or white vinegar, salt

## Recipe Cards

All ingredient tables below are scaled for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 servings.

### 1. Misir Wat (Spiced Red Lentil Stew)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Red split lentils | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Red onion, chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Garlic clove, minced | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Ginger, grated | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Berbere spice blend | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Tomato paste | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Vegetable oil or niter kibbeh | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Vegetable stock or water | 200ml | 400ml | 600ml | 800ml |
| Salt | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Warm the oil (or niter kibbeh) in a pot over medium heat. Add the onion and cook 8–10 minutes, stirring, until soft and golden.
2. Add the garlic and ginger and cook 1 minute until fragrant.
3. Stir in the berbere and tomato paste, cooking 1–2 minutes until deep red and fragrant — this blooms the spices, don't rush it.
4. Rinse the lentils, then add them to the pot along with the stock or water. Bring to a boil.
5. Reduce heat and simmer, uncovered, 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the lentils are fully soft and the stew has thickened to a thick, spoonable consistency.
6. Season with salt to taste. Add a splash more water if it's thicker than you like.
7. Serve warm with injera, or over rice as a shortcut.

### 2. Tibs (Sautéed Beef with Peppers & Onions)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beef sirloin, cut into thin strips | 130g | 260g | 390g | 520g |
| Salt | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Onion, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Green bell pepper or jalapeño, sliced | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Garlic clove, minced | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Ginger, grated | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Berbere spice blend | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Niter kibbeh or butter | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Rosemary sprig (optional, traditional) | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |

**Steps:**
1. Pat the beef strips dry and season with salt and pepper.
2. Heat the niter kibbeh (or butter) in a skillet over high heat until shimmering.
3. Sear the beef 2–3 minutes, tossing occasionally, until browned on the outside but still pink in the middle. Remove to a plate.
4. In the same skillet, add the onion and pepper, cooking 4–5 minutes until softened and lightly charred in spots.
5. Add the garlic, ginger, and rosemary (if using), cooking 1 minute until fragrant.
6. Stir in the berbere and a pinch of salt.
7. Return the beef (and any juices) to the skillet and toss 2–3 minutes until everything is coated and the beef is cooked to your liking.
8. Serve hot, straight from the pan, with injera or flatbread.

### 3. Shiro Wat (Spiced Chickpea-Flour Stew)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chickpea flour (or shiro powder) | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Onion, finely chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic clove, minced | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Tomato, chopped | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Berbere spice blend | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Vegetable oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Water | 200ml | 400ml | 600ml | 800ml |
| Salt | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Heat the oil in a saucepan over medium heat. Add the onion and cook 5 minutes until soft and translucent.
2. Add the garlic and cook 1 minute until fragrant.
3. Add the chopped tomato and berbere, cooking 2–3 minutes until the tomato breaks down and the mixture is deep red.
4. In a separate bowl, whisk the chickpea flour into the water until completely smooth with no lumps.
5. Pour the chickpea-flour mixture into the pot, whisking constantly as it heats to prevent clumping.
6. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook 8–10 minutes, stirring frequently, until thick, glossy, and no longer tastes raw/floury.
7. Season with salt, thin with a splash more water if it's thicker than you'd like, and serve hot with injera.

### 4. Atkilt Wat (Cabbage, Carrot & Potato Stew)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Green cabbage, sliced | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Carrot, sliced | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Potato, cubed | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Onion, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic clove, minced | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Ginger, grated | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Ground turmeric | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Ground cumin | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Vegetable oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Water | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Salt | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Heat the oil in a wide pan or pot over medium heat. Add the onion, garlic, and ginger, cooking 3–4 minutes until softened.
2. Stir in the turmeric and cumin, cooking 1 minute until fragrant and the onions are stained gold.
3. Add the potato and the water, cover, and cook 8–10 minutes until the potato is nearly tender.
4. Add the carrot, cover, and cook 5 minutes more.
5. Add the cabbage and salt, cover, and cook 8–10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until all the vegetables are tender but the cabbage still has a little bite.
6. Taste and adjust salt. Serve warm, on its own or alongside a wat.

### 5. Doro Wat (Spiced Chicken Stew)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bone-in, skin removed | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Red onion, finely diced | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Garlic clove, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ginger, grated | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Niter kibbeh or butter | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Berbere spice blend | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Tomato paste | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Chicken stock or water | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Hard-boiled eggs | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Salt | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Melt the niter kibbeh (or butter) in a heavy pot over low heat. Add the red onion, cover, and cook 20–25 minutes, stirring every few minutes, until deeply softened and jammy. (Traditional versions cook the onions even longer — this is the weeknight version, but low and slow is still the whole point of the dish, so don't rush this step.)
2. Add the garlic and ginger, cooking 2–3 minutes until fragrant.
3. Stir in the berbere and tomato paste, cooking 2 minutes until the mixture turns a deep brick red and smells toasted.
4. Add the chicken thighs, turning to coat every piece in the spiced onions.
5. Pour in the stock or water, bring to a gentle simmer, cover, and cook 25–30 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the chicken is cooked through and tender.
6. Nestle the hard-boiled eggs into the sauce, spooning some sauce over the top of each. Simmer uncovered 5–10 minutes so the eggs soak up flavor and the sauce thickens slightly.
7. Season with salt to taste. Serve hot with injera — this is the centerpiece dish, so give it the good bread.

### 6. Key Wat (Spiced Beef Stew)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beef stew meat (chuck), cubed | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Salt | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Red onion, finely diced | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Garlic clove, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ginger, grated | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Niter kibbeh, butter, or oil | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Berbere spice blend | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Tomato paste | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Beef stock or water | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |

**Steps:**
1. Heat the niter kibbeh (or oil) in a heavy pot or Dutch oven over medium heat. Season the beef with salt and pepper, then brown it in batches, 2–3 minutes per side. Remove to a plate.
2. In the same pot, add the onion and cook 12–15 minutes, stirring occasionally, until deeply golden and softened.
3. Add the garlic and ginger, cooking 1–2 minutes until fragrant.
4. Stir in the berbere and tomato paste, cooking 2 minutes until deep red and fragrant.
5. Return the beef and any juices to the pot, then pour in the stock or water. Bring to a simmer.
6. Cover and simmer on low 35–40 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the beef is fork-tender. (For extra-tender beef, an hour is even better if you have the time.)
7. Uncover and simmer 5 more minutes to thicken slightly if needed. Season with salt.
8. Serve hot with injera or rice.

### 7. Gomen (Collard Greens with Garlic & Ginger)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Collard greens, stemmed and sliced | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Onion, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic clove, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ginger, grated | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Vegetable oil or niter kibbeh | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Water or stock | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Ground cumin (optional) | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Salt | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion, garlic, and ginger, cooking 8–10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until deeply softened.
2. Add the collard greens in batches, stirring to wilt each addition before adding more.
3. Stir in the cumin (if using) and a splash of water or stock. Cover and simmer 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the greens are tender.
4. Season with salt to taste. Serve warm, on its own or alongside Doro Wat or Key Wat.

### 8. Injera (Teff Sourdough Flatbread) (Bonus)
*A note on this one:* traditional injera ferments for 2–3 days for its full sour tang and requires a fairly specific batter consistency to get right. This is a same-day, weeknight-friendly shortcut using yeast and vinegar to fake that tang fast — it won't be quite as sour or as bubbly-holed as the real 3-day version, but it's a legitimate, edible flatbread you can actually make on a Tuesday. If you have the time, let the batter ferment overnight in the fridge instead of just 45–60 minutes on the counter — it gets noticeably better.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Teff flour | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| All-purpose flour | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Instant yeast | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Salt | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Warm water | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Apple cider vinegar | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Baking soda | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Vegetable oil or niter kibbeh | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |

**Steps:**
1. Whisk the teff flour, all-purpose flour, yeast, and salt together in a large bowl.
2. Add the warm water and whisk until you have a smooth, thin, pourable batter — thinner than pancake batter, closer to heavy cream.
3. Stir in the vinegar. Cover and let sit in a warm spot 45–60 minutes until bubbly on the surface (or refrigerate overnight for better flavor, then bring back to room temperature before cooking).
4. Just before cooking, whisk in the baking soda — this gives a last burst of bubbles that helps form the characteristic surface holes.
5. Heat a large nonstick skillet over medium heat and lightly oil it.
6. Pour a thin, even layer of batter into the pan, swirling to coat the bottom (or pour in a spiral from the outside in).
7. Cover and cook 2–3 minutes, undisturbed, until the surface is covered in small holes ("eyes") and the edges lift away from the pan on their own. Do not flip.
8. Slide onto a clean plate or towel to cool. Repeat with the remaining batter, stacking cooked injera between layers of parchment or a clean towel so they stay soft.
9. Serve at room temperature, torn into pieces, alongside the wats — use it as an edible scoop, the traditional way.
