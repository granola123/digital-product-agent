### 1. Coq au Vin (Chicken Braised in Red Wine)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs & drumsticks, bone-in | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Bacon lardons (poitrine fumée) | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Pearl onions | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Cremini or button mushrooms | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Garlic cloves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Carrot, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Red wine (Burgundy-style) | 125ml | 250ml | 375ml | 500ml |
| Chicken stock | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Tomato paste | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Bay leaf | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Thyme sprig | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Butter | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| All-purpose flour (for beurre manié) | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Olive oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Brandy or cognac (optional, for flambé) | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Parsley, chopped (garnish) | 2g | 4g | 6g | 8g |

**Steps:**
1. Pat the chicken dry and season all over with salt and pepper.
2. In a Dutch oven or heavy pot, cook the bacon lardons over medium heat until crisp and the fat has rendered, about 5 minutes. Remove with a slotted spoon, leaving the fat behind.
3. Sear the chicken skin-side down in the bacon fat until deeply browned, 4–5 minutes per side. Remove and set aside with the bacon.
4. Add the pearl onions and mushrooms to the pot, cooking 5–6 minutes until browned.
5. Add the garlic and carrot, cooking 1–2 minutes until fragrant.
6. Stir in the tomato paste and cook 1 minute.
7. If flambéing, return the chicken and bacon to the pot, warm the brandy separately, pour it over, and carefully ignite, letting the flames subside on their own.
8. Add the wine, stock, bay leaf, and thyme, and bring to a gentle simmer.
9. Cover and simmer gently 40–45 minutes (or braise in a 160°C/325°F oven) until the chicken is very tender.
10. Lift out the chicken, onions, and mushrooms and keep warm. Mash the butter and flour together into a smooth paste (beurre manié), then whisk it into the simmering sauce and cook 5 minutes until thickened.
11. Return the chicken and vegetables to the pot to coat in the sauce.
12. Garnish with parsley and serve hot with crusty bread, mashed potatoes, or buttered tagliatelle.

### 2. Ratatouille
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Eggplant, cubed | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Zucchini, cubed | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Yellow squash, cubed | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Red bell pepper, chopped | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Yellow bell pepper, chopped | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Onion, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Tomatoes, chopped (fresh or canned) | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Olive oil | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Herbes de Provence | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Bay leaf | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Fresh basil, torn | 2g | 4g | 6g | 8g |

**Steps:**
1. Cut the eggplant, zucchini, squash, and peppers into even 2cm chunks. Salt the eggplant and let it sit 15 minutes to draw out bitterness, then pat dry.
2. Heat the olive oil in a wide skillet or pot over medium heat. Cook the onion 4–5 minutes until softened.
3. Add the garlic and cook 30 seconds until fragrant.
4. Add the eggplant and cook 5–6 minutes, stirring, until it starts to soften and color.
5. Add the zucchini and squash, cooking 5 minutes more.
6. Add the peppers and cook 3–4 minutes.
7. Stir in the tomatoes, herbes de Provence, bay leaf, salt, and pepper.
8. Reduce the heat, cover, and simmer gently 20–25 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the vegetables are tender but still hold their shape.
9. Uncover and simmer 5 minutes more if there's excess liquid to cook off.
10. Discard the bay leaf, stir in the torn basil, and serve warm or at room temperature.

*Chef's note: purists in Nice traditionally cook each vegetable separately before layering them (the technique the film* Ratatouille *popularized as "confit byaldi"). The single-pot stewed method above is the more common home-cook version found across France and is faster for a weeknight — both are considered legitimate.*

### 3. Beef Bourguignon
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beef chuck, cubed | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Bacon lardons | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Carrot, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Onion, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic cloves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Button mushrooms | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Pearl onions | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Red wine (Burgundy-style) | 180ml | 360ml | 540ml | 720ml |
| Beef stock | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Tomato paste | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Bay leaf | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Thyme sprig | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| All-purpose flour | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Butter | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Olive oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Parsley, chopped (garnish) | 2g | 4g | 6g | 8g |

**Steps:**
1. Pat the beef dry and season with salt and pepper.
2. In a Dutch oven, cook the bacon over medium heat until crisp. Remove with a slotted spoon, leaving the fat in the pot.
3. Sear the beef in batches in the bacon fat until deeply browned on all sides; remove and set aside.
4. Add the chopped onion and carrot to the pot, cooking 5 minutes until softened.
5. Add the garlic and cook 30 seconds.
6. Stir in the flour and cook 1–2 minutes to coat the vegetables.
7. Stir in the tomato paste and cook 1 minute.
8. Return the beef and bacon to the pot, add the wine and stock, scraping up any browned bits from the bottom.
9. Add the bay leaf and thyme and bring to a simmer.
10. Cover and simmer gently on low heat (or braise in a 160°C/325°F oven) 1½–2 hours until the beef is fork-tender.
11. Meanwhile, sauté the pearl onions and mushrooms in the butter in a separate pan until browned; set aside.
12. Stir the browned onions and mushrooms into the stew for the last 15 minutes of cooking.
13. Discard the bay leaf and thyme sprig, and adjust seasoning.
14. Garnish with parsley and serve with mashed potatoes, egg noodles, or crusty bread.

### 4. Quiche Lorraine
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pie dough (single 9-inch crust) | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Bacon or lardons | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Onion, thinly sliced | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Gruyère cheese, shredded | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Eggs | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Heavy cream | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Whole milk | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Nutmeg, ground | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Salt | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Preheat the oven to 190°C (375°F). Roll out the pie dough and fit it into a tart pan or pie dish, then prick the base all over with a fork.
2. Blind bake: line the crust with parchment and pie weights, bake 12–15 minutes, remove the weights, then bake 5 minutes more until lightly golden. Reduce the oven to 180°C (350°F).
3. Cook the bacon in a skillet over medium heat until crisp; remove and drain.
4. In the same fat, cook the onion 4–5 minutes until softened; drain off excess fat.
5. Scatter the bacon, onion, and Gruyère over the base of the crust.
6. Whisk together the eggs, cream, milk, nutmeg, salt, and pepper.
7. Pour the egg mixture over the filling in the crust.
8. Bake 30–35 minutes until the custard is just set with a slight jiggle in the center and the top is golden.
9. Let cool 10 minutes before slicing. Serve warm or at room temperature.

*Chef's note: in Lorraine itself, the traditionalist version is cheese-free — just an egg-and-cream custard with lardons, so the filling stays silky rather than stretchy. Gruyère became a near-universal addition outside the region over the last century and is what most people now expect from "quiche lorraine," so it's kept here — but leave it out for the version Lorraine bakers would recognize as original.*

### 5. Poulet Provençal (Provençal Chicken)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bone-in, skin-on | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Onion, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic cloves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Bell peppers (mixed), sliced | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Tomatoes, diced (fresh or canned) | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Niçoise or Kalamata olives | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| White wine | 40ml | 80ml | 120ml | 160ml |
| Herbes de Provence | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Olive oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Fresh basil or parsley, chopped | 2g | 4g | 6g | 8g |

**Steps:**
1. Pat the chicken dry and season with salt and pepper.
2. Heat the olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Sear the chicken skin-side down 5–6 minutes until golden, flip, and sear 3 minutes more. Remove.
3. Add the onion to the pan and cook 3–4 minutes until softened.
4. Add the garlic and bell peppers, cooking 3–4 minutes.
5. Pour in the white wine, scraping up any browned bits, and simmer 2 minutes.
6. Add the tomatoes, olives, and herbes de Provence.
7. Return the chicken to the pan, skin-side up, nestling it into the sauce.
8. Cover and simmer 25–30 minutes until the chicken is cooked through and tender.
9. Uncover and simmer 5 minutes more to thicken the sauce slightly.
10. Garnish with basil or parsley and serve with crusty bread or rice.

### 6. Salade Niçoise
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tuna (oil-packed), drained | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Baby potatoes | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Green beans (haricots verts) | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Eggs | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Cherry tomatoes, halved | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Niçoise olives | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Red onion, thinly sliced | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Mixed or butter lettuce | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Anchovy fillets (optional) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Olive oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Red wine vinegar | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Dijon mustard | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Boil the potatoes in salted water 12–15 minutes until fork-tender. Drain, cool slightly, and halve.
2. Blanch the green beans in boiling salted water 3–4 minutes until crisp-tender, then shock in ice water and drain.
3. Boil the eggs 8–9 minutes for jammy centers (or 10 minutes for fully set), cool in ice water, peel, and halve.
4. Whisk the olive oil, red wine vinegar, Dijon mustard, salt, and pepper together to make the vinaigrette.
5. Arrange the lettuce on a platter or individual plates.
6. Top with the potatoes, green beans, cherry tomatoes, red onion, and flaked tuna.
7. Add the halved eggs, olives, and anchovy fillets if using.
8. Drizzle with the vinaigrette just before serving.

*Chef's note: purists from Nice leave out the potatoes and green beans entirely — the original composed salad is just tomatoes, olives, anchovies or tuna, and eggs, dressed simply in olive oil. The version with potatoes and beans (sometimes credited to Escoffier's Parisian take) is what most people worldwide now call "Niçoise" and is kept here for a heartier weeknight dinner — drop the two if you want the version Nice locals would call authentic.*

### 7. Croque Monsieur
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sandwich bread (pain de mie), slices | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Ham, sliced | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Gruyère cheese, shredded | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Butter | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| All-purpose flour | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Whole milk | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Dijon mustard | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Nutmeg, ground | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Salt | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Make the béchamel: melt the butter in a small saucepan over medium heat, whisk in the flour, and cook 1 minute.
2. Gradually whisk in the milk and cook, whisking, 3–4 minutes until thickened. Season with nutmeg, salt, and pepper.
3. Spread one side of each bread slice with Dijon mustard.
4. Layer the ham and half the Gruyère between two mustard-spread bread slices to make a sandwich.
5. Spread a layer of béchamel over the top of the sandwich and sprinkle with the remaining Gruyère.
6. Toast in a skillet with a little butter, or bake at 220°C (425°F) for 8–10 minutes until the cheese is bubbling and golden.
7. For a Croque Madame, top with a fried egg before serving — an easy variation.
8. Serve hot.

### 8. Crème Brûlée (Bonus)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Heavy cream | 120ml | 240ml | 360ml | 480ml |
| Egg yolks | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Sugar (for the custard) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Vanilla extract (or ¼ bean) | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Sugar (for the brûlée topping) | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |

**Steps:**
1. Preheat the oven to 150°C (300°F).
2. Heat the cream with the vanilla in a saucepan until just steaming — do not let it boil.
3. Whisk the egg yolks and sugar together until pale.
4. Slowly whisk the hot cream into the yolk mixture in a thin stream to temper it.
5. Strain the mixture through a fine sieve into a jug to remove any bits.
6. Divide among ramekins, place in a baking dish, and pour hot water halfway up the sides to make a water bath.
7. Bake 30–35 minutes until the custards are set around the edges but still slightly jiggly in the center.
8. Remove from the water bath, cool to room temperature, then refrigerate at least 2 hours (or overnight).
9. Just before serving, sprinkle an even layer of sugar over each custard.
10. Caramelize with a kitchen torch until deep amber and crisp (or under a hot broiler, watching closely).
11. Let sit 1–2 minutes for the sugar to harden, then serve immediately.
