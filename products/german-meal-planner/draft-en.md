### 1. Wiener/Jäger-Style Pork Schnitzel with Lemon
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pork loin cutlet, pounded thin | 1 (120g) | 2 (240g) | 3 (360g) | 4 (480g) |
| All-purpose flour | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Egg, beaten | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Dried breadcrumbs | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Vegetable oil (for frying) | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Butter | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Lemon, cut into wedges | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |

**Steps:**
1. Place the pork cutlet between two sheets of plastic wrap. Ask an adult to help you pound it with the flat side of a meat mallet (or a heavy pan) until it's about ½cm thin and evenly flat all over — this is what makes schnitzel tender instead of chewy.
2. Season both sides of the cutlet with the salt and black pepper.
3. Set up three shallow plates in a row: one with the flour, one with the beaten egg, and one with the breadcrumbs.
4. Dip the cutlet in the flour first, shaking off the extra. Then dip it in the egg, letting the extra drip off. Then press it into the breadcrumbs so they stick to both sides in an even coat. Don't press too hard or the coating gets dense instead of light and crackly.
5. Ask an adult to help heat the vegetable oil and butter in a large frying pan over medium-high heat. The oil is ready when a breadcrumb dropped in sizzles right away.
6. Carefully lay the cutlet into the hot oil. Fry 2–3 minutes on the first side until deep golden brown, gently shaking the pan now and then so the coating puffs up a little.
7. Flip and fry 2–3 minutes on the other side, until the outside is golden brown and crisp all over and the meat is no longer pink in the center when you cut into the thickest part.
8. Lift the schnitzel out and rest it on a paper towel-lined plate for a minute to drain extra oil.
9. Serve right away with a lemon wedge on the side — squeezing the lemon over the hot schnitzel right before eating is the classic Wiener style.

### 2. Bratwurst mit Sauerkraut
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bratwurst sausages | 1 (100g) | 2 (200g) | 3 (300g) | 4 (400g) |
| Vegetable oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Sauerkraut, drained | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Yellow onion, sliced | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Caraway seeds | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Bay leaf | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Apple, grated (optional, for sweetness) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Vegetable or chicken stock | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Yellow mustard (for serving) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Salt | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ⅛ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help heat the vegetable oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Add the sliced onion and cook for 3–4 minutes, stirring, until it turns soft and see-through.
2. Push the onions to one side and add the bratwurst to the empty part of the pan. Cook 8–10 minutes, turning every couple of minutes with tongs, until every side is browned and the inside is no longer pink (cut into the fattest one to check).
3. While the sausages cook, put the drained sauerkraut in a separate small pot with the caraway seeds, bay leaf, grated apple, and stock.
4. Bring the sauerkraut to a gentle simmer over medium heat, then turn the heat down low and let it cook 10–12 minutes, stirring occasionally, until it's warmed through and the liquid is mostly soaked in.
5. Taste the sauerkraut and season with a little salt and pepper if it needs it — it should taste tangy but not sharp.
6. Fish out the bay leaf and throw it away — it's for flavor only, not eating.
7. Scoop the warm sauerkraut onto plates, lay the browned bratwurst on top or beside it, and serve with a spoonful of mustard on the side.

### 3. Rinderrouladen (Beef Roll-Ups)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beef top round steak, thin-sliced | 1 (150g) | 2 (300g) | 3 (450g) | 4 (600g) |
| Yellow mustard | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Bacon slices | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Yellow onion, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Dill pickle (gherkin), quartered lengthwise | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Vegetable oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Tomato paste | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Beef stock | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Carrot, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Bay leaf | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| All-purpose flour (to thicken) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Steps:**
1. Lay the beef slice flat on a cutting board. If it isn't already thin, place plastic wrap over it and ask an adult to help pound it to an even thickness with a meat mallet.
2. Season the beef with the salt and pepper, then spread the mustard evenly over the whole surface.
3. Lay a slice of bacon on top of the mustard, then scatter the chopped onion over the bacon, and lay a pickle spear along one edge of the meat.
4. Starting from the pickle edge, roll the beef up tightly like a jelly roll, tucking in the sides as you go so the filling doesn't fall out.
5. Tie the roll with kitchen string in two or three places, or secure it with a couple of toothpicks, so it holds its shape while cooking.
6. Ask an adult to help heat the vegetable oil in a heavy pot or Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Sear the roulade on all sides for about 6–8 minutes total, turning with tongs, until deeply browned all over.
7. Remove the roulade and set aside. Add any leftover chopped onion and the carrot to the same pot and cook 2–3 minutes until softened.
8. Stir in the tomato paste and cook 1 minute, then pour in the beef stock and add the bay leaf, scraping up the browned bits from the bottom of the pot.
9. Return the roulade to the pot, bring to a gentle simmer, then cover with a lid left slightly ajar. Cook on low heat for 1½–2 hours, turning the roulade once or twice, until it's fork-tender.
10. Lift the roulade out onto a cutting board and remove the string or toothpicks. Fish out and discard the bay leaf.
11. Whisk the flour with a splash of cold water until smooth, then whisk it into the simmering sauce and cook 2–3 minutes until it thickens slightly.
12. Slice the roulade into thick rounds and spoon the sauce over the top. Serve with red cabbage and potato dumplings or mashed potatoes.

### 4. Kartoffelsuppe (German Potato Soup with Sausage)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Potatoes, peeled and diced | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Carrot, diced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Celeriac or celery, diced | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Leek, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Butter | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Yellow onion, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Vegetable or chicken stock | 300ml | 600ml | 900ml | 1200ml |
| Bay leaf | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Frankfurter or bratwurst sausage, sliced | 1 (60g) | 2 (120g) | 3 (180g) | 4 (240g) |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Parsley, chopped (garnish) | 2g | 4g | 6g | 8g |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help melt the butter in a large soup pot over medium heat. Add the chopped onion, leek, carrot, and celeriac, and cook 5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the vegetables start to soften but not brown.
2. Add the diced potatoes and stir so everything gets coated in the buttery vegetables.
3. Pour in the stock and add the bay leaf. Turn the heat up to bring it to a boil, then turn it down so it's gently simmering.
4. Cover partway with a lid and cook 15–18 minutes, until the potatoes are soft enough to easily mash against the side of the pot with a spoon.
5. Fish out and discard the bay leaf.
6. Using a potato masher or the back of a spoon, mash some of the potatoes right in the pot — just enough to thicken the soup a little, while leaving plenty of potato chunks for texture.
7. Add the sliced sausage and simmer 4–5 minutes more, until it's warmed through.
8. Season with the salt and pepper, tasting as you go.
9. Ladle into bowls and sprinkle with chopped parsley before serving.

### 5. Käsespätzle (Cheese Spätzle with Caramelized Onions)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All-purpose flour | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Eggs | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Whole milk or water | 40ml | 80ml | 120ml | 160ml |
| Salt (for dough) | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Emmental or Gruyère cheese, shredded | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Yellow onion, thinly sliced | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Butter | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Black pepper | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Chives, chopped (garnish) | 2g | 4g | 6g | 8g |

**Steps:**
1. In a mixing bowl, whisk together the flour, eggs, milk, and salt for the dough until smooth and a little stretchy — it should be thick like a heavy pancake batter, not pourable like a thin one. Let it rest 10 minutes.
2. While the dough rests, ask an adult to help melt half the butter in a small pan over medium-low heat. Add the sliced onions and cook slowly, stirring every few minutes, for 15–20 minutes until they turn deep golden brown and taste sweet, not raw. Turn the heat down if they start browning too fast.
3. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil.
4. Working in batches, press the spätzle dough through a spätzle press or colander with large holes directly into the boiling water — ask an adult to help hold the press steady, since the pot is hot. The little dumplings/noodles will sink, then float to the top when they're done, after about 1–2 minutes.
5. Scoop the floating spätzle out with a slotted spoon into a colander to drain, and repeat until all the dough is cooked.
6. Ask an adult to help melt the rest of the butter in a large oven-safe pan over medium heat. Add the drained spätzle and toss to coat.
7. Sprinkle in the shredded cheese in two batches, tossing gently after each addition so it melts through the spätzle instead of clumping in one spot.
8. Season with the black pepper.
9. Top with the caramelized onions and scatter the chopped chives over everything just before serving.

### 6. Currywurst mit Pommes
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bratwurst or frankfurter sausage | 1 (100g) | 2 (200g) | 3 (300g) | 4 (400g) |
| Vegetable oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Ketchup | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Curry powder | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Worcestershire sauce | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Sweet paprika (extra, for sprinkling) | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Russet potatoes, cut into fries | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Vegetable oil (for frying fries) | 500ml | 500ml | 750ml | 1000ml |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Cut the potatoes into even fry-shaped sticks. Rinse them in cold water to wash off extra starch, then pat completely dry with a towel — wet potatoes splatter dangerously in hot oil.
2. Ask an adult to help heat the frying oil in a deep pot or fryer to about 160°C/325°F (a piece of potato dropped in should bubble gently, not violently).
3. Fry the potatoes in batches for 4–5 minutes, until pale and soft but not browned. Lift out with a slotted spoon or basket and drain on paper towels — this first fry cooks them through.
4. Ask an adult to help turn the oil up to about 190°C/375°F for the second fry. While it heats, make the sauce: stir the ketchup, curry powder, and Worcestershire sauce together in a small saucepan over low heat for 2–3 minutes, just until warmed and fragrant.
5. Fry the par-cooked potatoes a second time, 2–3 minutes, until golden and crisp. Lift out, drain, and season immediately with the salt while they're still hot and glistening (salt sticks better to hot, oily fries).
6. While the fries cook, heat a splash of vegetable oil in a separate pan over medium heat. Add the sausage and cook 8–10 minutes, turning occasionally, until browned all over and hot in the middle.
7. Slice the cooked sausage into bite-sized rounds and arrange on a plate.
8. Spoon the warm curry-ketchup sauce generously over the sliced sausage and dust with extra sweet paprika.
9. Serve immediately with the hot fries alongside.

### 7. Rindergulasch (Beef Goulash)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beef chuck, cut into cubes | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Vegetable oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Yellow onion, chopped | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Garlic, minced | 1 clove | 2 cloves | 3 cloves | 4 cloves |
| Sweet paprika | 1 tbsp | 2 tbsp | 3 tbsp | 4 tbsp |
| Tomato paste | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Beef stock | 200ml | 400ml | 600ml | 800ml |
| Bell pepper, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Caraway seeds | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Dried marjoram | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Bay leaf | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Pat the beef cubes dry and season with a little of the salt and pepper.
2. Ask an adult to help heat the vegetable oil in a heavy pot or Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Working in batches so the pot isn't crowded, sear the beef on all sides for 2–3 minutes per batch, until deeply browned. Set the browned beef aside.
3. Turn the heat down to medium and add the chopped onion to the same pot. Cook 5–6 minutes, stirring, until soft and golden.
4. Add the garlic and cook 30 seconds until fragrant.
5. Take the pot off the heat for a moment and stir in the paprika and tomato paste — this keeps the paprika from scorching and turning bitter, which happens fast over direct heat.
6. Return the pot to the heat, pour in the beef stock, and stir well, scraping up any browned bits from the bottom.
7. Return the browned beef to the pot along with the bell pepper, caraway seeds, marjoram, and bay leaf — marjoram is the traditional herb that rounds out a German-style goulash and sets it apart from a plain paprika stew.
8. Bring to a gentle simmer, then turn the heat down low and cover with a lid left slightly ajar. Cook 1½–2 hours, stirring occasionally, until the beef is completely fork-tender and falling apart easily.
9. Fish out and discard the bay leaf. Taste and add the remaining salt and pepper as needed.
10. Ladle into bowls and serve with crusty bread, spätzle, or boiled potatoes.

### 8. Apfelstrudel (Bonus Dessert)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tart apples, peeled and thinly sliced | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Sugar | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Raisins | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Lemon juice | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Ground cinnamon | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Breadcrumbs | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Butter, melted | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Strudel or phyllo dough sheets | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Powdered sugar (for dusting) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Steps:**
1. In a bowl, toss the sliced apples with the sugar, raisins, lemon juice, and cinnamon. Let the mixture sit for about 10 minutes so the apples release a little juice.
2. Ask an adult to help melt the butter. In a small dry pan, toast the breadcrumbs over medium heat for 1–2 minutes, stirring constantly, until light golden and nutty-smelling — this keeps the strudel from getting soggy on the bottom.
3. Lay a clean kitchen towel on the counter and place the strudel or phyllo dough sheet on top. Brush it lightly all over with melted butter.
4. If using more than one sheet, layer additional sheets on top, brushing each one with melted butter before adding the next.
5. Sprinkle the toasted breadcrumbs evenly over the buttered dough, leaving a bare border of about 3cm around the edges.
6. Spoon the apple mixture in a line along one long edge of the dough, on top of the breadcrumbs.
7. Using the kitchen towel to help lift and guide the dough, roll the strudel up tightly around the filling, starting from the apple-filled edge, tucking in the ends as you go like wrapping a burrito.
8. Carefully transfer the rolled strudel, seam-side down, onto a parchment-lined baking sheet. Brush the outside all over with the remaining melted butter.
9. Ask an adult to help bake in an oven preheated to 190°C/375°F for 30–35 minutes, until deep golden brown and crisp.
10. Let the strudel cool for at least 15 minutes (the filling is very hot) before dusting with powdered sugar, slicing, and serving.
