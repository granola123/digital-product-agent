## Grocery List (by category)

**Produce & Herbs:** onion (needed for nearly every dinner — buy in bulk), garlic, carrots, potatoes (needed twice — buy a large bag), green bell pepper, banana peppers or sweet frying peppers (Hungarian wax peppers if available), fresh tomatoes, 1 large head of green cabbage (or pre-pickled cabbage leaves), raisins

**Protein:** beef chuck (for goulash), chicken thighs — a mix of bone-in skin-on (paprikash) and boneless (palacsinta filling), ground pork, pork shoulder, Hungarian smoked sausage (kolbász or any good smoked sausage — needed 3 times, buy in bulk), eggs (buy a dozen — needed for paprikash sauce, stuffed cabbage, crêpes, rakott krumpli, and the bonus dessert)

**Grains, Legumes & Starch:** rice (for stuffed cabbage filling), all-purpose flour (needed often — nokedli, paprikash sauce, crêpe batter, palacsinta sauce, sponge cake — buy a large bag), cornstarch

**Dairy & Pantry Staples:** sour cream (needed 4 times — paprikash, stuffed cabbage, székely gulyás, rakott krumpli — buy a large tub), milk, butter, heavy/whipping cream, sweet Hungarian paprika (the backbone of the week — buy a good fresh tin, needed in nearly every recipe), caraway seeds (ground or whole), dried marjoram, bay leaves, tomato paste, canned diced tomatoes, jarred sauerkraut (needed twice), baking powder, cocoa powder, dark chocolate (for melting), walnuts, dark rum, granulated sugar, vanilla extract, beef stock, chicken stock, cooking oil or lard, salt, black pepper

## Recipe Cards

All ingredient tables below are scaled for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 servings.

### 1. Gulyás (Hungarian Beef Goulash Soup)
A rich, paprika-red beef and vegetable soup simmered low and slow until the beef falls apart — Hungary's national dish, built on sweet paprika, a whisper of caraway, and marjoram, not a splash of heavy cream in sight.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beef chuck, cubed | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Onion, chopped | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Oil or lard | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Sweet Hungarian paprika | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Caraway seeds, ground | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Dried marjoram | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Tomato paste | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Canned diced tomatoes | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Bay leaf | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Beef stock or water | 250ml | 500ml | 750ml | 1000ml |
| Potatoes, diced | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Carrot, diced | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Green bell pepper, diced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help heat the oil or lard in a large pot over medium-high heat. Brown the beef cubes in batches, then remove and set aside.
2. In the same pot, sauté the onion and garlic until soft.
3. Take the pot off the heat for a moment and stir in the paprika, caraway, and marjoram so the paprika doesn't scorch.
4. Return the beef to the pot, then add the tomato paste, diced tomatoes, bay leaf, and stock or water.
5. Cover and simmer 60–75 minutes, until the beef is nearly tender.
6. Add the potatoes, carrot, and green pepper, and simmer 20 minutes more, until the vegetables are tender and the beef is fully tender.
7. Season with salt and pepper. Serve hot, with crusty bread.

### 2. Chicken Paprikash with Nokedli (Csirkepaprikás)
Bone-in chicken braised in a silky, deep-red paprika and sour cream sauce, ladled over soft little egg dumplings — the dish most people picture first when they think of Hungarian cooking.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bone-in, skin-on | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Onion, finely chopped | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Lard or oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Sweet Hungarian paprika | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Green bell pepper, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Tomato, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Chicken stock | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Sour cream | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| All-purpose flour, to thicken the sour cream | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| All-purpose flour, for nokedli | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Egg, for nokedli | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Water, for nokedli | 2 tbsp | 4 tbsp | 6 tbsp | 8 tbsp |
| Salt, for nokedli | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help heat the lard or oil in a pot over medium-high heat. Brown the chicken thighs skin-side down, then remove and set aside.
2. In the same pot, sauté the onion and garlic until soft.
3. Take the pot off the heat for a moment and stir in the paprika so it blooms without burning.
4. Add the green pepper, tomato, and chicken stock, then return the chicken to the pot.
5. Cover and simmer 30–35 minutes, until the chicken is cooked through.
6. Meanwhile, make the nokedli: whisk the nokedli flour, egg, water, and salt into a thick, sticky batter. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil.
7. Drop small spoonfuls of the batter into the boiling water (or push through a colander) and cook 2–3 minutes, until they float. Drain.
8. Remove the chicken from the sauce. Whisk the flour into the sour cream until smooth, then stir it into the sauce and simmer gently 2 minutes to thicken (don't let it boil hard).
9. Return the chicken to the sauce, and season with salt and pepper.
10. Serve the chicken and sauce hot over the nokedli.

### 3. Töltött Káposzta (Stuffed Cabbage Rolls)
Tender cabbage leaves rolled around a paprika-and-caraway-seasoned pork and rice filling, braised for an hour on a bed of sauerkraut with smoked sausage tucked between the rolls — a dish every Hungarian family has its own version of.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cabbage leaves, blanched | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Ground pork | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Rice, parboiled | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Egg | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Onion, finely chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Sweet Hungarian paprika | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Caraway seeds, ground | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Sauerkraut | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Smoked sausage (kolbász) or smoked bacon, sliced | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Bay leaf | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Water or stock, for braising | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Sour cream, to serve | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |

**Steps:**
1. In a bowl, mix the ground pork, rice, egg, onion, garlic, paprika, caraway, salt, and pepper.
2. Lay a cabbage leaf flat, spoon some filling near the base, fold in the sides, and roll up snugly. Repeat with the remaining leaves.
3. Spread half the sauerkraut over the bottom of a heavy pot.
4. Arrange the cabbage rolls seam-side down on top and scatter the smoked sausage or bacon around them.
5. Cover with the remaining sauerkraut, then add the bay leaf and the water or stock.
6. Bring to a boil, then cover, reduce the heat, and simmer 60 minutes, until the rolls are cooked through and the cabbage is tender.
7. Serve hot with a generous dollop of sour cream.

### 4. Lecsó with Sausage and Egg
A jammy, paprika-red stew of onions, sweet frying peppers, and tomatoes, simmered with slices of smoked sausage and finished with scrambled egg folded through — a beloved everyday Hungarian summer-into-weeknight dish.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Onion, sliced | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Oil or lard | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Sweet Hungarian paprika | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Banana peppers or sweet frying peppers, sliced | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Tomatoes, chopped | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Hungarian smoked sausage (kolbász), sliced | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Egg, beaten | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help heat the oil or lard in a pan and sauté the onion until soft.
2. Take the pan off the heat for a moment and stir in the paprika.
3. Add the peppers and cook 5 minutes, until starting to soften.
4. Add the tomatoes and sausage, cover, and simmer 15 minutes, until the peppers are tender and everything is saucy.
5. Season with salt and pepper.
6. Push everything to one side of the pan, pour in the beaten egg, and scramble gently until just set, then stir it through the rest of the lecsó.
7. Serve hot with bread.

### 5. Hortobágyi Palacsinta (Savory Meat-Filled Crêpes)
Delicate savory crêpes rolled around a slow-cooked, shredded paprika-chicken filling and baked under a smooth sour cream and paprika sauce — created for Hungary's pavilion at the 1958 Brussels World's Fair and a showpiece of Hungarian cuisine ever since.

**Crêpe Batter**

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All-purpose flour | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Egg | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Milk | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Oil or butter, for frying crêpes | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |

**Filling**

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oil, for the filling | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Chicken thighs, boneless, diced | 120g | 240g | 360g | 480g |
| Onion, finely chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Sweet Hungarian paprika | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Caraway seeds, ground | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Tomato, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Chicken stock | 80ml | 160ml | 240ml | 320ml |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Sauce**

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sour cream | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| All-purpose flour, for the sauce | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Sweet Hungarian paprika, for the sauce | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Make the crêpe batter: whisk the flour, egg, milk, and salt together until smooth. Let rest 10 minutes.
2. Heat a little oil or butter in a nonstick pan and cook thin crêpes, one at a time, until lightly golden on both sides. Stack them and set aside.
3. For the filling, heat the oil in a pot and sauté the onion and garlic until soft.
4. Take the pot off the heat for a moment and stir in the paprika and caraway.
5. Add the chicken, tomato, and chicken stock. Cover and simmer 20–25 minutes, until the chicken is very tender, then shred it with two forks right in the sauce.
6. Season the filling with salt and pepper. Spoon some filling onto each crêpe, roll it up, and arrange the rolls seam-side down in a baking dish.
7. For the sauce, whisk the flour into the sour cream until smooth, then stir in a few tablespoons of the hot liquid left in the filling pot along with the paprika for the sauce, until pourable.
8. Pour the sauce evenly over the crêpes.
9. Warm through, covered, over low heat or in a low oven for about 10 minutes, until hot and bubbling at the edges.
10. Serve hot.

### 6. Székely Gulyás (Pork and Sauerkraut Stew)
A hearty pork and sauerkraut stew, born in an 1846 Budapest restaurant from leftover pörkölt and sauerkraut and now a beloved dish in its own right — tangy, smoky-sweet with paprika, and finished with a swirl of sour cream.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pork shoulder, cubed | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Onion, chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Oil or lard | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Sweet Hungarian paprika | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Caraway seeds, ground | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Sauerkraut, rinsed and drained | 120g | 240g | 360g | 480g |
| Bay leaf | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Water or stock | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Sour cream | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help heat the oil or lard in a pot and sauté the onion until soft.
2. Add the pork and brown it on all sides.
3. Take the pot off the heat for a moment and stir in the paprika and caraway (adding them off the heat, with a splash of the water or stock, keeps the paprika from burning). Stir in the garlic.
4. Cover and simmer 40 minutes, adding a little more water or stock if it looks dry, until the pork is nearly tender.
5. Stir in the sauerkraut, bay leaf, and the remaining water or stock.
6. Cover and simmer 20 minutes more, until the pork and sauerkraut are both tender.
7. Stir in the sour cream and season with salt and pepper. Warm through gently without letting it boil hard.
8. Serve hot with bread.

### 7. Rakott Krumpli (Layered Potato, Egg & Sausage Bake)
Sliced potatoes, hard-boiled egg, and smoked sausage layered with sour cream and baked until bubbling and golden — one of Hungary's most beloved comfort-food casseroles, first written down in an 1840 Hungarian cookbook.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Potatoes, waxy, boiled and sliced | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Eggs, hard-boiled and sliced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Hungarian smoked sausage (kolbász), sliced | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Sour cream | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Butter, for the dish | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Sweet Hungarian paprika, for topping | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Preheat the oven to 190°C (375°F). Grease a baking dish with the butter.
2. Layer a third of the sliced potatoes in the bottom of the dish and season with salt and pepper.
3. Top with half the sliced egg and half the sausage, then dollop over a third of the sour cream.
4. Repeat the layers once more, then finish with a top layer of potatoes and the last of the sour cream spread evenly over the top.
5. Sprinkle with paprika.
6. Bake 30–35 minutes, until bubbling and lightly golden on top.
7. Let rest 5 minutes before serving hot.

### 8. Somlói Galuska (Bonus — Hungarian Trifle Sponge Dessert)
Cubes of cocoa and walnut sponge cake, soaked in rum syrup and layered with vanilla custard, whipped cream, chocolate, and walnuts — created for the 1958 Brussels World's Fair and still Hungary's most famous dessert.

**Sponge Cake**

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All-purpose flour | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Granulated sugar | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Egg | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Baking powder | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Cocoa powder, for half the batter | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Walnuts, chopped, for half the batter | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |

**Rum Syrup**

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Water | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Granulated sugar, for syrup | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Dark rum | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Raisins | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Vanilla Custard**

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Milk | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Granulated sugar, for custard | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Egg yolk | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Cornstarch | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Vanilla extract | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |

**Topping**

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Heavy cream, whipped | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Dark chocolate, melted, for drizzle | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Walnuts, chopped, for topping | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Steps:**
1. Preheat the oven to 180°C (350°F) and line a small baking pan.
2. Whisk the egg and sugar together until pale, then fold in the flour and baking powder to make a smooth batter.
3. Split the batter in half. Stir the cocoa powder into one half and the chopped walnuts (for the batter) into the other half.
4. Spread each half into the pan side by side, keeping them separate, and bake 12–15 minutes, until springy to the touch. Let cool, then cut into cubes.
5. Meanwhile, make the rum syrup: warm the water and sugar together until the sugar dissolves, remove from the heat, and stir in the rum. Soak the raisins in a splash of the warm syrup.
6. Make the custard: whisk the egg yolk, sugar, and cornstarch together, then whisk in the milk. Return to low heat and stir constantly until thickened. Stir in the vanilla and let cool.
7. To assemble, layer the cocoa and walnut sponge cubes in a glass or dish, brushing each layer generously with the rum syrup and scattering over the soaked raisins.
8. Spoon the vanilla custard over the top.
9. Finish with the whipped cream, a drizzle of melted chocolate, and a scattering of the walnuts for topping.
10. Chill briefly, then serve.
