### 1. Butter Chicken (Murgh Makhani)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thigh, boneless skinless | 120g | 240g | 360g | 480g |
| Plain yogurt | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Ginger-garlic paste | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Lemon juice | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Kashmiri chili powder | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Butter | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Onion, chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Tomato passata | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Cashews, raw | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Heavy cream | 20ml | 40ml | 60ml | 80ml |
| Garam masala | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Kasuri methi (dried fenugreek leaves) | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Sugar | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Cut the chicken thighs into bite-size pieces. In a bowl, mix the chicken with the yogurt, half the ginger-garlic paste, the lemon juice, and half the Kashmiri chili powder. Cover and marinate in the fridge at least 20 minutes — the longer it sits, even overnight, the more tender and flavorful the chicken gets.
2. Ask an adult to help heat a splash of oil in a pan over medium-high heat until it shimmers. Add the marinated chicken in a single layer (cook in batches if the pan looks crowded) and sear 3-4 minutes per side, until the outside turns golden-brown with some dark charred spots — that char is where a lot of the "tandoori" flavor comes from. Set the chicken aside; it doesn't need to be fully cooked yet, since it will finish cooking in the sauce.
3. In the same pan, melt half the butter over medium heat. Add the chopped onion and cook, stirring often, 4-5 minutes until soft and see-through, not browned.
4. Stir in the remaining ginger-garlic paste and cook 30 seconds, just until you can smell it — garlic burns fast, so keep stirring.
5. Add the tomato passata, cashews, remaining Kashmiri chili powder, and a splash of water. Simmer gently 8-10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the tomato deepens from bright red to a darker brick-red and a little oil starts to separate at the edges.
6. Ask an adult to help carefully transfer the sauce to a blender (let it cool a couple minutes first — hot liquid can burst the lid off). Blend until completely smooth, then pour it back into the pan.
7. Return the seared chicken and its juices to the pan. Simmer gently 8-10 minutes, until a piece cut in half shows no pink inside.
8. Stir in the sugar, remaining butter, and the cream. The sauce should turn a lighter orange-red and taste rich, a little sweet, and a little tangy.
9. Crush the kasuri methi between your fingers to release its fragrance and sprinkle it in, along with a final pinch of garam masala. Simmer 2 more minutes.
10. Taste and adjust — a little more salt if it's flat, a little more cream if it's too spicy. Serve hot with steamed basmati rice or warm naan.

### 2. Dal Tadka (Yellow Lentil Dal)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Split yellow lentils (toor or moong dal) | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Water (for cooking dal) | 250ml | 500ml | 750ml | 1000ml |
| Turmeric | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Ghee | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 tbsp | 4 tsp |
| Cumin seeds | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Asafoetida (hing) | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Garlic, sliced | 1 clove | 2 cloves | 3 cloves | 4 cloves |
| Dried red chili | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Onion, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Tomato, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |

**Steps:**
1. Rinse the lentils in a bowl of water, swishing with your hand, then draining — repeat 2-3 times until the water runs mostly clear instead of cloudy. This washes off extra starch so the dal doesn't turn gluey.
2. Put the rinsed lentils, water, turmeric, and a pinch of salt in a pot. Ask an adult to help bring it to a boil over high heat, then turn the heat down to low, cover with the lid slightly ajar, and simmer.
3. Cook 20-25 minutes, stirring occasionally and skimming off any white foam that rises to the top. The dal is ready when the lentils are completely soft and mushy — press one against the side of the pot with a spoon; it should squish easily with no firm center.
4. Whisk the cooked dal until it turns creamy and slightly thick, like a hearty soup. If it looks too thick, stir in a splash more hot water.
5. Now make the tadka (tempering), which gives dal its signature aroma: heat the ghee in a small pan over medium heat until fully melted and hot — a cumin seed dropped in should sizzle right away.
6. Add the cumin seeds, dried red chili, and a pinch of asafoetida (hing). Let them sizzle about 15 seconds until the cumin turns a shade darker and smells toasty — don't let it turn black or it will taste bitter.
7. Add the garlic and cook 30 seconds until light golden. Add the onion and cook 3-4 minutes until soft and lightly browned at the edges.
8. Add the chopped tomato and cook 2-3 minutes until it turns soft and pulpy.
9. Carefully pour this sizzling tadka straight into the pot of cooked dal — stand back a little, since it will hiss and sputter when it hits the liquid. Stir it in, taste and add more salt if needed, and sprinkle with fresh cilantro before serving with rice or roti.

### 3. Chicken Biryani (Hyderabadi-Style)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basmati rice | 75g | 150g | 225g | 300g |
| Chicken thigh, cut into pieces | 130g | 260g | 390g | 520g |
| Plain yogurt | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Ginger-garlic paste | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Red chili powder | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Garam masala | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Crispy fried onions | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Mint leaves, chopped | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Cilantro, chopped | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Ghee | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Bay leaf | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Green cardamom pods | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Cloves | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 |
| Cinnamon stick (small) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Saffron threads + warm milk | pinch + 1 tbsp | pinch + 2 tbsp | ¼ tsp + 3 tbsp | ¼ tsp + 4 tbsp |

**Steps:**
1. Cut the chicken into pieces and mix with the yogurt, ginger-garlic paste, red chili powder, half the garam masala, and a pinch of salt in a bowl. Cover and marinate at least 30 minutes in the fridge (longer is better — even a few hours).
2. Rinse the basmati rice in a bowl of water until it runs mostly clear, then soak it in fresh water 20-30 minutes. This helps the grains cook up long and separate instead of clumping together.
3. Ask an adult to help bring a large pot of water to a rolling boil — a big, vigorous, bubbling boil, not a gentle simmer. Add the bay leaf, cinnamon stick, cardamom pods, cloves, and a generous pinch of salt — the water should taste almost like seawater, which seasons the rice from the inside.
4. Drain the soaked rice and add it to the boiling water. Cook uncovered 5-6 minutes — you want the rice about 70% cooked, meaning if you bite a grain, the outside is soft but there's still a firm, chalky-white core in the center. Drain immediately in a colander so it doesn't overcook.
5. While the rice cooks, ask an adult to help heat the ghee in a wide, heavy pot with a tight-fitting lid over medium-high heat. Add the marinated chicken (shake off excess marinade first) and sear 2-3 minutes per side, until lightly browned on the outside. It doesn't need to be fully cooked yet — it finishes during the final steaming step.
6. Spread the seared chicken and its juices evenly across the bottom of the pot.
7. Layer the partially cooked rice on top of the chicken, spreading it into an even layer with a spoon — don't stir it into the chicken, just layer it on top like a blanket.
8. Sprinkle the fried onions, chopped mint, chopped cilantro, and remaining garam masala over the rice. If using saffron, stir it into the warm milk until golden-orange, then drizzle it over the rice in a few streaks.
9. Cover the pot with the tight-fitting lid (add a layer of foil underneath first if it doesn't seal well — you want as little steam escaping as possible). Turn the heat to very low.
10. Cook 25-30 minutes without lifting the lid — this slow steaming is called "dum," and it's what gently finishes cooking the rice and chicken together while the flavors mingle. Peeking lets out steam you need, so be patient.
11. Turn off the heat and let the pot rest, still covered, 10 minutes.
12. Remove the lid and check the chicken is fully cooked (cut a piece open — no pink, juices run clear). Using a large spoon, gently fluff and mix the rice and chicken together from the bottom up so every scoop gets a bit of both. Serve hot, with raita or a simple cucumber salad on the side if you like.

### 4. Chana Masala (Chickpea Curry)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Canned chickpeas, drained | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Onion, chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Tomato, chopped | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Ginger-garlic paste | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Cumin seeds | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Ground coriander | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Ground cumin | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Turmeric | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Red chili powder | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Chana masala spice blend | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Amchur (or lemon juice) | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Neutral oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help heat the oil in a pot over medium heat until it shimmers. Add the cumin seeds and let them sizzle about 10 seconds, until they turn a shade darker and smell toasty.
2. Add the chopped onion and cook, stirring often, 5-6 minutes until soft and golden-brown — this slow browning builds a lot of the dish's flavor, so don't rush it.
3. Stir in the ginger-garlic paste and cook 30 seconds, just until fragrant.
4. Add the ground coriander, ground cumin, turmeric, and red chili powder. Stir constantly about 20 seconds — this "blooms" the spices in the oil so they taste rounder instead of raw and dusty. If the pan looks dry, splash in a spoonful of water so the spices don't burn.
5. Add the chopped tomato and cook 5-6 minutes, mashing it a bit with your spatula, until it breaks down into a thick, jammy sauce and the oil starts to separate at the edges of the pan.
6. Add the drained chickpeas and about ½ cup water per serving (use more for bigger batches so everything stays saucy, not dry). Stir well so every chickpea gets coated.
7. Bring to a gentle simmer, partly cover, and cook 10-12 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens slightly and the chickpeas have soaked up a lot of the flavor. For an extra-creamy texture, mash a few chickpeas against the side of the pot with your spoon.
8. Stir in the chana masala spice blend and amchur (or a squeeze of lemon juice). Simmer 2 more minutes, then taste — it should be warm, tangy, and a little smoky.
9. Turn off the heat, sprinkle with fresh cilantro, and serve hot with rice, roti, or warm naan.

### 5. Paneer Tikka Masala
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paneer, cubed | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Bell pepper, chunked | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Onion, chunked (for searing) | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Plain yogurt (marinade) | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Ginger-garlic paste | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Kashmiri chili powder | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Garam masala | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Neutral oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Butter (gravy) | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Onion, pureed (gravy) | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Tomato passata (gravy) | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Cashews (gravy) | 8g | 16g | 24g | 32g |
| Heavy cream (gravy) | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Sugar | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Kasuri methi | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Cut the paneer into bite-size cubes and the bell pepper and onion into similar-sized chunks, so everything cooks evenly.
2. In a bowl, mix the yogurt, ginger-garlic paste, Kashmiri chili powder, and half the garam masala into a marinade. Add the paneer, bell pepper, and onion and gently toss to coat — be gentle, since paneer cubes crumble if you stir too hard. Marinate 15-20 minutes.
3. Ask an adult to help heat the oil in a grill pan or regular pan over medium-high heat. Add the marinated paneer and vegetables in a single layer (don't crowd the pan) and sear 2-3 minutes per side, until the paneer picks up golden-brown char spots and the peppers soften slightly at the edges. Set aside.
4. In the same pan, melt the butter over medium heat. Add the gravy onion and cook 4-5 minutes, stirring often, until soft and see-through.
5. Add the tomato passata and cashews. Simmer 8-10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the tomato deepens in color and thickens slightly.
6. Ask an adult to help carefully blend this sauce until smooth (let it cool a few minutes first so it doesn't splatter or burst the blender lid), then return it to the pan.
7. Stir in the cream, sugar, and remaining garam masala. The sauce should turn a soft orange and taste rich and a little sweet.
8. Add the seared paneer, bell pepper, and onion back into the sauce. Simmer gently 3-4 minutes, just enough to warm everything through and let the paneer soak up some sauce — stir gently so the cubes stay whole.
9. Crush the kasuri methi between your fingers and sprinkle it over the top, stirring it in.
10. Taste and add a little salt if needed, then serve hot with naan or steamed rice.

### 6. Kerala Fish Curry
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| White fish fillet (tilapia, cod, or snapper) | 130g | 260g | 390g | 520g |
| Coconut milk | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Turmeric | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Red chili powder | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Ground coriander | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Fenugreek seeds | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Mustard seeds | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Curry leaves | 6 | 12 | 18 | 24 |
| Shallot, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Tomato, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Ginger, julienned | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Garlic, sliced | 1 clove | 2 cloves | 3 cloves | 4 cloves |
| Tamarind paste | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Coconut oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Pat the fish chunks dry with a paper towel and rub them with a pinch of turmeric and salt. Set aside — this pre-seasoning firms up the fish slightly and seasons it from the start.
2. Ask an adult to help heat the coconut oil in a pot over medium heat until it shimmers. Add the mustard seeds and stand back a little — they'll pop and crackle within a few seconds, which is normal.
3. As soon as most of the seeds have popped, add the fenugreek seeds and curry leaves. Fry about 10 seconds until the curry leaves turn a shade darker and crisp slightly — they'll also crackle a bit, so keep your face back.
4. Add the sliced shallot, ginger, and garlic. Cook 3-4 minutes, stirring often, until the shallot turns soft and light golden.
5. Add the remaining turmeric, red chili powder, and ground coriander. Stir constantly about 15 seconds so the spices toast in the oil without burning — if it looks dry, splash in a spoonful of water.
6. Add the sliced tomato and cook 2-3 minutes until it softens and starts to break down.
7. Pour in the coconut milk and tamarind paste, and stir well. Bring to a gentle simmer — small bubbles breaking the surface, not a hard boil, since boiling coconut milk too hard can make it look curdled and separated.
8. Carefully slide the fish pieces into the simmering curry in a single layer. Let them cook gently 6-8 minutes without stirring too much (a gentle shake of the pot instead of a spoon keeps the fish from breaking apart) — the fish is done when it turns from translucent to opaque white all the way through and flakes apart easily when pressed with a fork.
9. Taste the sauce and add a little more salt or tamarind if it needs it — it should taste rich, tangy, and a little spicy. Serve hot over steamed rice.

### 7. Lamb Rogan Josh
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lamb shoulder, stew chunks | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Plain yogurt | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Kashmiri chili powder | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Ground fennel | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Onion, sliced | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Garlic, minced | 2 cloves | 4 cloves | 6 cloves | 8 cloves |
| Ginger, minced | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Bay leaf | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Green cardamom pods | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Cinnamon stick (small) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Cloves | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Ghee | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Water | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |

**Steps:**
1. In a bowl, mix the lamb chunks with the yogurt, Kashmiri chili powder, and ground fennel. Cover and marinate at least 30 minutes (or up to overnight in the fridge) — Kashmiri chili powder is mild and mostly for color, so this dish won't be blazing hot.
2. Ask an adult to help heat the ghee in a heavy pot over medium heat. Add the bay leaf, cardamom pods, cinnamon stick, and cloves and let them sizzle about 20 seconds, until they smell strongly fragrant — this wakes up their flavor before anything else goes in.
3. Add the sliced onion and cook, stirring occasionally, 6-8 minutes until it turns a deep golden-brown, not just soft — real color here means real flavor later. Lower the heat if it browns too fast.
4. Add the garlic and ginger and cook 30 seconds until fragrant.
5. Add the marinated lamb along with all its marinade. Turn the heat up to medium-high and cook, stirring occasionally, 5-6 minutes until the outside of the lamb turns from pink to gray-brown all over.
6. Pour in the water and stir, scraping up any browned bits stuck to the bottom of the pot — those bits are packed with flavor.
7. Bring to a boil, then turn the heat down low so the pot is just gently bubbling. Cover with a lid, leaving a small gap for steam to escape.
8. Simmer slowly 45-60 minutes, stirring every 15 minutes or so, until the lamb is fork-tender — a fork slides in and twists the meat apart easily, with almost no resistance. If the sauce looks too thin at the end, simmer uncovered the last 10 minutes to thicken it; if it looks too dry, add a splash more water.
9. Taste and adjust the salt. The sauce should be deep red, rich, and warmly spiced rather than fiery hot.
10. Fish out the bay leaf, cinnamon stick, and cloves before serving if you can find them (or just warn everyone at the table to watch for them, the same way you would with a soup). Serve hot with steamed rice or warm naan.

### 8. Gulab Jamun (Bonus)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full-fat milk powder | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| All-purpose flour | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Baking soda | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Ghee, melted | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Milk (to bind dough) | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Sugar (for syrup) | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Water (for syrup) | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Cardamom pods, crushed | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Rose water | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Oil or ghee for deep frying | as needed | as needed | as needed | as needed |

**Steps:**
1. First make the syrup so it's ready when the fried balls come out: in a small pot, combine the sugar, water, and crushed cardamom pods. Ask an adult to help bring it to a boil over medium heat, stirring until the sugar fully dissolves.
2. Once it boils, turn the heat down to low and simmer gently 5 minutes, until it thins out slightly and turns a bit syrupy — it shouldn't be as thick as honey, thinner, so the fried balls can soak it up. Turn off the heat, stir in the rose water, and set the pot aside somewhere warm.
3. In a mixing bowl, combine the milk powder, flour, and baking soda.
4. Add the melted ghee and rub it into the dry mixture with your fingertips until it looks like damp sand.
5. Add the milk a little at a time, kneading gently, until it comes together into a soft, smooth dough — like play dough, not sticky on your fingers and not cracked and dry. Don't overwork it, or the balls can turn tough.
6. Divide the dough into small equal portions and roll each one between your palms into a smooth ball with no cracks on the surface — cracks will cause the balls to split open in the hot oil, so take your time smoothing them out.
7. Ask an adult to help heat oil or ghee in a deep, heavy pot over medium-low heat. Test the temperature by dropping in a tiny pinch of dough — it should sink first, then slowly float back up within a few seconds without any aggressive bubbling. If it browns immediately, the oil is too hot and the balls will burn outside while staying raw inside.
8. Carefully lower the dough balls into the oil a few at a time using a slotted spoon — never drop them in from a height, to avoid splashing hot oil. Don't crowd the pot.
9. Fry gently, rolling the balls around often with the slotted spoon so they brown on all sides evenly, 6-8 minutes total, until they turn a deep, even reddish-brown all over, like the color of a chestnut, not just tan.
10. Lift the fried balls out with the slotted spoon, let the extra oil drip off for a moment, then slide them directly into the warm syrup while they're still hot. This is important — warm balls into warm syrup soak up the sweetness properly; cold balls won't absorb it well.
11. Let the balls soak in the syrup at least 30 minutes (they'll puff up slightly and turn soft and syrup-soaked all the way through) before serving warm or at room temperature.
