## Recipe Cards

### 1. Nasi Goreng (Indonesian Fried Rice)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cooked jasmine rice, chilled/day-old | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Chicken breast, diced | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Shallots, thinly sliced | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Garlic clove, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Sambal oelek (or similar chili paste) | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Terasi (shrimp paste) | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Kecap manis (sweet soy sauce) | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Light soy sauce | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Egg (for frying on top) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Vegetable oil | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Cucumber, sliced (garnish) | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Tomato, wedges (garnish) | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Fried shallots (garnish) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Steps:**
1. If using freshly cooked rice, spread it on a tray and chill uncovered at least 30 minutes — day-old, dried-out rice is what keeps the grains separate instead of turning mushy.
2. Dry-toast the terasi wrapped in foil over a low flame 30 seconds per side until fragrant, then crumble (or skip this if using a pre-toasted shrimp paste).
3. Heat the oil in a wok over high heat. Add the shallots and garlic and stir-fry 1 minute until fragrant.
4. Add the chicken and stir-fry 3–4 minutes until cooked through.
5. Push everything to one side of the wok, add the sambal oelek and terasi to the cleared space, and fry 30 seconds until fragrant.
6. Add the cold rice, breaking up any clumps with the back of the spatula, and toss to combine.
7. Pour the kecap manis and light soy sauce over the rice and stir-fry 3–4 minutes until every grain is coated a deep glossy brown.
8. In a separate small pan, fry the eggs sunny-side up.
9. Plate the fried rice topped with a fried egg, cucumber slices, tomato wedges, and crispy fried shallots.

### 2. Sate Ayam (Chicken Satay with Peanut Sauce)
**Chicken & Marinade — Ingredients by Serving**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, boneless & skinless, cubed | 130g | 260g | 390g | 520g |
| Kecap manis (sweet soy sauce) | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Vegetable oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Garlic clove, minced | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Ground coriander | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Peanut Sauce — Ingredients by Serving**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Roasted peanuts, ground | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Candlenuts (kemiri) or macadamia | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Shallot | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Garlic clove | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Red chilies (bird's eye, or to taste) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Palm sugar | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Kecap manis | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Tamarind pulp (mixed with a little water) | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Water (to thin sauce) | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Combine the chicken with the kecap manis, oil, garlic, and coriander in a bowl. Mix well, cover, and marinate in the fridge at least 30 minutes.
2. Meanwhile, make the peanut sauce: pound or blend the peanuts, candlenuts, shallot, garlic, and chilies into a coarse paste.
3. Fry the paste in a splash of oil over medium heat 3–4 minutes until fragrant and the oil starts to separate slightly.
4. Add the palm sugar, kecap manis, tamarind, salt, and water. Simmer 8–10 minutes, stirring often, until thickened to a pourable, clingy sauce. Set aside.
5. Thread the marinated chicken onto skewers (soak bamboo skewers in water first if using).
6. Grill or broil the skewers 8–10 minutes, turning and basting with the leftover marinade, until charred in spots and cooked through with no pink at the center.
7. Warm the peanut sauce back through if it has thickened too much, thinning with a splash of water.
8. Serve the skewers hot with the peanut sauce spooned over or served alongside, with steamed rice or lontong and a lime wedge.

### 3. Gado-Gado (Vegetable Salad with Peanut Sauce)

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Roasted peanuts, ground | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Garlic clove | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Red chilies | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Palm sugar | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Tamarind pulp (mixed with a little water) | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Kecap manis | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Terasi (shrimp paste), toasted | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Water (to thin sauce) | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Salad — Ingredients by Serving**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Potato, boiled & sliced | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Long beans (or green beans), blanched | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Bean sprouts, blanched | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Cabbage, shredded & blanched | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Cucumber, sliced | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Firm tofu, fried & cubed | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Tempeh, fried & cubed | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Hard-boiled egg | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Fried shallots (garnish) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Steps:**
1. Make the peanut sauce: blend or pound the peanuts, garlic, chilies, and terasi into a paste.
2. Combine the paste with the palm sugar, tamarind, kecap manis, salt, and water in a small saucepan.
3. Simmer 5–6 minutes, stirring, until slightly thickened and pourable. Set aside to cool slightly.
4. Blanch the long beans, bean sprouts, and cabbage separately in boiling water 1–2 minutes each until just tender-crisp, then drain and cool.
5. Arrange the boiled potato, blanched vegetables, cucumber, fried tofu, fried tempeh, and hard-boiled egg on a plate.
6. Spoon the peanut sauce generously over the top.
7. Scatter with crispy fried shallots and serve with krupuk crackers on the side.

### 4. Ayam Goreng (Indonesian Fried Chicken)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs or drumsticks, bone-in | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Shallots | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Garlic cloves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ground coriander | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Ground turmeric | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Galangal, sliced | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Bay leaf (or Indonesian salam leaf) | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Lemongrass stalk, bruised | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Coconut water (or water) | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Vegetable oil (for frying) | 250ml | 500ml | 750ml | 1000ml |

**Steps:**
1. Blend the shallots and garlic into a smooth paste (a splash of water helps), then stir in the coriander, turmeric, and salt.
2. Rub the spice paste all over the chicken pieces in a wide pot or deep skillet.
3. Add the galangal, bay leaf, lemongrass, and coconut water, and bring to a simmer — coconut water is the traditional braising liquid and gives the chicken its characteristic mellow sweetness; plain water works in a pinch.
4. Cover and simmer over low heat 20–25 minutes, turning once, until the chicken is cooked through and the liquid has mostly reduced.
5. Remove the chicken and let it sit uncovered 5–10 minutes to air-dry the surface — this is what lets it crisp up without any flour or batter.
6. Heat the oil in a wok or deep skillet to 175°C (350°F).
7. Fry the chicken in batches 5–7 minutes per side until deeply golden and crisp — the meat is already fully cooked, so you're frying purely for color and crunch.
8. Drain on a wire rack or paper towels and serve hot with steamed rice, sliced cucumber, and sambal.

### 5. Mie Goreng (Indonesian Fried Noodles)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fresh yellow egg noodles (or dried, boiled) | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Chicken breast, sliced (or shrimp) | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Shallots, sliced | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Red chili, sliced (optional) | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Cabbage, shredded | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Carrot, julienned | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Kecap manis | 20ml | 40ml | 60ml | 80ml |
| Dark soy sauce | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Oyster sauce | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Egg | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Vegetable oil | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Fried shallots (garnish) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Lime wedge | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |

**Steps:**
1. If using dried noodles, boil until just tender, then drain and toss with a little oil so they don't stick together.
2. Heat the oil in a wok over high heat. Add the garlic, shallots, and chili, stir-frying 1 minute until fragrant.
3. Add the chicken (or shrimp) and stir-fry 2–3 minutes until nearly cooked through.
4. Push everything to one side, crack the egg into the cleared space, and scramble briefly before mixing it through.
5. Add the cabbage and carrot, stir-frying 1–2 minutes until just starting to soften.
6. Add the noodles, kecap manis, dark soy sauce, and oyster sauce. Toss everything together over high heat 2–3 minutes until the noodles are evenly coated and slightly charred at the edges.
7. Taste and adjust the seasoning.
8. Plate the noodles topped with crispy fried shallots, with a lime wedge on the side.

### 6. Beef Rendang (this week's special)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beef chuck or shin, cut into chunks | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Coconut milk | 200ml | 400ml | 600ml | 800ml |
| Shallots | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Garlic cloves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ginger, sliced | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Galangal, sliced | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Lemongrass stalk, bruised | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Dried red chilies, soaked (or chili paste to taste) | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 |
| Kerisik (toasted grated coconut) | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Ground turmeric | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Tamarind pulp | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Kaffir lime leaves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Palm sugar | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |
| Vegetable oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |

**Steps:**
1. Blend the shallots, garlic, ginger, galangal, and soaked dried chilies into a smooth spice paste, adding a splash of water if needed.
2. This is the week's special dish — give it a little more stovetop time than the rest of the week, but there's no need to babysit it all day; it's a weeknight-feasible simmer, not an all-day project.
3. Heat the oil in a heavy pot or Dutch oven over medium heat. Fry the spice paste 5–7 minutes, stirring often, until fragrant and darkened.
4. Add the lemongrass, turmeric, and kaffir lime leaves, frying 1 minute more.
5. Add the beef and stir to coat every piece in the paste, searing 3–4 minutes.
6. Pour in the coconut milk, tamarind, palm sugar, and salt, and bring to a gentle simmer.
7. Reduce the heat to low and simmer uncovered, stirring occasionally, 1½–2 hours, until the sauce has reduced and thickened and the beef is completely tender.
8. Stir in the kerisik (toasted coconut) in the last 20 minutes — this is what gives rendang its signature dry, deeply toasted finish.
9. Keep simmering, stirring more frequently as the sauce thickens, until the oil separates slightly and the sauce clings to the meat rather than pooling around it.
10. Discard the lemongrass stalk and lime leaves, taste and adjust the seasoning, and serve hot with steamed rice.

### 7. Sayur Lodeh (Vegetable Coconut Curry)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shallots | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Garlic cloves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Red chilies | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Candlenuts (kemiri) or macadamia | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Galangal, sliced | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Ground coriander | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Terasi (shrimp paste) | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Coconut milk | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Water or stock | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Asian eggplant, chunked | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Long beans (or green beans), cut | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Tempeh, cubed | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Tomato, chopped | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Bay leaf | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Palm sugar | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Vegetable oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |

**Steps:**
1. Blend the shallots, garlic, chilies, candlenuts, galangal, and coriander into a smooth paste.
2. Heat the oil in a pot over medium heat. Fry the spice paste with the terasi and bay leaf 3–4 minutes until fragrant.
3. Pour in the coconut milk and water, stirring to combine. Bring to a gentle simmer — don't let it boil hard, or the coconut milk can split.
4. Add the eggplant and long beans, and simmer 5 minutes.
5. Add the tempeh and tomato, and simmer 5–8 minutes more until the vegetables are tender.
6. Season with the palm sugar and salt to taste.
7. Discard the bay leaf and serve hot with steamed rice.

### 8. Pisang Goreng (Fried Banana Fritters) (Bonus)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ripe saba banana or plantain, halved lengthwise | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| All-purpose flour | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Rice flour | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Sugar | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Baking powder | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Salt | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Water | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Vegetable oil (for frying) | 250ml | 500ml | 750ml | 1000ml |
| Honey or sweetened condensed milk (to finish, optional) | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |

**Steps:**
1. Whisk the all-purpose flour, rice flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt together in a bowl.
2. Gradually whisk in the water until you have a smooth, medium-thick batter, similar to pancake batter.
3. Heat the oil in a wok or deep skillet to 175°C (350°F).
4. Dip each piece of banana into the batter, coating it fully.
5. Carefully lower the battered bananas into the hot oil, frying in batches so the pan isn't crowded.
6. Fry 3–4 minutes, turning occasionally, until deep golden brown and crisp all over.
7. Drain on a wire rack or paper towels.
8. Drizzle with honey or condensed milk while still warm, and serve immediately.
