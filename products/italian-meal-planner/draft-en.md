### 1. Tagliatelle al Ragù alla Bolognese
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tagliatelle (or fettuccine) | 90g | 180g | 270g | 360g |
| Ground beef | 70g | 140g | 210g | 280g |
| Ground pork | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Pancetta (diced) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Onion | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Carrot | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Celery | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Tomato paste | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Crushed tomatoes (canned) | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Whole milk | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Dry white wine | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Bay leaf | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Parmesan (grated, for serving) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |

**Steps:**
1. Finely chop the onion, carrot, and celery into small, even pieces about the size of a pencil eraser — this trio is called *soffritto* in Italian, and cutting it small helps it melt into the sauce instead of staying crunchy. Ask an adult to help with the knife work if you're still learning.
2. Ask an adult to help you put a large, heavy pot (a Dutch oven is perfect) over medium heat. Add the diced pancetta and cook 2–3 minutes, stirring, until it turns golden and releases its fat — you'll see clear, glistening fat pooling in the pan.
3. Add the chopped onion, carrot, and celery to the pancetta fat. Cook 5–6 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the vegetables turn soft and a little see-through, but not brown — this slow softening builds the base flavor of the whole sauce.
4. Turn the heat up to medium-high and add the ground beef and ground pork. Break the meat apart with your spatula into small crumbles, like breaking apart cookie dough, and cook 5–7 minutes until no pink remains.
5. Add the tomato paste and stir it into the meat for 1–2 minutes until it turns from bright red to a deeper brick-red color — this toasts the paste and cooks away its raw, sour edge.
6. Pour in the white wine and stir, scraping up any browned bits stuck to the bottom of the pot with your spatula (this is called deglazing, and those stuck bits are packed with flavor). Let it bubble 2–3 minutes until most of the sharp wine smell has cooked off.
7. Add the crushed tomatoes and bay leaf, and stir well. Once it starts to bubble, turn the heat down low so it's just barely simmering — a few lazy bubbles breaking the surface every second or two, not a rolling boil.
8. Pour in the milk (yes, milk — this is a traditional Bolognese trick that rounds out the sauce and takes away sharpness) and stir it in. Put a lid on the pot, leaving a small gap for steam to escape, and simmer 30–35 minutes, stirring every 10 minutes so nothing sticks to the bottom.
9. While the sauce simmers, ask an adult to help bring a large pot of well-salted water to a rolling boil (it should taste like the sea) for the pasta. In the last 10 minutes of cooking, boil the tagliatelle according to the package directions until al dente — tender but with a tiny bit of firmness left when you bite it.
10. Scoop out a mugful of the starchy pasta water before draining, then drain the pasta. Remove the bay leaf from the sauce.
11. Add the drained pasta directly into the pot with the sauce and toss well so every strand gets coated — if it looks dry, splash in a little of the reserved pasta water.
12. Serve topped with plenty of grated Parmesan.

### 2. Linguine alle Vongole
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Littleneck clams | 350g | 700g | 1050g | 1400g |
| Linguine | 90g | 180g | 270g | 360g |
| Garlic (cloves) | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Olive oil | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Dry white wine | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Red pepper flakes | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Fresh parsley (chopped) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Lemon | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |

**Steps:**
1. Rinse the clams under cold running water, scrubbing off any sand or grit with your fingers or a soft brush. Tap any open clams gently on the counter — if they don't close up within a minute, they're dead and you should throw them out. This is an important food-safety step, not an optional one.
2. Fill a bowl with cold water and a big pinch of salt, and let the clams sit in it for 20–30 minutes. This encourages them to spit out any hidden sand so you don't bite into grit later.
3. Ask an adult to help bring a large pot of salted water to a rolling boil for the linguine. Cook it according to the package directions until al dente (tender with a little firmness left), then drain, saving a mugful of the starchy cooking water.
4. While the pasta cooks, thinly slice the garlic. Ask an adult to help heat the olive oil in a wide, deep pan over medium heat until it shimmers.
5. Add the sliced garlic and red pepper flakes to the hot oil and stir about 30 seconds, just until you can smell the garlic — don't let it turn brown or it will taste bitter.
6. Carefully lift the clams out of their soaking water (leave the sand behind in the bowl) and add them to the pan. Pour in the white wine, then immediately cover the pan with a lid — the wine will bubble up into steam that forces the clams open.
7. Let the clams steam 5–7 minutes, shaking the pan occasionally, until the shells pop open. Important: any clams that stay tightly shut after 7–8 minutes did not cook properly and must be thrown away, not eaten.
8. Using tongs, scoop the opened clams into a bowl and set aside, leaving all that flavorful liquid behind in the pan.
9. Add the drained linguine straight into the pan with the clam liquid and toss well over medium heat for 1–2 minutes so the pasta soaks up the briny sauce. If it looks dry, splash in some of the reserved pasta water.
10. Return the clams to the pan, sprinkle in the chopped parsley, and toss once more just to warm everything through.
11. Serve right away with a lemon wedge on the side to squeeze over the top.

### 3. Risotto ai Funghi
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arborio rice | 70g | 140g | 210g | 280g |
| Cremini mushrooms (fresh) | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Dried porcini mushrooms | 3g | 6g | 9g | 12g |
| Onion | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Garlic (cloves) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Vegetable or chicken stock | 350ml | 700ml | 1050ml | 1400ml |
| Dry white wine | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Butter | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Parmesan (grated) | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Olive oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |

**Steps:**
1. Put the dried porcini mushrooms in a small bowl and pour just enough hot water over them to cover. Soak 15–20 minutes until soft, then lift them out (save the soaking liquid!) and chop them finely. Strain the soaking liquid through a paper towel to catch any grit, and set it aside — it's packed with flavor.
2. Ask an adult to help you heat the stock (stir the reserved porcini liquid into it) in a small pot over low heat, just to keep it warm and steamy — not boiling. Keep it on the back burner near your risotto pan the whole time you cook.
3. Finely chop the onion and garlic. In a wide, heavy pan, heat the olive oil and half the butter over medium heat until the butter melts and foams slightly.
4. Add the onion and cook 3–4 minutes, stirring, until soft and see-through but not browned. Add the garlic and fresh mushrooms and cook 4–5 minutes more, until the mushrooms shrink and turn deep golden brown — this browning (not steaming) is what gives them their savory flavor, so don't crowd the pan.
5. Stir in the chopped porcini and the arborio rice. Stir constantly for 1–2 minutes until the edges of the rice grains look a little see-through — this toasting step helps the rice hold its shape while cooking.
6. Pour in the white wine and stir until it's almost completely absorbed and you can't smell raw alcohol anymore.
7. Now add the warm stock one ladleful at a time. Stir often and wait until each ladleful is almost fully absorbed (the pan looks just a little dry) before adding the next. This slow process, done over about 18–20 minutes, is what pulls the starch out of the rice and makes it creamy.
8. Keep tasting the rice as you go. It's done when it's creamy and just tender with a tiny firm bite left in the center — not crunchy, not mushy. You may not need all the stock, or you might need a splash more.
9. Turn off the heat. Stir in the remaining butter and the Parmesan vigorously — Italians call this final step *mantecatura*, and beating it in hard makes the risotto extra glossy and creamy.
10. Let the risotto sit, covered, for 2 minutes before serving — it should slowly spread out on the plate like a wave, not sit in a stiff mound. If it looks too thick, stir in a splash more warm stock.

### 4. Minestrone alla Genovese
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Onion | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Carrot | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Celery | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Zucchini | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Potato | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Green beans | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Canned diced tomatoes | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Cannellini beans (canned, drained) | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Ditalini (small pasta) | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Vegetable stock | 300ml | 600ml | 900ml | 1200ml |
| Basil pesto (for topping) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Parmesan (grated) | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Olive oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |

**Steps:**
1. Chop the onion, carrot, and celery into small, even cubes about the size of a die. Ask an adult to help with the knife if needed.
2. Ask an adult to help heat the olive oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion, carrot, and celery and cook 5–6 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the onion turns soft and see-through.
3. While that cooks, cut the zucchini, potato, and green beans into bite-sized pieces, roughly the size of a large coin.
4. Add the potato to the pot first (it takes the longest to cook) along with the canned tomatoes. Stir and cook 2 minutes.
5. Pour in the vegetable stock and bring it up to a boil — you'll see big bubbles breaking rapidly across the whole surface. Once boiling, turn the heat down so it's simmering gently, just a few bubbles here and there.
6. Let it simmer 10 minutes, then add the zucchini and green beans. Simmer another 8–10 minutes, until you can easily slide a fork into a piece of potato with no resistance.
7. Add the drained cannellini beans and the ditalini pasta. Simmer another 8–9 minutes, or however long the pasta package says, until the pasta is tender.
8. Carefully taste a spoonful (it's hot!) and add salt and pepper if it needs it. The soup should taste like a warm, savory garden.
9. Ladle the soup into bowls. Just before serving, drop a small spoonful of basil pesto right in the center of each bowl and sprinkle with grated Parmesan — stirring the pesto in at the table, not while cooking, keeps its bright green color and fresh basil taste.

### 5. Pizza Margherita
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 00 flour (or bread flour) | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Water (lukewarm) | 95ml | 190ml | 285ml | 380ml |
| Instant yeast | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Salt | 4g | 8g | 12g | 16g |
| Olive oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| San Marzano tomatoes (canned) | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Fresh mozzarella | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Fresh basil leaves | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 |

**Steps:**
1. In a large bowl, stir the instant yeast and salt into the flour. In a separate cup, stir the lukewarm water (it should feel like warm bathwater, not hot) with a drizzle of the olive oil.
2. Pour the water into the flour and mix with a spoon or your hands until a shaggy, rough dough forms and there's no dry flour left at the bottom of the bowl.
3. Turn the dough out onto a clean, lightly floured counter. Knead it — push it away from you with the heel of your hand, fold it back over itself, and turn it a quarter turn, over and over — for about 8–10 minutes, until the dough feels smooth and springs back slowly when you poke it.
4. Shape the dough into a ball, place it in a lightly oiled bowl, and cover with plastic wrap or a damp towel. Let it rise somewhere warm for 1.5–2 hours, until it has doubled in size — press a finger into it gently, and if the dent slowly fills back in, it's ready.
5. Ask an adult to help you preheat the oven as hot as it will go (many home ovens reach 500°F/260°C) with a pizza stone or upside-down baking sheet inside, for at least 30 minutes, so it's really, really hot before baking.
6. Punch the risen dough down gently to release the air, then divide it into one ball per person. On a floured surface, stretch or roll each ball into a thin round, leaving the edge slightly thicker — this puffy edge becomes the classic pizza crust.
7. Crush the canned San Marzano tomatoes by hand in a bowl (careful, they can squirt) until chunky, and season with a pinch of salt. Spread a thin layer over each dough round, leaving a bare border around the edge.
8. Tear the fresh mozzarella into small pieces with your hands and scatter it over the sauce — tearing rather than slicing lets it melt into little pools instead of one flat sheet.
9. Ask an adult to help slide the pizza onto the hot stone or baking sheet in the oven. Bake 8–12 minutes, watching closely near the end, until the crust puffs up with dark brown spots (called "leopard spotting") and the cheese is bubbling and melted.
10. Ask an adult to help remove the pizza using a pizza peel or spatula — it will be extremely hot. Tear the fresh basil leaves over the top and drizzle with a little olive oil right before slicing and serving.

### 6. Osso Buco alla Milanese
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Veal shanks (osso buco cut, ~300g each) | 1 piece | 2 pieces | 3 pieces | 4 pieces |
| All-purpose flour (for dredging) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Onion | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Carrot | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Celery | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic (cloves, divided) | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Crushed tomatoes (canned) | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Dry white wine | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Beef or chicken stock | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Butter | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Bay leaf | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Dried thyme | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Lemon zest | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Fresh parsley (for gremolata) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Steps:**
1. Pat the veal shanks completely dry with paper towels — this matters because wet meat won't brown, it'll just steam. Season both sides with salt and pepper, then dredge each shank in the flour, shaking off the extra.
2. Ask an adult to help heat the butter in a large, heavy pot (a Dutch oven works great) over medium-high heat until it's shimmering.
3. Carefully lay the shanks in the pot — ask an adult to help, since hot fat can splatter. Sear 3–4 minutes per side without moving them, until each side is deeply golden brown. This browning (called the Maillard reaction) builds most of the dish's flavor, so don't skip it or rush it.
4. Remove the shanks to a plate. In the same pot, add the chopped onion, carrot, and celery, and cook 5–6 minutes, scraping up the browned bits stuck to the bottom (that's built-in flavor) until the vegetables soften.
5. Add half the garlic and cook 30 seconds more, just until fragrant. Pour in the white wine and let it bubble 2–3 minutes until the sharp alcohol smell cooks away.
6. Stir in the crushed tomatoes and stock, then nestle the seared shanks back into the pot, standing them upright if they fit (this keeps the marrow from falling out of the bone). Add the bay leaf and thyme.
7. Bring it up to a gentle simmer, then turn the heat down low and cover with a lid, leaving a small gap for steam to escape. Braise 1.5–2 hours, checking every 30 minutes to make sure it's just barely bubbling, not boiling hard — a hard boil makes the meat tough and stringy instead of tender.
8. The dish is ready when the meat is so tender it's nearly falling off the bone and a fork slides in with almost no resistance. If it's still firm, put the lid back on and give it more time — low and slow is the whole secret here.
9. While the meat finishes, make the gremolata: finely chop the parsley and mix it in a small bowl with the lemon zest and the remaining raw garlic, minced very fine. This bright, fresh topping cuts through the rich, slow-cooked sauce.
10. Carefully lift the shanks out onto plates (the bone will be hot, and the marrow inside is a delicious treat to scoop out with a small spoon). Spoon the sauce and vegetables over the top.
11. Sprinkle each plate with a pinch of the fresh gremolata right before serving. This dish is traditionally served over risotto alla Milanese or creamy polenta.

### 7. Saltimbocca alla Romana
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Veal or chicken cutlets (thin) | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Prosciutto (thin slices) | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Fresh sage leaves | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| All-purpose flour (for dredging) | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Butter | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Olive oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Dry white wine (or Marsala) | 40ml | 80ml | 120ml | 160ml |

**Steps:**
1. Place each cutlet between two sheets of plastic wrap or in a zip-top bag. Ask an adult to help you pound them with a meat mallet or the bottom of a heavy pan until they're thin and even, about the thickness of a pencil — this tenderizes the meat and helps it cook fast and evenly.
2. Lay a sage leaf on top of each cutlet, then a slice of prosciutto over the sage, pressing gently so it sticks. If the prosciutto slice is bigger than the cutlet, fold the edges under.
3. Use a toothpick to pin the prosciutto and sage in place on each cutlet — this stops everything from curling up or falling off while it cooks. (Remember to tell everyone at the table there are toothpicks in the meat before eating!)
4. Lightly dust the prosciutto side of each cutlet with flour, tapping off the extra.
5. Ask an adult to help heat the butter and olive oil together in a large pan over medium-high heat until the butter foams.
6. Place the cutlets in the pan prosciutto-side down first. Cook 2 minutes, until the prosciutto turns lightly crisp and golden on the edges.
7. Flip the cutlets and cook the other side 1–2 minutes more, just until the meat turns from pink to white or light brown all the way through (cut into the thickest one to check, since these cook fast).
8. Move the cutlets to a plate. Pour the wine into the same hot pan (careful, it may sizzle and steam up) and scrape up the browned bits from the bottom with a spatula.
9. Let the wine bubble 1–2 minutes until it reduces slightly and thickens a little, then turn off the heat and swirl in any remaining butter for a silky, glossy sauce.
10. Remove the toothpicks from the cutlets, spoon the sauce over the top, and serve right away while hot.

### 8. Tiramisu (Bonus)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Egg yolks | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Sugar | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Mascarpone cheese | 65g | 130g | 195g | 260g |
| Strong brewed espresso (cooled) | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Ladyfingers (savoiardi) | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 |
| Unsweetened cocoa powder (for dusting) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Dark rum or Marsala (optional) | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |

**Steps:**
1. Separate the egg yolks from the whites (ask an adult to help if you haven't done this before — crack the egg and pass the yolk gently between the two shell halves, letting the white drip into a bowl below). You'll only need the yolks for this recipe. Because this dessert uses raw egg yolks, use the freshest eggs you can find, and swap in pasteurized eggs if you're serving young children, pregnant women, or anyone with a weakened immune system.
2. In a bowl, whisk the egg yolks and sugar together for 2–3 minutes, until the mixture turns pale yellow and thick enough that it falls off the whisk in a slow ribbon rather than dripping like water.
3. Add the mascarpone to the yolk mixture and gently fold it in with a spatula — stir in wide, scooping motions rather than fast beating, so you don't knock all the air out — until completely smooth with no lumps.
4. Pour the cooled espresso into a shallow dish (add the rum or Marsala here if using). Make sure the coffee is fully cool — dipping the ladyfingers in hot coffee would make them fall apart.
5. Working one at a time, dip each ladyfinger into the coffee for only about 1 second per side — just long enough to soak the surface. Any longer and they'll turn to mush instead of staying soft-but-firm.
6. Arrange a single layer of dipped ladyfingers in the bottom of a dish, breaking pieces to fill any gaps.
7. Spread half of the mascarpone mixture evenly over the ladyfingers with a spatula or the back of a spoon.
8. Repeat with a second layer of dipped ladyfingers, then spread the rest of the mascarpone mixture on top, smoothing the surface flat.
9. Cover the dish and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, though overnight is even better — this resting time lets the layers firm up and the flavors soak together, so don't skip it or the tiramisu will be too soft to slice.
10. Right before serving, dust the top generously with cocoa powder through a small sieve for an even, snow-like layer. Slice into squares and serve chilled.
