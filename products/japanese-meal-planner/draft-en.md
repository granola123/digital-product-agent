## Grocery List (by category)

**Produce & Herbs:** green onion, napa cabbage, regular cabbage, yellow onion, carrot, potato, garlic, fresh ginger, shiitake mushrooms, enoki mushrooms, mitsuba or extra green onion (garnish)

**Protein:** chicken breast, chicken thigh, salmon fillet, ground pork, thinly sliced beef (ribeye or chuck, "sukiyaki-cut" if your grocer offers it), sliced pork belly or extra chicken thigh (for ramen), eggs, firm tofu, silken tofu

**Rice & Noodles:** short-grain Japanese rice, fresh or dried ramen noodles, shirataki (konjac) noodles or bean-thread noodles, gyoza/dumpling wrappers

**Sauces & Pantry:** soy sauce, mirin, sake, sugar, rice vinegar, miso paste, dashi granules (or kombu + bonito flakes), tonkatsu sauce, Japanese curry roux (blocks, e.g. Golden Curry or Vermont Curry), panko breadcrumbs, all-purpose flour, sesame oil, honey, baking powder, sweet red bean paste (anko), toasted nori sheets, dried wakame seaweed, menma (seasoned bamboo shoots, optional), chili oil (optional)

## Recipe Cards

All ingredient tables below are scaled for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 servings.

### 1. Chicken Katsu
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken breast (thin cutlet) | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Salt & pepper | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Flour | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Egg | 1 | 2 | 2 | 3 |
| Panko breadcrumbs | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Shredded cabbage (to serve) | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Steamed rice | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Tonkatsu sauce | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |

**Steps:**
1. Lay the chicken breast flat on a cutting board. If it's thick on one side, ask an adult to help slice it horizontally into a thinner, even piece, or gently pound it with the flat side of a heavy pan until it's about as thick as your thumb — this helps it cook through evenly without drying out.
2. Sprinkle both sides with a pinch of salt and pepper.
3. Set up three shallow bowls in a row: one with flour, one with the egg beaten until smooth, and one with the panko breadcrumbs.
4. Dip the chicken in the flour first, patting off the extra so it's just a thin dusty coat. Then dip it in the egg, letting the extra drip off. Then press it into the panko, turning and patting so breadcrumbs stick to every part, including the edges.
5. Ask an adult to help pour oil into a frying pan until it's about 1cm deep, then heat it over medium heat. The oil is ready when a single breadcrumb dropped in sizzles right away and floats.
6. Carefully lay the cutlet into the oil away from you (so it doesn't splash toward your hands). Fry 3–4 minutes, until the bottom is a deep golden brown — lift an edge with tongs to check the color before flipping.
7. Flip once and fry the other side another 3–4 minutes, until it's also deep golden brown and the panko looks crisp and crackly.
8. Ask an adult to help lift the cutlet out onto a wire rack or paper towels to drain. Let it rest 2 minutes — this keeps the coating crunchy instead of soggy, and lets the juices settle back into the meat.
9. Slice the cutlet into strips about as wide as two fingers. Slicing lets you check the middle is fully white with no pink, and makes it easy to fan out on the plate.
10. Pile the shredded cabbage on a plate, fan the sliced katsu next to it, and serve with steamed rice and tonkatsu sauce drizzled over the top or on the side for dipping.

### 2. Teriyaki Salmon + Miso Soup
**Teriyaki Salmon**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Salmon fillet | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Soy sauce | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Mirin | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Sake (or water) | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Sugar | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Steamed rice | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |

**Miso Soup**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Water | 300ml | 600ml | 900ml | 1200ml |
| Dashi granules | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Miso paste | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Silken tofu (cubed) | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Dried wakame | 2g | 4g | 6g | 8g |
| Green onion (sliced) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Steps:**
1. Start the miso soup base first since it needs to simmer gently in the background: ask an adult to help bring the water to a boil in a small pot, then stir in the dashi granules until dissolved. Turn the heat down to low so it's just barely bubbling.
2. Drop the dried wakame into the pot — it will unfold and grow bigger as it soaks up the warm broth, so a small pinch goes a long way. Add the cubed tofu too. Let it sit on low heat while you start the salmon.
3. In a small bowl, stir together the soy sauce, mirin, sake, and sugar until the sugar disappears — this is your teriyaki sauce.
4. Ask an adult to help heat a splash of oil in a frying pan over medium heat. Pat the salmon dry with a paper towel (this helps it brown instead of steam) and lay it skin-side down in the pan.
5. Cook without moving it for 4 minutes, until the skin looks crisp and golden brown at the edges. Flip carefully with a spatula and cook the other side 2–3 minutes.
6. Pour the teriyaki sauce into the pan around the salmon. It will bubble and thicken quickly — tilt the pan and spoon the bubbling sauce over the top of the fish a few times so it gets glossy and coated all over.
7. The salmon is done when it flakes apart easily if you press a fork into the thickest part, and the inside has turned from translucent pink-orange to an opaque, solid pink. This takes about 1–2 more minutes once the sauce is glazing it.
8. Back to the soup: turn off the heat completely before the last step — boiling miso makes it lose its delicate flavor. Scoop a spoonful of the hot broth into a small cup, stir the miso paste into that cup until smooth (this stops lumps), then pour it back into the pot and stir gently.
9. Ladle the miso soup into bowls and sprinkle sliced green onion on top. Plate the glazed salmon over rice, spooning any extra sauce from the pan over the top.

### 3. Gyoza (Pan-Fried Pork Dumplings)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ground pork | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Cabbage (finely chopped) | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Garlic (cloves, minced) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ginger (grated) | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 tbsp | 4 tsp |
| Green onion (minced) | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Soy sauce | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Sesame oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Gyoza wrappers | 8 | 16 | 24 | 32 |
| Dipping: soy sauce | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Dipping: rice vinegar | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |

**Steps:**
1. Put the chopped cabbage in a bowl and sprinkle it with a small pinch of salt. Let it sit 5 minutes, then squeeze small handfuls over the sink to wring out the extra water — skipping this makes your filling watery and your dumplings soggy.
2. In a big bowl, mix the ground pork, squeezed cabbage, garlic, ginger, green onion, soy sauce, and sesame oil together with your hands or a spoon until it's evenly combined, like mixing meatloaf.
3. Set up a small bowl of water next to your wrappers. Lay a wrapper flat in your palm and spoon about a teaspoon of filling into the center — don't overfill, or it won't seal.
4. Dip a finger in the water and trace it around the edge of the wrapper. Fold it in half over the filling to make a half-moon shape, then pinch 3–4 small pleats along the curved edge, pressing firmly so it seals tightly with no gaps. Set it down with the flat bottom touching the plate so it can stand up.
5. Repeat with the rest of the filling and wrappers, keeping finished dumplings under a damp paper towel so they don't dry out while you work.
6. Ask an adult to help heat a splash of oil in a large non-stick pan over medium heat. Arrange the dumplings standing up in rows, flat-bottom down, so they all touch the pan.
7. Fry undisturbed for 2–3 minutes, until the bottoms turn a deep golden brown — you can lift one with a spatula to peek.
8. Carefully pour in about 60ml of water per batch of 8 dumplings and immediately cover the pan with a lid — it will splatter and steam loudly, so stand back and let an adult handle this part. This steams the tops of the dumplings until fully cooked.
9. Let it steam 3–4 minutes, until most of the water has disappeared and you can hear the sizzle return. Remove the lid and let any remaining water cook off for another minute so the bottoms crisp back up.
10. Stir together the dipping soy sauce and rice vinegar (add a few drops of chili oil if you like heat). Slide the dumplings onto a plate, golden-crisp side up, and serve with the dipping sauce.

### 4. Oyakodon (Chicken & Egg Rice Bowl)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thigh (bite-size) | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Onion (thinly sliced) | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Dashi stock (or water + granules) | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Soy sauce | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Mirin | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Sugar | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Egg | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Steamed rice | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Green onion (sliced, garnish) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Steps:**
1. In a small, shallow pan (one with a lid — a frying pan works if you have a lid that fits), stir together the dashi stock, soy sauce, mirin, and sugar. Ask an adult to help bring it to a gentle simmer over medium heat.
2. Add the sliced onion and let it cook 2–3 minutes, until the edges start turning soft and slightly see-through.
3. Add the chicken pieces in a single layer. Let them simmer in the broth for 4–5 minutes, until there's no pink left when you cut the biggest piece open.
4. While that cooks, crack the eggs into a bowl and beat them just a little — you want streaks of yolk and white still visible, not a totally smooth mix, so the finished dish has soft pockets of egg.
5. Once the chicken is cooked, pour about two-thirds of the beaten egg evenly over the top of the chicken and onion in a swirl. Don't stir it in — you want it to sit on top and set into place.
6. Cover the pan with the lid and let it cook undisturbed for 30 seconds, until the egg around the edges just starts to turn from clear yellow to opaque and slightly firm.
7. Pour the remaining egg over the top in the gaps, cover again, and cook another 30–45 seconds. You're aiming for the egg to look mostly set but still a little glossy and soft in the middle, like a just-set custard — not hard and rubbery.
8. Turn off the heat right away, even if it looks slightly underdone — it will keep gently cooking from the pan's heat for another minute.
9. Scoop a bowl of hot rice, and use a spatula to slide the whole chicken-and-egg mixture on top in one gentle motion, keeping it together like sliding a pancake off a pan. Sprinkle with sliced green onion.

### 5. Japanese Chicken Curry Rice
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thigh (bite-size) | 120g | 240g | 360g | 480g |
| Onion (sliced) | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Carrot (chunks) | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Potato (chunks) | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Water or chicken stock | 200ml | 400ml | 600ml | 800ml |
| Japanese curry roux | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Steamed rice | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help heat a splash of oil in a pot over medium heat. Add the sliced onion and cook, stirring occasionally, for 5–6 minutes, until it turns soft and golden — this slow start is what makes the curry taste rich instead of flat.
2. Add the chicken pieces and cook 3–4 minutes, stirring, until the outside is no longer pink (it doesn't need to be fully cooked yet — it'll finish in the broth).
3. Add the carrot and potato chunks and stir everything together for 1 minute so they get lightly coated in the pan's flavor.
4. Pour in the water or stock. Turn the heat up until it reaches a boil (small bubbles rising steadily), then turn it down to a gentle simmer.
5. Using a spoon, skim off and discard any grey foam that rises to the surface — this is just released protein and can make the sauce taste slightly bitter if left in.
6. Cover partway with a lid and simmer for 15 minutes, until a fork slides easily into the potato and carrot, like poking a soft-baked potato.
7. Turn off the heat completely — this matters, because boiling roux can make the sauce gluey or cause it to separate. Break the curry roux blocks into pieces and drop them in.
8. Stir gently but thoroughly until every piece of roux has melted into the liquid with no lumps floating around.
9. Turn the heat back on to low and simmer 5 more minutes, stirring often so nothing sticks to the bottom, until the sauce has thickened enough to coat the back of a spoon.
10. Serve the curry ladled over a bowl of steamed rice, keeping the curry and rice sitting side by side rather than fully mixed, the way it's traditionally plated.

### 6. Sukiyaki (Beef & Vegetable Hot Pot)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thinly sliced beef (ribeye/chuck) | 120g | 240g | 360g | 480g |
| Napa cabbage (chopped) | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Green onion (diagonal slices) | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Shiitake mushrooms (sliced) | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Enoki mushrooms | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Firm tofu (cubed) | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Shirataki noodles | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Soy sauce | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Mirin | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Sugar | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Sake | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Egg (for dipping, optional) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |

**Steps:**
1. First make the "warishita" sauce: stir together the soy sauce, mirin, sugar, sake, and about the same amount of water as the soy sauce in a small pot or bowl until the sugar dissolves.
2. Ask an adult to help boil a small pot of water. Drop the shirataki noodles in for 1–2 minutes to remove their natural odor, then drain and set aside.
3. Arrange the beef, cabbage, green onion, shiitake, enoki, tofu, and drained noodles on a big platter, grouped by type — this is called the "hot pot spread," and having everything ready before you start cooking matters because sukiyaki cooks fast, right at the table or stove.
4. Ask an adult to help heat a wide, heavy pan or hot pot over medium heat. Add a small piece of beef fat (if your beef came with any) or a little oil, and let it melt to lightly grease the pan.
5. Lay a few slices of beef in the pan in a single layer. Sear for about 30–45 seconds per side, just until the outside turns from red to light brown — it will finish cooking later in the broth, so don't worry about the inside yet.
6. Pour in about a third of the warishita sauce, letting it bubble around the beef. Push the seared beef to one side of the pan.
7. Add the vegetables, tofu, and noodles to the empty side, and pour in more of the sauce so everything is partly covered. Let it simmer gently — small steady bubbles, not a hard boil — for 5–7 minutes, until the cabbage turns soft and the mushrooms shrink slightly.
8. Check the beef is fully cooked by cutting the thickest piece open — no red or pink should remain inside.
9. If you're using the raw egg for dipping, crack it into a small individual bowl and beat it — this is a traditional way to eat sukiyaki, letting the hot food cool slightly and coating it in silky egg. If you'd rather skip raw egg, this dish is just as delicious without it.
10. Serve straight from the pot: everyone picks pieces of beef and vegetables with chopsticks, dips them in the beaten egg if using, and adds more raw ingredients from the platter to the pot as things get eaten, topping up sauce and water as needed.

### 7. Shoyu Ramen (Soy Sauce Ramen)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fresh ramen noodles | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Chicken or pork stock | 400ml | 800ml | 1200ml | 1600ml |
| Marinade: soy sauce | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Marinade: mirin | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Broth: soy sauce | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Broth: mirin | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Garlic (cloves, minced) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Egg | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Sliced pork belly or chicken thigh | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Green onion (sliced) | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Nori sheet | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Menma (optional) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |

**Steps:**
1. Start the soft-boiled eggs first since they need time to cool: ask an adult to help lower the eggs gently into a pot of already-boiling water with a spoon (this prevents cracking). Set a timer for exactly 6½–7 minutes for a jammy, soft-set yolk.
2. The moment the timer goes off, use the spoon to transfer the eggs straight into a bowl of ice water. This stops them from cooking further and makes them much easier to peel.
3. Once cool, peel the eggs carefully and place them in a small container with the marinade soy sauce and marinade mirin. Let them marinate at least 15 minutes (longer, even a few hours, deepens the flavor and color).
4. Ask an adult to help heat a pan over medium heat and sear the sliced pork or chicken for 2–3 minutes per side, until browned outside and no longer pink in the middle when you cut a piece open. Set aside.
5. In a pot, bring the chicken or pork stock to a gentle simmer. Stir in the broth soy sauce, broth mirin, and minced garlic. Taste carefully (it's hot!) — it should taste savory and rich, a little sweet, with a noticeable soy flavor. Adjust with a little more soy sauce if it tastes flat.
6. Bring a separate large pot of water to a rolling boil (big, fast bubbles all over the surface). Add the ramen noodles and cook according to the package time, usually 2–4 minutes for fresh noodles, stirring once so they don't clump.
7. The noodles are ready when they bend easily but still have a slight bite in the center when you taste one — not soft and mushy.
8. Drain the noodles quickly and shake off excess water, then swirl them into the bottom of each serving bowl using tongs or chopsticks, twisting into a neat nest.
9. Ladle the hot broth over the noodles, filling each bowl about three-quarters full.
10. Slice the marinated eggs in half lengthwise so the jammy yolk shows. Arrange the sliced pork or chicken, egg halves, green onion, a nori sheet propped against the bowl's edge, and menma on top of each bowl.
11. Serve immediately while the broth is hot and the noodles are still springy — ramen is best eaten fast, before the noodles soften too much in the broth.

### 8. Dorayaki (Red Bean Pancake Sandwiches) — Bonus
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Egg | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Sugar | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Honey | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Flour | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Baking powder | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Water or milk | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Sweet red bean paste (anko) | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |

**Steps:**
1. In a bowl, whisk the egg, sugar, and honey together for about a minute, until the mixture looks pale yellow and slightly thick — this whisking traps a little air, which is part of what makes dorayaki pancakes fluffy.
2. Sift the flour and baking powder into the bowl (sifting breaks up any clumps so your batter stays smooth) and gently fold it in with a spatula, using a scooping motion rather than stirring hard — overmixing makes the pancakes tough.
3. Add the water or milk a little at a time, folding until the batter is smooth and falls off the spatula in a slow, thick ribbon, like pancake batter. Let it rest 15 minutes so the baking powder can start working and the flour can relax.
4. Ask an adult to help heat a non-stick pan over low-medium heat. Test the heat by flicking a tiny drop of water onto it — it should sizzle gently, not violently.
5. Pour a small ladle of batter into the pan to form a circle about the size of your palm. Cook without touching it until bubbles appear all over the surface and the edges look dry and matte, about 2 minutes — this is your cue to flip.
6. Flip carefully with a spatula and cook the other side for about 1 minute, until it's golden brown and springs back gently when you poke it with a finger.
7. Stack the cooked pancakes under a clean kitchen towel as you go — this traps steam and keeps them soft instead of drying out.
8. Once all the pancakes are cooked and cooled to warm, spread a generous spoonful of sweet red bean paste on the flat (bottom) side of one pancake and sandwich it together with another pancake, flat sides facing in, like a sandwich.
