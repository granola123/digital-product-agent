## Grocery List (by category)

**Produce & Herbs:** napa cabbage kimchi (store-bought, well-fermented), scallions, garlic, ginger, yellow onion, zucchini, potato, green cabbage, sweet potato, carrot, spinach, bean sprouts, shiitake mushrooms (fresh or dried), Asian pear (or a Bosc pear/apple substitute), green chili pepper (Korean or jalapeño)

**Protein:** thin-sliced beef ribeye or sirloin (for bulgogi and japchae — ask the butcher counter for "shabu-shabu cut" if pre-sliced isn't available), ground or thin-strip beef (bibimbap), pork belly or canned Spam (kimchi jjigae), boneless chicken thighs (dakgalbi), chicken wings/drumettes (yangnyeom chicken), firm or soft tofu, eggs

**Rice & Noodles:** short-grain white rice, sweet potato glass noodles (dangmyeon), Korean rice cake (tteok, optional add-in for dakgalbi)

**Sauces & Pantry — Korean grocery run recommended for the starred items:** gochujang* (fermented chili paste), gochugaru* (Korean red chili flakes — not the same as regular chili powder), doenjang* (fermented soybean paste), soy sauce, toasted sesame oil, toasted sesame seeds, sugar, cornstarch or potato starch, all-purpose flour, glutinous (sweet) rice flour, instant yeast, ketchup, honey, black pepper, salt, neutral oil for frying, anchovy-kelp stock bags (optional shortcut for jjigae broths — plain water works fine too)

*Most large supermarkets now stock a small Korean/Asian aisle with jarred gochujang and doenjang; if yours doesn't, one trip to a Korean or general Asian grocery store (or an online order) covers all three starred pantry items for the whole week.

## Recipe Cards

All ingredient tables below are scaled for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 servings.

### 1. Bulgogi (Marinated Grilled Beef)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beef ribeye or sirloin, thinly sliced | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Soy sauce | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Sugar | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Asian pear, grated | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Garlic, minced | 1 clove | 2 cloves | 3 cloves | 4 cloves |
| Ginger, minced | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Sesame oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Onion, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Scallion, cut into 2-inch pieces | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Black pepper | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Toasted sesame seeds | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. If your beef isn't already thinly sliced, ask an adult to help: putting the beef in the freezer for 20–30 minutes first firms it up so it's much easier to slice paper-thin. You want pieces about as thick as a coin.
2. Peel and grate the Asian pear into a big bowl (ask an adult to help with the grater — the fine holes can scrape fingers). The pear does two jobs: it adds a little sweetness and its natural enzymes gently tenderize the beef.
3. Add the soy sauce, sugar, minced garlic, minced ginger, sesame oil, and black pepper to the bowl with the grated pear. Stir until the sugar disappears — this is your marinade.
4. Add the sliced beef to the marinade and use your hands (or tongs, if you don't want sticky fingers) to make sure every piece is coated.
5. Cover the bowl and put it in the fridge for at least 1 hour — overnight is even better. The longer it sits, the more flavorful and tender the beef gets.
6. Ask an adult to help heat a large pan or grill pan over medium-high heat with a little oil, until the oil looks shiny and moves easily when you tilt the pan.
7. Add the sliced onion and cook for 1 minute, just until it starts to soften and turn slightly see-through.
8. Add the marinated beef in a single layer, spreading pieces out so they aren't stacked — if the pan is crowded, cook it in two batches so the beef sears instead of steaming.
9. Let it cook 2–3 minutes without moving it much, then flip and cook another 1–2 minutes, until every piece has turned from red/pink to a deep brown color with no pink left inside.
10. Add the scallion pieces in the last 30 seconds and stir once — they should stay bright green and just barely wilted.
11. Pile onto plates or into bowls over warm rice, and sprinkle toasted sesame seeds on top before serving.

### 2. Kimchi Jjigae (Kimchi Stew)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Napa cabbage kimchi, chopped | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Kimchi brine/juice | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Pork belly, sliced (or Spam, cubed) | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Tofu, cubed | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Onion, sliced | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Scallion, chopped | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Gochugaru | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Gochujang | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Garlic, minced | 1 clove | 2 cloves | 3 cloves | 4 cloves |
| Water or anchovy stock | 200ml | 400ml | 600ml | 800ml |
| Sesame oil | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help you put a pot on the stove over medium heat and add the sesame oil.
2. Add the pork belly (or Spam) and cook for 2–3 minutes, stirring now and then, until the edges start turning golden brown — this builds a savory base flavor for the whole stew.
3. Add the chopped kimchi and cook another 2 minutes, stirring, until the kimchi looks slightly softer and smells fragrant instead of sharp and raw.
4. Stir in the gochugaru and gochujang, coating the kimchi and pork so nothing sticks to the bottom of the pot.
5. Pour in the water (or stock) and the kimchi brine. Turn the heat up to medium-high until you see steady bubbles rising from the bottom — that's a boil.
6. Once boiling, turn the heat back down so it's gently bubbling (a simmer), put a lid on with a small gap, and let it cook for 10 minutes so the flavors blend together and the pork finishes cooking through.
7. Add the sliced onion and minced garlic, and simmer 3 more minutes until the onion turns soft and translucent.
8. Gently slide in the tofu cubes — add them last and stir carefully, since tofu breaks apart easily if you stir too hard.
9. Simmer 2 more minutes just to warm the tofu through, then carefully taste a spoonful (it's hot!). If it needs more saltiness, add a small splash more kimchi brine.
10. Turn off the heat, sprinkle the chopped scallion on top, and ladle the stew into bowls straight from the pot — kimchi jjigae is traditionally served bubbling hot, alongside a bowl of warm rice.

### 3. Japchae (Glass Noodle Stir-Fry)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sweet potato glass noodles (dangmyeon) | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Beef sirloin, thin strips | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Spinach | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Carrot, julienned | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Onion, sliced | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Shiitake mushrooms, sliced | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Soy sauce | 20ml | 40ml | 60ml | 80ml |
| Sugar | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Sesame oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Garlic, minced | 1 clove | 2 cloves | 3 cloves | 4 cloves |
| Toasted sesame seeds | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Bring a large pot of water to a boil (ask an adult to help). Add the glass noodles and cook according to the package time, usually 6–8 minutes, until they turn from stiff and opaque to soft, stretchy, and translucent.
2. Drain the noodles in a colander and rinse briefly with cold water so they stop cooking and don't clump together. Using kitchen scissors, snip through the noodles a few times in the colander — they're very long and this makes them much easier to eat.
3. In a small bowl, mix the soy sauce and sugar together until the sugar dissolves — this seasons the noodles directly.
4. Toss the drained noodles with about two-thirds of this soy-sugar mixture and 1 teaspoon of the sesame oil, so they don't stick together while you cook everything else. Set aside.
5. Ask an adult to help heat a splash of oil in a large pan over medium-high heat. Add the beef strips and cook 2–3 minutes, until no pink remains, then scoop onto a plate.
6. In the same pan, add a little more oil if needed and cook the onion, carrot, and shiitake mushrooms together for 3–4 minutes, stirring often, until the carrot has softened slightly but still has a little crunch.
7. Add the spinach to the pan and stir just until it wilts down and turns bright green, about 1 minute — it shrinks a lot, so don't worry if the pan looks very full at first.
8. Add the cooked beef back into the pan along with the seasoned noodles and the rest of the soy-sugar mixture. Using tongs, toss everything together like tossing a big salad for 1–2 minutes, until the noodles turn a light golden-brown color and everything is evenly mixed.
9. Turn off the heat and drizzle in the remaining sesame oil, tossing once more — this adds a fresh, nutty aroma that fades if added too early in the cooking.
10. Taste a strand of noodle — it should be a little sweet and savory, not plain. Adjust with a touch more soy sauce if needed.
11. Sprinkle with toasted sesame seeds and serve warm or at room temperature — japchae is one of the few Korean dishes that's just as good after it cools a bit.

### 4. Doenjang Jjigae (Soybean Paste Stew)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Doenjang | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Zucchini, diced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Potato, diced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Onion, diced | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Tofu, cubed | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Garlic, minced | ½ clove | 1 clove | 1½ cloves | 2 cloves |
| Green chili, sliced | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Scallion, chopped | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Water or anchovy-kelp stock | 200ml | 400ml | 600ml | 800ml |
| Gochugaru (optional) | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help bring the water (or stock) to a boil in a small pot over medium-high heat — you'll know it's boiling when bubbles rise steadily from the bottom, not just a few here and there.
2. Add the doenjang to the pot. Instead of just dropping the spoonful in, use the back of the spoon to press and stir it against the side of a small strainer (or just stir well directly in the pot) until it fully dissolves into the water with no lumps — lumps of paste taste too strong in one bite.
3. Add the diced potato first, since it takes the longest to cook. Let it simmer for 5 minutes.
4. Add the diced onion and zucchini, and simmer another 5 minutes, until you can easily poke a fork through the potato.
5. Stir in the minced garlic and the gochugaru if using — the gochugaru is optional and just adds a little extra color and warmth, not real heat at this small amount.
6. Add the sliced green chili and let everything simmer 2 more minutes.
7. Gently add the tofu cubes last, stirring softly so they don't break apart, and simmer 2 more minutes just to heat them through.
8. Carefully taste a spoonful (it's hot!) — doenjang jjigae should taste savory and a little earthy, like a hearty vegetable soup with deep umami flavor. If it tastes flat, stir in a little more doenjang, a small spoonful at a time.
9. Turn off the heat, sprinkle the chopped scallion on top, and serve bubbling hot with a bowl of warm rice — this stew is meant to be shared straight from a small pot at the center of the table.

### 5. Dakgalbi (Spicy Stir-Fried Chicken)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thigh, bite-size pieces | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Gochujang | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Gochugaru | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Soy sauce | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Sugar | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Garlic, minced | 1 clove | 2 cloves | 3 cloves | 4 cloves |
| Ginger, minced | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Green cabbage, chopped | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Sweet potato, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Onion, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Rice cake (tteok), optional | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Scallion, cut into pieces | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Sesame oil | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. In a large bowl, mix the gochujang, gochugaru, soy sauce, sugar, minced garlic, and minced ginger together into a thick red paste — this is the dakgalbi sauce.
2. Add the chicken pieces to the bowl and mix well with a spoon (or your hands) so every piece is coated in red sauce. Cover and let it marinate at least 15 minutes in the fridge — 30 minutes to an hour is even better if you have time.
3. If using rice cake and it's the hard, refrigerated kind, soak it in warm water for 10 minutes first so it softens and cooks evenly instead of staying tough in the middle.
4. Ask an adult to help heat the sesame oil in a large, wide pan over medium heat. Add the sliced sweet potato first, since it's the hardest vegetable and needs the most time — cook 3–4 minutes, stirring occasionally.
5. Add the marinated chicken and spread it out in the pan. Let it cook without stirring too much for 3 minutes so the sauce starts to caramelize slightly on the bottom, then flip the pieces.
6. Add the onion and cabbage on top of the chicken and continue cooking, stirring every minute or so, for about 8–10 minutes total, until the chicken is completely white inside with no pink (cut the thickest piece open to check) and the vegetables have softened.
7. If using rice cake, add it in during the last 4 minutes of cooking, stirring it in among the chicken so it heats through and turns soft and chewy rather than stiff.
8. Keep an eye on the pan's moisture — dakgalbi should look glossy and saucy, not dry. If it starts sticking or looking dry before the chicken is done, add a small splash of water and stir.
9. Add the scallion pieces in the last minute of cooking and stir once, just until they turn bright green and slightly wilted.
10. Serve straight from the pan, family-style, with rice on the side — many Korean restaurants even fry rice right in the leftover sauce afterward, so save any extra sauce in the pan if you want to try that too.

### 6. Yangnyeom Chicken (Korean Fried Chicken)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken wings/drumettes | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Cornstarch or potato starch | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| All-purpose flour | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Neutral oil, for frying | as needed | as needed | as needed | as needed |
| Gochujang | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Ketchup | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Honey or corn syrup | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Soy sauce | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Garlic, minced | 1 clove | 2 cloves | 3 cloves | 4 cloves |
| Toasted sesame seeds | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Pat the chicken wings completely dry with paper towels — this step matters more than it sounds, because wet chicken makes the coating gummy instead of crispy and can cause dangerous oil splatter.
2. In a bowl, toss the chicken with a pinch of salt and pepper, then coat every piece evenly in the cornstarch mixed with the flour, shaking off any extra loose powder.
3. This is a hot-oil step, so ask an adult to handle the frying with you nearby to help, not alone. Fill a deep, heavy pot with about 2 inches of oil and heat it over medium heat until a wooden chopstick dipped in the oil releases a steady stream of small bubbles — that means the oil is ready, usually around 340°F/170°C.
4. Carefully lower a few pieces of chicken into the oil at a time using tongs, laying them away from you so hot oil doesn't splash back — never drop chicken in from above or overcrowd the pot, since both cause splattering and uneven cooking.
5. Fry for 7–8 minutes, turning occasionally, until the coating turns light golden and the chicken floats and feels firm. Lift a piece out and cut into the thickest one to check — it should be completely white with clear juices, no pink.
6. Scoop the chicken out onto a wire rack or paper towels to drain, and let the oil come back up to temperature for 2–3 minutes.
7. For extra crispiness (this is the Korean fried chicken secret), fry all the pieces a second time for 2–3 minutes, until deep golden brown and very crunchy when you tap a piece with a spoon. Drain again.
8. While the chicken drains, ask an adult to help make the sauce: in a small pan over low heat, stir together the gochujang, ketchup, honey, soy sauce, and minced garlic for 1–2 minutes, just until it turns glossy and starts to bubble gently at the edges. Don't walk away — this sauce can burn quickly on higher heat.
9. Turn off the heat, add the crispy fried chicken straight into the pan, and toss quickly with a spatula or tongs until every piece is coated in shiny red sauce.
10. Transfer to a plate right away and sprinkle with toasted sesame seeds — yangnyeom chicken is best eaten hot and crispy within a few minutes of saucing, before the coating has time to soften.

### 7. Bibimbap (Mixed Rice Bowl)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cooked short-grain rice | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Beef, ground or thin strips | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Spinach | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Carrot, julienned | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Zucchini, julienned | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Bean sprouts | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Shiitake mushrooms, sliced | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Egg | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Gochujang | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Sesame oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Garlic, minced | 1 clove | 2 cloves | 3 cloves | 4 cloves |
| Soy sauce | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |

**Steps:**
1. Bring a small pot of water to a boil (ask an adult to help). Add the bean sprouts and cook for 2–3 minutes, until they turn slightly translucent and lose their raw crunch but still have a little bite. Scoop them out into a bowl, leaving the water boiling.
2. Using the same water, boil the spinach for just 30 seconds, until it wilts and turns bright green. Drain and rinse briefly with cold water so it stops cooking, then squeeze out the extra water with your hands.
3. Toss the drained bean sprouts and spinach each with a small pinch of salt, a little minced garlic, and a few drops of sesame oil in separate small bowls — keeping the vegetables separate (not mixed together) is what gives bibimbap its signature look of colorful piles side by side.
4. Ask an adult to help heat a little oil in a pan over medium heat. Cook the carrot for 1–2 minutes, just until slightly softened but still a bit crisp, then scoop into its own small bowl. Repeat the same way for the zucchini and then the shiitake mushrooms, cooking each vegetable separately for 1–2 minutes so their flavors and colors stay distinct.
5. In the same pan, cook the beef with a splash of soy sauce and a pinch of garlic for 3–4 minutes, breaking it apart with a spatula if using ground beef, until completely browned with no pink or red remaining.
6. Wipe the pan clean and fry the egg(s) sunny-side up over medium-low heat — cook until the white is fully set but the yolk is still soft and runny in the middle, about 3 minutes. The runny yolk becomes part of the "sauce" when you mix the bowl later.
7. Scoop warm rice into the bottom of each bowl as the base.
8. Arrange small piles of each topping around the edge of the rice in sections, like a clock face: beef, spinach, bean sprouts, carrot, zucchini, and mushrooms, keeping every pile separate and colorful.
9. Place the fried egg right in the center on top of the rice, and add a spoonful of gochujang next to it.
10. Just before eating, drizzle a little extra sesame oil over the top, then mix everything together vigorously with a spoon until the rice turns an even reddish color and every bite has a bit of everything — this final mixing step is where the dish gets its name ("bibim" means "mixed").

### 8. Hotteok (Sweet Filled Pancakes, Bonus)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All-purpose flour | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Glutinous (sweet) rice flour | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Sugar (for dough) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Instant yeast | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Warm milk or water | 45ml | 90ml | 135ml | 180ml |
| Salt | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Brown sugar (filling) | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Cinnamon | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Chopped peanuts or walnuts | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Oil, for pan-frying | as needed | as needed | as needed | as needed |

**Steps:**
1. In a large bowl, stir the warm milk (it should feel like a warm bath — not hot enough to sting your finger) with the sugar and yeast. Let it sit for 5 minutes until it looks a little foamy on top — that foam means the yeast is alive and working.
2. Add the flour, glutinous rice flour, and salt to the bowl. Mix with a spoon and then your hands until it forms a soft, slightly sticky dough — it should be much softer and wetter than bread dough, more like a thick, stretchy batter you can still shape with oiled hands.
3. Cover the bowl with a damp towel or plastic wrap and let it rise somewhere warm for 45–60 minutes to 1 hour, until it looks puffy and roughly doubled in size.
4. While the dough rises, mix the brown sugar, cinnamon, and chopped nuts together in a small bowl — this is the filling that turns into a gooey syrup once the pancake is cooked.
5. Rub a little oil on your hands (this keeps the sticky dough from sticking to you). Take a portion of the dough about the size of a golf ball and flatten it into a disc in your palm.
6. Place a spoonful of the brown sugar filling in the center, then gather the edges of the dough up and around the filling like a little sealed pouch, pinching the top closed so no filling can leak out while cooking.
7. Ask an adult to help heat a splash of oil in a flat pan over medium heat. Place the dough pouch seam-side down and let it sit undisturbed for 1 minute to seal.
8. Using a spatula, press down firmly on the pouch to flatten it into a thick disc about ½ inch tall — press slowly, since the hot filling can bubble out the sides if you press too fast or too hard.
9. Cook 2–3 minutes on this side, until the bottom turns deep golden brown, then flip carefully and press again lightly. Cook another 2–3 minutes on the second side, until both sides are golden brown and you can hear a faint sizzle from the melted sugar inside.
10. Lift the hotteok out with a spatula and let it sit on a plate for 1–2 minutes before eating — the sugar filling turns molten hot during cooking, hotter than the dough around it, so a short wait keeps you from burning your tongue.
11. Serve warm, ideally cut in half so you can see the syrupy filling — hotteok is a classic Korean street food, especially popular as a warm treat in colder months.
