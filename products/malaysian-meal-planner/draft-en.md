### 1. Nasi Lemak (Coconut Rice with Sambal, Fried Anchovies & Egg)
**Coconut Rice — Ingredients by Serving**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jasmine rice | 75g | 150g | 225g | 300g |
| Coconut milk | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Water | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Pandan leaf, knotted | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ginger, sliced | 2g | 4g | 6g | 8g |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Sambal & Accompaniments — Ingredients by Serving**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dried red chilies, soaked | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 |
| Shallots | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Garlic clove | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Belacan (shrimp paste) | 3g | 6g | 9g | 12g |
| Onion, sliced | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Tamarind juice | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Sugar | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Cooking oil | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Dried anchovies (ikan bilis), whole, for frying | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Roasted peanuts | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Egg, hard-boiled | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Cucumber, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |

**Steps:**
1. Rinse the rice, then combine with coconut milk, water, pandan leaf, ginger, and salt in a rice cooker or pot. Cook until tender and fragrant.
2. Soak the dried chilies in hot water 15 minutes until softened, then drain.
3. Blend the soaked chilies, shallots, garlic, and belacan into a fine paste.
4. Heat the oil in a pan or wok and fry the paste over medium heat until fragrant and the oil starts to separate, about 8–10 minutes.
5. Add the sliced onion and cook 2 minutes, then stir in the tamarind juice and sugar. Simmer 8–10 minutes until thick, glossy, and deep red. Season with salt.
6. Meanwhile, fry the whole dried anchovies in a little oil over medium heat until crisp and golden, 2–3 minutes. Drain on paper towel.
7. Hard-boil the eggs (about 10–11 minutes), cool in cold water, peel, and halve.
8. Toast the peanuts in a dry pan until golden and fragrant.
9. Plate the coconut rice with a generous spoonful of sambal, the fried anchovies, roasted peanuts, egg halves, and cucumber slices arranged around it.

### 2. Malaysian Chicken Curry
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bone-in, cut into pieces | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Malaysian meat curry powder | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Water (to make curry paste) | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Potatoes, chunked | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Onion, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic clove, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ginger, minced | 3g | 6g | 9g | 12g |
| Lemongrass, bruised | ½ stalk | 1 stalk | 1½ stalks | 2 stalks |
| Fresh curry leaves | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 |
| Cinnamon stick | ⅛ | ¼ | ⅜ | ½ |
| Star anise | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Coconut milk | 80ml | 160ml | 240ml | 320ml |
| Chicken stock or water | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Cooking oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |

**Steps:**
1. Mix the curry powder with the water to form a smooth paste.
2. Heat the oil in a pot over medium heat and fry the cinnamon, star anise, and curry leaves until fragrant, about 30 seconds.
3. Add the onion, garlic, ginger, and lemongrass, and cook 3–4 minutes until softened.
4. Add the curry paste and fry 3–4 minutes, stirring constantly, until fragrant and the oil begins to separate.
5. Add the chicken pieces and stir to coat well in the paste, cooking 3–4 minutes.
6. Add the potatoes and chicken stock, bring to a boil, then reduce heat and simmer, covered, 20 minutes.
7. Stir in the coconut milk and simmer, uncovered, 10 more minutes until the chicken is cooked through and the sauce has thickened slightly.
8. Season with salt and a pinch of sugar to taste. Serve hot with steamed rice.

### 3. Char Kway Teow (Stir-Fried Flat Rice Noodles)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fresh flat rice noodles (kway teow) | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Shrimp, peeled | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Chinese sausage (lap cheong), sliced | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Egg | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Bean sprouts | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic chives, cut into 2-inch lengths | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Garlic, minced | 1 clove | 2 cloves | 3 cloves | 4 cloves |
| Dark soy sauce | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Light soy sauce | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Kicap manis (sweet soy sauce) | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Chili paste (sambal, optional) | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Cooking oil | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |

**Steps:**
1. Have all ingredients prepped and within arm's reach — this dish cooks in minutes over very high heat.
2. Heat a wok until it just starts to smoke, then add the oil.
3. Add the garlic and stir-fry 10 seconds, then add the sausage and fry 1 minute.
4. Add the shrimp and stir-fry until just turning pink, about 1 minute.
5. Push everything to one side, crack the egg into the empty space, and scramble briefly before mixing it through.
6. Add the noodles, dark soy sauce, light soy sauce, and kicap manis. Toss vigorously to coat evenly and pick up a little char, 2–3 minutes.
7. Add the bean sprouts and garlic chives, tossing just until wilted, about 30–60 seconds.
8. Stir in the chili paste if using, taste and adjust seasoning, and serve immediately while hot.

### 4. Sambal Udang (Prawn Sambal)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prawns, peeled and deveined, tail-on | 120g | 240g | 360g | 480g |
| Dried red chilies, soaked | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 |
| Shallots | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Garlic clove | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Belacan (shrimp paste) | 3g | 6g | 9g | 12g |
| Onion, sliced | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Tamarind juice | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Sugar | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Kaffir lime leaves, torn | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Cooking oil | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |

**Steps:**
1. Blend the soaked chilies, shallots, garlic, and belacan into a fine paste (a mortar and pestle gives the most authentic texture, but a food processor works fine).
2. Heat the oil in a wok over medium heat and fry the paste until fragrant and darkened, with the oil visibly separating around the edges, about 8 minutes.
3. Add the sliced onion and cook 2 minutes until softened.
4. Stir in the tamarind juice, sugar, and kaffir lime leaves, and simmer 3–4 minutes until thick and glossy.
5. Add the prawns and toss to coat evenly in the sambal. Cook 3–4 minutes until just pink and cooked through — don't overcook or they'll turn rubbery.
6. Season with salt to taste and serve hot with steamed rice.

### 5. Curry Laksa
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, boneless, sliced | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Prawns, peeled | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Laksa curry paste (store-bought, or blended dried chili, shallot, garlic, galangal, candlenut & belacan) | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Lemongrass, bruised | ½ stalk | 1 stalk | 1½ stalks | 2 stalks |
| Coconut milk | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Chicken stock | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Tofu puffs | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Fish cake, sliced | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Fresh yellow egg noodles (or rice vermicelli) | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Bean sprouts | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Cooking oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Sambal belacan, for garnish | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Heat the oil in a pot and fry the curry paste with the lemongrass over medium heat until fragrant and the oil starts to separate, about 5 minutes.
2. Add the chicken stock, bring to a boil, then reduce heat and simmer 10 minutes.
3. Add the sliced chicken and simmer until just cooked through, about 8–10 minutes.
4. Stir in the coconut milk, tofu puffs, and fish cake, and simmer 5 minutes. Season with salt and a little sugar to taste.
5. Add the prawns and cook 2–3 minutes until pink and just cooked through.
6. Meanwhile, blanch the noodles and bean sprouts separately in boiling water, then drain and divide between bowls.
7. Ladle the hot curry broth with chicken and prawns over the noodles.
8. Top with a dollop of sambal belacan and serve hot.

### 6. Hainanese Chicken Rice
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thigh or leg quarters, bone-in | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Ginger, sliced | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Scallions | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Garlic, minced (for rice) | 1 clove | 2 cloves | 3 cloves | 4 cloves |
| Jasmine rice | 75g | 150g | 225g | 300g |
| Reserved poaching stock (for cooking rice) | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Sesame oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Cucumber, sliced, for garnish | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Dark soy sauce, for drizzling | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Red chili, for dipping sauce | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Lime, for dipping sauce | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |

**Steps:**
1. Rub the chicken pieces all over with salt. Bring a pot of water to a boil with the ginger slices and scallions.
2. Add the chicken, reduce to a gentle simmer, and poach 20–25 minutes until just cooked through (juices run clear, no pink near the bone).
3. Remove the chicken and plunge it into ice water for 5 minutes to tighten the skin, then drain. Reserve the poaching stock.
4. Rinse the rice. Sauté the minced garlic in a little oil (or skimmed chicken fat from the stock) until fragrant, then add the rice and toast 1–2 minutes.
5. Cook the rice in the reserved poaching stock (rice cooker or covered pot) until tender and fragrant.
6. Make the dipping sauce: blend the chili, garlic, ginger, and lime juice with a splash of the poaching stock and a pinch each of sugar and salt.
7. Slice the chicken and brush the skin with sesame oil for shine.
8. Serve the chicken over or alongside the rice with cucumber slices, a drizzle of dark soy sauce, the chili-ginger dipping sauce, and a small bowl of the hot poaching broth on the side.

### 7. Mee Goreng Mamak (Malaysian-Indian Fried Noodles)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fresh yellow egg noodles | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Firm tofu, cubed and pan-fried | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Potato, boiled and cubed | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Egg | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Bean sprouts | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Choy sum (or cabbage), chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic, minced | 1 clove | 2 cloves | 3 cloves | 4 cloves |
| Curry powder | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Chili paste or sambal | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Tomato ketchup | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Kicap manis (sweet soy sauce) | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Cooking oil | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Lime wedge, for serving | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |

**Steps:**
1. Heat the oil in a wok and sauté the garlic until fragrant, about 30 seconds.
2. Add the curry powder and fry 30 seconds until aromatic.
3. Push the aromatics to one side, crack in the egg, scramble briefly, then mix through.
4. Add the noodles, fried tofu, and potato cubes, tossing to combine.
5. Stir in the chili paste, ketchup, and kicap manis, tossing until the noodles are evenly coated and starting to caramelize at the edges, 2–3 minutes.
6. Add the bean sprouts and choy sum, tossing just until wilted, about 1 minute.
7. Serve hot with lime wedges on the side.

### 8. Roti Canai with Dal (Bonus)
**Roti Dough — Ingredients by Serving**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All-purpose flour | 70g | 140g | 210g | 280g |
| Water | 35ml | 70ml | 105ml | 140ml |
| Egg (optional, for a richer dough) | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Sweetened condensed milk (or sugar) | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Ghee or oil, for laminating and cooking | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |

**Dal — Ingredients by Serving**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Red lentils (masoor dal) | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Water or stock | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Onion, chopped | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Garlic clove | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Fresh curry leaves | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 |
| Mustard seeds | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Ground turmeric | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Cooking oil or ghee | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |

**Steps:**
1. Make the roti dough: combine the flour, salt, condensed milk, egg (if using), and water. Knead 8–10 minutes until smooth and elastic.
2. Rub the dough all over with a little oil, cover, and rest at least 2 hours at room temperature (or overnight in the fridge) — this long rest is what lets it stretch paper-thin later.
3. Divide the rested dough into balls, coat each generously in oil, and rest 30 minutes more.
4. Make the dal: heat the oil in a pot and temper the mustard seeds and curry leaves until the seeds start to pop, about 30 seconds.
5. Add the onion and garlic, cooking 3–4 minutes until softened, then stir in the turmeric.
6. Add the red lentils and water, bring to a boil, then reduce heat and simmer 15–20 minutes until the lentils are soft and the dal has thickened. Season with salt.
7. On a well-oiled surface, flatten and stretch each dough ball as thin as possible — ideally almost see-through — then fold it into a square or coil and flatten again to build flaky layers.
8. Cook each roti on a hot, well-oiled griddle or skillet 2–3 minutes per side until golden brown and crisp, pressing the edges gently to release any steam pockets.
9. Clap the hot roti between your hands (or crush gently with two spatulas) to flake the layers apart.
10. Serve hot with the dal for dipping.
   *(Tip: good-quality frozen roti canai can stand in for the dough step if you want to keep this bonus dish truly weeknight-easy — the technique takes a little practice.)*

Deliberately distinct from the Thai pack's palette and from Indonesia's (a parallel release in this series), even though the cuisines share chili-and-coconut DNA.
