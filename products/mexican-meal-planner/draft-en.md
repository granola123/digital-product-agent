## Recipe Cards

All ingredient tables below are scaled for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 servings.

### 1. Tacos al Pastor
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pork shoulder, thin-sliced | 120g | 240g | 360g | 480g |
| Achiote (annatto) paste | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Dried guajillo chiles | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Pineapple juice | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| White vinegar | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Garlic cloves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ground cumin | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Dried oregano | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Fresh pineapple, diced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| White onion, diced | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Corn tortillas | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 |
| Cilantro, chopped | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Lime wedges | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |

**Steps:**
1. Put the dried guajillo chiles in a heatproof bowl and cover with boiling water (ask an adult to help pour it). Let them soak 15 minutes until soft and floppy, like a wet dried apricot. Drain, pull off the stems, and shake out the seeds.
2. In a blender, combine the softened chiles, achiote paste, pineapple juice, vinegar, garlic, cumin, and oregano. Blend until completely smooth — this reddish-orange paste is your marinade, and it should look like a thick smoothie.
3. Put the pork slices in a bowl or zip-top bag and pour the marinade over them. Use your hands (or squish the bag) to make sure every piece is coated. Cover and refrigerate at least 30 minutes — a few hours or overnight makes it even tastier.
4. Ask an adult to help heat a large skillet over medium-high heat with a small splash of oil. Wait until the oil looks shiny and a drop of marinade sizzles right away when it hits the pan.
5. Lay the marinated pork in a single layer (cook in batches if it's crowded — crowding steams the meat instead of browning it). Cook 3–4 minutes without moving, until the underside is deeply browned with some dark, slightly crispy edges.
6. Flip and cook 2–3 minutes more, until there's no pink left when you cut into the thickest piece. Move the cooked pork to a cutting board and chop it into smaller bite-sized bits.
7. In the same hot pan, add the diced pineapple and let it sit untouched for 1–2 minutes until it picks up some golden-brown, caramelized spots — this is what gives al pastor its signature sweet-savory flavor.
8. Stir the chopped pork back in with the caramelized pineapple for 30 seconds to warm everything together.
9. Warm the corn tortillas: ask an adult to help you toast each one for about 20 seconds per side in a dry pan, until soft and slightly puffed with a few light brown spots.
10. Pile the pork and pineapple mixture onto the warm tortillas, and top with diced white onion, chopped cilantro, and a squeeze of lime.

### 2. Chicken Enchiladas Verdes
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken breast | 120g | 240g | 360g | 480g |
| Tomatillos | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Jalapeño | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Garlic cloves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| White onion | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Chicken stock | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Cilantro | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Corn tortillas | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Vegetable oil (for softening tortillas) | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Mexican crema or sour cream | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Shredded Oaxaca or Monterey Jack cheese | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Queso fresco, crumbled | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |

**Steps:**
1. Fill a pot with water, add the chicken breast, and ask an adult to help bring it to a gentle boil over medium-high heat. Turn the heat down so it's just barely bubbling and poach 12–15 minutes, until the chicken is completely white inside with no pink (cut the thickest piece to check).
2. Pull the chicken out and let it cool enough to touch, then shred it into thin strips using two forks — pull them in opposite directions like you're tearing paper.
3. While the chicken poaches, pull the papery husks off the tomatillos and rinse off their sticky coating under warm water.
4. Put the tomatillos, jalapeño, and garlic in a pot, cover with water, and ask an adult to help bring it to a boil. Boil 8–10 minutes until the tomatillos turn from bright green to a duller olive green and feel soft when poked with a fork.
5. Drain and transfer the tomatillos, jalapeño, and garlic to a blender along with the onion and cilantro. Blend until smooth — this is your salsa verde, and it should be pourable, like a thick soup.
6. Ask an adult to help pour the salsa into a saucepan over medium heat. Add the chicken stock and simmer 5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until it darkens slightly and smells less sharp and more mellow.
7. Preheat the oven to 190°C (375°F) — ask an adult for help with the oven.
8. Warm a little oil in a small pan and quickly dip each tortilla in for about 5 seconds per side, just until soft and pliable — this stops them from cracking when you roll them (skip this if your tortillas are already very fresh and bendy).
9. Spoon shredded chicken down the center of each tortilla, roll it up tightly, and place seam-side down in a baking dish.
10. Pour the salsa verde evenly over the rolled enchiladas and sprinkle the shredded cheese on top. Bake 15–20 minutes, until the cheese is melted and bubbling at the edges.
11. Drizzle with crema and sprinkle with crumbled queso fresco right before serving.

### 3. Pozole Rojo (Pork & Hominy Soup)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pork shoulder, cubed | 130g | 260g | 390g | 520g |
| White hominy (canned, drained) | 130g | 260g | 390g | 520g |
| Dried guajillo chiles | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Dried ancho chile | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Garlic cloves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| White onion | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Dried oregano | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Bay leaf | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Shredded cabbage (garnish) | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Sliced radish (garnish) | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Lime wedges | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |

**Steps:**
1. Put the pork in a large pot with a quarter of the onion, one garlic clove, and the bay leaf. Cover with water (about 4 cups per serving) and ask an adult to help bring it to a boil.
2. Skim off any grey foam that rises to the top with a spoon — this keeps the broth clear instead of cloudy. Turn the heat down to a gentle simmer, cover partway, and cook 45–60 minutes, until a fork slides into the pork easily.
3. While the pork simmers, tear the stems off the dried guajillo and ancho chiles and shake out the seeds. Toast them in a dry pan over medium heat for about 30 seconds per side, just until they smell fragrant and puff slightly — watch closely, since burnt chiles turn bitter.
4. Put the toasted chiles in a bowl, cover with boiling water (ask an adult to help), and soak 15 minutes until soft.
5. Blend the softened chiles with the remaining onion, remaining garlic, and oregano, using a little of the soaking water to help it blend smooth. Pour this through a strainer into a bowl, pressing with a spoon, to catch any tough bits of chile skin.
6. Once the pork is tender, lift it out onto a plate with tongs. Let it cool slightly, then shred or cut it into bite-sized chunks. Discard the bay leaf.
7. Pour the strained red chile sauce into the pork broth and stir well — the broth will turn a deep brick-red color.
8. Add the shredded pork and hominy back into the pot. Simmer uncovered 20 minutes, stirring occasionally, so the flavors blend and the broth thickens slightly.
9. Carefully taste a spoonful (it's hot!) and add salt as needed — pozole broth should taste rich and a little savory-sour from the chiles.
10. Ladle into big bowls and set out the shredded cabbage, sliced radish, and lime wedges so everyone can top their own bowl.

### 4. Chiles Rellenos
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Poblano peppers | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Oaxaca or Monterey Jack cheese | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Eggs, separated | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| All-purpose flour | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Crushed tomatoes | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| White onion, chopped | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Garlic clove | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Chicken stock | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Vegetable oil (for frying) | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help you char the poblano peppers: place them directly over a gas flame, under a broiler, or on a hot grill, turning with tongs every minute or so, until the skin is blackened and blistered all over — this usually takes 8–10 minutes total.
2. Immediately put the charred peppers in a bowl and cover tightly with plastic wrap (or put them in a paper bag and fold it shut). Let them steam for 10 minutes — this loosens the skin so it peels off easily.
3. Once cool enough to touch, rub the blackened skin off each pepper with your fingers or a paper towel under running water. Don't worry about getting every last black fleck.
4. Make a small slit down one side of each pepper with a knife (ask an adult to help) and carefully scoop out the seeds and white membrane inside, keeping the pepper as whole as possible.
5. Stuff each pepper with cheese through the slit, pressing gently to fill it without tearing the pepper. Set the stuffed peppers aside.
6. Make the sauce: ask an adult to help warm a splash of oil in a saucepan over medium heat. Add the onion and garlic and cook 2–3 minutes until soft and see-through. Add the crushed tomatoes and stock, and simmer 10 minutes until slightly thickened. Keep it warm on low heat.
7. Separate the eggs, putting whites in one large bowl and yolks in a small one. Using a whisk or electric mixer, beat the egg whites until they form soft, fluffy peaks that hold their shape when you lift the whisk out — this takes a few minutes of steady beating.
8. Gently fold the egg yolks into the whipped whites with a spatula, using a slow, scooping motion (not stirring hard) so you don't knock all the air back out. This fluffy mixture is your batter.
9. Roll each stuffed pepper lightly in flour so the batter has something to stick to, then dip and coat it completely in the fluffy egg batter.
10. Ask an adult to help heat the vegetable oil in a deep pan over medium heat until it shimmers (a drop of batter should sizzle right away). Carefully lower each battered pepper into the oil — hot oil can splatter and burn, so this step needs an adult.
11. Fry 3–4 minutes, turning gently, until the batter is golden brown and puffed on all sides. Lift out with a slotted spoon and drain on paper towels.
12. Spoon the warm tomato sauce onto plates and set a chile relleno on top of each pool of sauce to serve.

### 5. Tinga de Pollo (Smoky Chipotle Chicken Tostadas)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken breast | 120g | 240g | 360g | 480g |
| White onion | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic cloves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Roma tomatoes | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Chipotle peppers in adobo | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Adobo sauce (from the can) | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Chicken stock | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Tostadas (or corn tortillas) | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Avocado | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Queso fresco, crumbled | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Shredded lettuce | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |

**Steps:**
1. Fill a pot with water, add the chicken, and ask an adult to help bring it to a boil. Turn down to a gentle simmer and poach 12–15 minutes, until there's no pink inside when you cut into the thickest piece.
2. Lift out the chicken, let it cool a little, then shred it into thin strands with two forks.
3. In a blender, combine the tomatoes, chipotle peppers, and adobo sauce. Blend until mostly smooth — it's okay if it's a little chunky.
4. Ask an adult to help warm a splash of oil in a pan over medium heat. Add the onion and cook 3–4 minutes, stirring, until soft and turning translucent.
5. Add the garlic and cook 30 seconds more, just until you can smell it — don't let it brown.
6. Pour in the blended tomato-chipotle sauce and the chicken stock. Simmer 8–10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens slightly and deepens to a brick-orange color.
7. Add the shredded chicken and stir well so every strand is coated in sauce. Simmer 5 more minutes so the chicken soaks up the smoky flavor.
8. Taste carefully (it's hot!) and add a pinch of salt if needed — it should taste smoky, a little spicy, and savory.
9. Pile the tinga onto crisp tostadas and top with shredded lettuce, sliced avocado, and crumbled queso fresco.

### 6. Carne Asada
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flank or skirt steak | 130g | 260g | 390g | 520g |
| Lime juice | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Orange juice | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Garlic cloves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Olive oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Ground cumin | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Cilantro, chopped | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| White onion (for grilling) | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Corn tortillas | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Pico de gallo (side) | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |

**Steps:**
1. In a bowl, whisk together the lime juice, orange juice, garlic, olive oil, cumin, and half the cilantro to make a marinade.
2. Put the steak in a shallow dish or zip-top bag and pour the marinade over it, making sure it's coated on both sides. Cover and refrigerate at least 30 minutes (up to 4 hours — much longer than that and the citrus starts to "cook" the meat and turn it mushy).
3. Take the steak out of the fridge about 15 minutes before cooking so it isn't ice-cold — this helps it cook more evenly.
4. Ask an adult to help heat a grill or grill pan over high heat until it's very hot — you should hear a loud sizzle the instant the meat touches it.
5. Lay the steak on the hot grill and let it cook undisturbed for 3–4 minutes, until deep brown grill marks form on the bottom.
6. Flip once and cook 3–4 minutes on the other side for medium doneness — the center should feel springy, not squishy, when you press it, and read about 60°C (145°F) on a meat thermometer if you have one.
7. Move the steak to a cutting board and let it rest, untouched, for 5–10 minutes. This lets the juices settle back into the meat instead of running out when you cut it — skipping this makes the meat dry.
8. Slice the steak thinly against the grain (cutting across the visible muscle lines, not along them) — this is what makes each bite tender instead of chewy.
9. Serve the sliced steak with warm tortillas, grilled onion, chopped cilantro, and pico de gallo on the side.

### 7. Pollo en Mole Poblano
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs | 130g | 260g | 390g | 520g |
| Dried ancho chiles | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Dried mulato chiles | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Dried pasilla chile | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Roma tomato | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Garlic cloves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| White onion | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Raisins | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Almonds (or peanuts) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Sesame seeds | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Mexican chocolate (or dark chocolate + pinch cinnamon) | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Chicken stock | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help you tear the stems off the ancho, mulato, and pasilla chiles and shake out the seeds. Toast them in a dry pan over medium heat for about 20–30 seconds per side, until they smell rich and toasty and puff up slightly — pull them off the heat fast if they start to smoke, since burnt chile turns the whole sauce bitter.
2. Put the toasted chiles in a bowl, cover with boiling water, and soak 15 minutes until completely soft.
3. In the same dry pan, toast the almonds and sesame seeds separately for 1–2 minutes each, shaking the pan often, until they turn golden and smell nutty. Set a small pinch of sesame seeds aside for garnish later.
4. Ask an adult to help warm a little oil in the pan and cook the onion and garlic for 3–4 minutes, until soft, then add the tomato and cook 3 more minutes until it breaks down and turns saucy.
5. In a blender, combine the soaked chiles, toasted almonds and sesame seeds, the onion-garlic-tomato mixture, and the raisins. Add about half the chicken stock and blend until completely smooth — this takes a minute or two of blending, and the sauce should be thick like a smoothie, not watery.
6. Ask an adult to help pour this thick paste into a pot over medium heat. Cook, stirring often (it will splutter and pop, so stand back a bit), for 5 minutes until it darkens and smells deeply toasty.
7. Pour in the rest of the chicken stock and stir until it becomes a smooth, pourable sauce, about the thickness of gravy.
8. Add the chicken thighs, spooning sauce over them to coat. Turn the heat down so the sauce is gently bubbling, cover partway, and simmer 25–30 minutes, until the chicken is completely cooked through with no pink near the bone (cut the thickest piece to check).
9. Break the chocolate into pieces and stir it into the sauce until it completely melts and disappears.
10. Simmer 5 more minutes so the chocolate blends fully into the sauce — it should taste rich, a little spicy, a little sweet, and a little bitter all at once, not like straight chocolate.
11. Carefully taste a spoonful (it's hot!) and add a pinch of salt if it needs it.
12. Spoon the chicken and mole sauce over rice, and sprinkle the reserved toasted sesame seeds on top before serving.

### 8. Churros with Mexican Chocolate Sauce (Bonus)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Water | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Butter | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Sugar (for dough) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Salt | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| All-purpose flour | 35g | 70g | 105g | 140g |
| Egg | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Vegetable oil (for frying) | 250ml | 500ml | 750ml | 1000ml |
| Cinnamon sugar (for coating) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Mexican chocolate tablet (or dark chocolate) | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Milk | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |

**Steps:**
1. In a small pot, combine the water, butter, sugar, and salt. Ask an adult to help bring it to a boil over medium heat, stirring until the butter completely melts.
2. As soon as it boils, take the pot off the heat and dump in all the flour at once. Stir hard and fast with a wooden spoon until it forms a smooth ball of dough that pulls cleanly away from the sides of the pot.
3. Let the dough cool for about 5 minutes, stirring occasionally — it should be warm, not hot, before the next step, or it will cook the egg too fast.
4. Beat in the egg (or eggs) a little at a time, mixing well after each addition. At first it will look like it's breaking apart, but keep stirring — it comes back together into a smooth, thick, glossy dough.
5. Spoon the dough into a piping bag fitted with a star-shaped tip (or a sturdy zip-top bag with one corner snipped off, though the ridges won't be as deep).
6. Ask an adult to help heat the vegetable oil in a deep, heavy pot to about 190°C (375°F) — a small pinch of dough dropped in should sizzle and float up right away. This step needs an adult, since hot oil can cause serious burns if it splashes.
7. Carefully pipe 10–13cm (4–5 inch) lengths of dough directly into the hot oil, using scissors or a knife to snip each one free. Only fry a few at a time so the pot isn't crowded.
8. Fry 2–3 minutes per side, turning with tongs, until deep golden brown and crisp all over. Lift out with a slotted spoon onto a plate lined with paper towels.
9. While still warm, roll each churro in the cinnamon sugar until fully coated.
10. For the chocolate sauce, warm the milk in a small pot until steaming (not boiling), then take it off the heat and stir in the chopped chocolate until completely melted and smooth.
11. Serve the warm churros with the chocolate sauce for dipping.
