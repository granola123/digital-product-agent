## Recipe Cards

All ingredient tables below are scaled for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 servings.

### 1. Chicken Tagine with Preserved Lemon & Olives
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bone-in | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Onion, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic cloves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Fresh ginger, grated | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Ras el hanout | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Ground turmeric | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Saffron threads | pinch | pinch | 2 pinches | 2 pinches |
| Cinnamon stick | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Olive oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Chicken stock or water | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Preserved lemon, quartered | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Green or violet olives | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Salt | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Cilantro & parsley, chopped | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help warm the olive oil in a wide heavy pot or tagine base over medium heat. Add the sliced onion and cook 4–5 minutes, stirring, until soft and see-through.
2. Stir in the garlic, ginger, ras el hanout, turmeric, saffron, and cinnamon stick. Cook 1 minute, stirring, just until you can smell the spices — don't let them burn or they'll turn bitter.
3. Nestle the chicken thighs into the onions and spices, turning each piece so it's coated all over.
4. Pour the stock or water around (not directly over) the chicken. Ask an adult to help bring it to a gentle simmer, then cover and cook low 40–45 minutes, until the chicken is completely cooked through with no pink near the bone.
5. Slice the preserved lemon rind into strips and add it to the pot along with the olives. Simmer uncovered 10 more minutes so the sauce thickens slightly and the flavors blend.
6. Carefully taste the sauce (it's hot!) — preserved lemon and olives are salty, so add extra salt only if it still needs it.
7. Sprinkle with the chopped cilantro and parsley.
8. Serve hot straight from the pot, spooning the sauce over the chicken, with warm bread or couscous alongside.

### 2. Harira (Moroccan Lentil & Chickpea Soup)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brown or green lentils, rinsed | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Chickpeas, soaked overnight (or canned, drained) | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Crushed tomatoes | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Onion, finely chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Celery, finely chopped | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Olive oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Fresh ginger, grated | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Ground black pepper | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Ground turmeric | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Ground cinnamon | pinch | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Saffron threads | pinch | pinch | 2 pinches | 2 pinches |
| All-purpose flour (for tedouira) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Water | 250ml | 500ml | 750ml | 1000ml |
| Salt | pinch | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Cilantro & parsley, chopped | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Vermicelli (optional) | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Lemon wedges (to serve) | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Dates (to serve) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help warm a splash of oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion and celery and cook 4–5 minutes until soft.
2. Stir in the ginger, black pepper, turmeric, cinnamon, and saffron. Cook 30 seconds, stirring, until fragrant.
3. Add the crushed tomatoes, lentils, drained chickpeas, and water. Ask an adult to help bring it to a boil.
4. Turn the heat down to a gentle simmer, cover partway, and cook 45–60 minutes, until the lentils and chickpeas are completely tender.
5. If using vermicelli, stir it in and simmer 5–8 minutes more, until tender.
6. Make the tedouira: in a small bowl, whisk the flour with a few tablespoons of cold water until completely smooth, with no lumps, like a thin pancake batter.
7. Slowly drizzle the tedouira into the simmering soup while stirring constantly, so it doesn't clump into balls. Simmer 5 more minutes, stirring, until the soup turns thicker and velvety — this silky texture is what gives harira its name (from the Arabic word for "silk").
8. Carefully taste a spoonful (it's hot!) and season with salt.
9. Stir in the chopped cilantro and parsley.
10. Ladle into bowls and serve with lemon wedges and dates on the side — the traditional way Moroccan families break the Ramadan fast.

### 3. Kefta Tagine (Spiced Meatball Tagine in Tomato Sauce)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ground beef or lamb | 120g | 240g | 360g | 480g |
| Onion, grated (for meatballs) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Garlic cloves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Parsley & cilantro, chopped | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Ground cumin | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Sweet paprika | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Ground cinnamon | pinch | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Olive oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Onion, finely chopped (for sauce) | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Ground ginger | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Crushed tomatoes | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Eggs | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Cilantro, chopped (garnish) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Steps:**
1. In a bowl, mix the ground meat with the grated onion, half the garlic, parsley, cilantro, cumin, paprika, and cinnamon until just combined — don't overwork it, or the meatballs turn tough and dense.
2. Roll the mixture into small meatballs, each about the size of a walnut.
3. Ask an adult to help warm the olive oil in a wide pan or tagine base over medium heat. Add the finely chopped onion and cook 4–5 minutes until soft.
4. Stir in the remaining garlic and the ginger, then add the crushed tomatoes and a splash of water. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook uncovered 10 minutes, until slightly thickened.
5. Carefully lower the meatballs into the simmering sauce in a single layer. Cover and cook 15–20 minutes, spooning sauce over them occasionally, until cooked through with no pink in the center.
6. Use the back of a spoon to make small wells in the sauce between the meatballs.
7. Crack an egg into each well. Cover and cook 7–10 minutes, until the egg whites are completely set but the yolks are still a little runny.
8. Sprinkle with chopped cilantro.
9. Bring the pot straight to the table and serve with warm crusty bread for scooping up the sauce.

### 4. Moroccan Fish Chermoula
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| White fish fillets (hake, cod, or sea bass) | 120g | 240g | 360g | 480g |
| Fresh cilantro, chopped | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Fresh parsley, chopped | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Garlic cloves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ground cumin | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Sweet paprika | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Lemon juice | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Olive oil | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Bell pepper, sliced (optional) | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Tomato, sliced (optional) | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Lemon wedges (to serve) | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |

**Steps:**
1. Make the chermoula: in a food processor (or by hand with a knife), blend the cilantro, parsley, garlic, cumin, paprika, lemon juice, and olive oil into a slightly coarse, spoonable paste — it shouldn't be perfectly smooth, more like a thick pesto.
2. Rub the fish fillets all over with about two-thirds of the chermoula. Cover and marinate in the fridge at least 30 minutes, up to 2 hours.
3. Ask an adult to help warm a little oil in a wide pan over medium heat. If using, add the sliced bell pepper and tomato and cook 3–4 minutes until just softened, then push them to the edges of the pan.
4. Lay the marinated fish fillets in the pan. Cook 3–4 minutes per side (less for thin fillets), until the fish flakes easily with a fork and is opaque all the way through.
5. Spoon the remaining chermoula over the fish during the last minute of cooking.
6. Carefully lift the fish and vegetables onto a plate with a spatula.
7. Serve warm with lemon wedges and crusty bread or rice.

### 5. Moroccan Chicken with Chickpeas & Apricots
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bone-in or boneless | 140g | 280g | 420g | 560g |
| Onion, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Olive oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Garlic cloves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Fresh ginger, grated | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Ground cinnamon | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Ground coriander | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Ground turmeric | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Saffron threads | pinch | pinch | 2 pinches | 2 pinches |
| Honey | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Chickpeas, cooked or canned (drained) | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Dried apricots | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Chicken stock or water | 80ml | 160ml | 240ml | 320ml |
| Salt | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Toasted almonds (garnish) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Cilantro, chopped | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help warm a little oil in a heavy pot over medium heat. Add the onion and cook 4–5 minutes until soft.
2. Stir in the garlic, ginger, cinnamon, coriander, turmeric, and saffron. Cook 1 minute, stirring, until fragrant.
3. Nestle the chicken into the spiced onions, turning each piece to coat it on all sides.
4. Add the stock or water and bring to a gentle simmer. Cover and cook low 25–30 minutes.
5. Stir in the honey, chickpeas, and dried apricots. Simmer uncovered 15–20 minutes more, until the chicken is fully cooked (no pink near the bone) and the sauce has thickened slightly.
6. Carefully taste the sauce and season with salt as needed.
7. Scatter the toasted almonds and chopped cilantro over the top.
8. Serve hot with couscous or warm bread to soak up the sauce.

### 6. Couscous with Seven Vegetables
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Medium couscous (semolina) | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Bone-in chicken or lamb pieces (for broth) | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Carrot, cut into chunks | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Turnip, cut into chunks | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Zucchini, cut into chunks | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Pumpkin or butternut squash, cut into chunks | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Cabbage, cut into wedges | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Tomato, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Chickpeas, soaked or canned (drained) | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Onion, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Olive oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Ground ginger | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Ground turmeric | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Saffron threads | pinch | pinch | 2 pinches | 2 pinches |
| Butter (or smen) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Water or stock | 200ml | 400ml | 600ml | 800ml |
| Cilantro & parsley (tied bouquet) | 2g | 4g | 6g | 8g |
| Raisins (optional garnish) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Steps:**
1. In the bottom of a couscoussière (or a large heavy pot), ask an adult to help warm a little oil and cook the chopped onion with the ginger, turmeric, and saffron for 2–3 minutes.
2. Add the tomato and cook 3 minutes more, until it breaks down and turns saucy.
3. Add the chicken or lamb pieces and enough water to cover everything. Ask an adult to help bring it to a boil, skimming off any foam that rises to the top.
4. Add the herb bouquet and chickpeas. Cover partway and simmer 30–40 minutes.
5. While the broth simmers, prepare the couscous: spread the dry grains in a wide, shallow dish, drizzle with a little water and oil, and rub the grains between your palms to separate every clump — it should feel like dry, loose sand when you're done.
6. Set the couscous in a steamer basket over the simmering broth (or a fine sieve set over the pot). Steam uncovered 15–20 minutes, letting the steam rise up through the grains.
7. Tip the steamed couscous back into the wide dish, drizzle with a little warm salted water, and rub it between your palms again to fluff and separate the grains. Let it rest a few minutes — for the fluffiest texture, repeat the steaming step once more if you have time.
8. Meanwhile, add the carrot and turnip (the vegetables that take longest to cook) to the broth and simmer 10 minutes.
9. Add the zucchini, pumpkin, and cabbage. Simmer 10–15 more minutes, until all the vegetables are fork-tender but not falling apart — add each vegetable so nothing turns to mush.
10. Mound the fluffed couscous on a large serving platter and dot with the butter, tossing gently so it melts through.
11. Arrange the cooked vegetables and chickpeas on top of and around the couscous mound, and scatter raisins over the top if using. Ladle a little broth over everything.
12. Serve the extra broth in a separate bowl so everyone can spoon more over their own plate.

### 7. Lamb Tagine with Apricots & Almonds
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lamb shoulder, cubed | 130g | 260g | 390g | 520g |
| Olive oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Onion, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic cloves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Fresh ginger, grated | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Ground cinnamon | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Ground cumin | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Saffron threads | pinch | pinch | 2 pinches | 2 pinches |
| Honey | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Dried apricots | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Blanched almonds | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Sesame seeds (garnish) | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Water or stock | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Cilantro & parsley, chopped | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Steps:**
1. Season the lamb cubes with the cinnamon, cumin, saffron, and a pinch of salt, tossing to coat evenly.
2. Ask an adult to help warm a little oil in a heavy pot over medium-high heat. Brown the lamb in batches, 3–4 minutes per side, until deeply colored all over; set aside on a plate. (Don't crowd the pot, or the lamb steams instead of browning.)
3. In the same pot, cook the onion 4–5 minutes until soft, then add the garlic and ginger and cook 1 minute more, until fragrant.
4. Return the lamb to the pot and add the water or stock. Ask an adult to help bring it to a gentle simmer, then cover and cook low 1½–2 hours, stirring occasionally, until the lamb is fork-tender — add a splash of water if the sauce reduces too fast.
5. Stir in the honey and dried apricots. Simmer uncovered 30–45 minutes more, until the apricots are plump and the sauce has thickened slightly.
6. Meanwhile, toast the almonds in a dry pan over medium heat 3–4 minutes, stirring often, until golden; set aside.
7. Carefully taste the sauce (it's hot!) and season with salt.
8. Spoon the lamb and apricots onto a serving platter, scatter the toasted almonds and sesame seeds on top, and sprinkle with the chopped cilantro and parsley.
9. Serve hot with couscous or crusty bread to soak up the sauce.

### 8. Moroccan Orange & Cinnamon Salad (Fenjaan) (Bonus)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Navel oranges, peeled & pith removed | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ground cinnamon | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Orange blossom water | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Honey | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Fresh mint leaves (garnish) | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Toasted almonds or chopped dates (optional garnish) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Steps:**
1. Using a small sharp knife, ask an adult to help peel the oranges all the way down to the flesh, cutting away every bit of the white pith so no bitterness remains.
2. Slice the oranges crosswise into thin rounds, catching any juice in a bowl, and arrange the slices on a serving plate, overlapping slightly.
3. In a small bowl, whisk together the collected orange juice, the orange blossom water, and the honey.
4. Drizzle the juice mixture evenly over the orange slices.
5. Sprinkle the cinnamon evenly over the top.
6. Scatter the mint leaves (and almonds or chopped dates, if using) over the salad.
7. Chill in the refrigerator at least 15 minutes before serving, so the flavors have time to meld.
