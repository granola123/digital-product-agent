## Recipe Cards

All ingredient tables below are scaled for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 servings.

### 1. Swedish Meatballs (Köttbullar)
Sweden's national dish: tender, spice-warmed beef-and-pork meatballs — bound with milk-soaked breadcrumbs for a pillowy texture — bathed in a silky cream gravy and served the classic way, with a spoonful of tart-sweet lingonberry jam cutting through the richness.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ground beef | 75g | 150g | 225g | 300g |
| Ground pork | 75g | 150g | 225g | 300g |
| Plain breadcrumbs | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Whole milk (for breadcrumbs) | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Egg, beaten | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Yellow onion, finely grated | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Ground allspice | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Ground nutmeg | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Butter, for frying | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Butter (for gravy) | 8g | 15g | 23g | 30g |
| All-purpose flour (for gravy) | 8g | 15g | 23g | 30g |
| Beef broth | 75ml | 150ml | 225ml | 300ml |
| Heavy cream | 50ml | 100ml | 150ml | 200ml |
| Soy sauce | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Lingonberry jam, to serve | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Mashed potatoes, to serve | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |

**Steps:**
1. Soak the breadcrumbs in the milk in a small bowl for 5 minutes, until softened into a paste.
2. In a large bowl, combine the ground beef, ground pork, soaked breadcrumbs, egg, grated onion, allspice, nutmeg, salt, and pepper. Mix gently with your hands just until combined — overmixing makes tough meatballs.
3. Roll into small, walnut-sized meatballs (about 1 inch/2.5cm), keeping them uniform so they cook evenly.
4. Ask an adult to help melt the butter in a large skillet over medium heat. Fry the meatballs in batches, turning often, 8–10 minutes, until deeply browned all over and cooked through with no pink in the middle. Transfer to a plate.
5. In the same skillet, melt the gravy butter and whisk in the flour, cooking 1 minute to remove the raw taste.
6. Gradually whisk in the beef broth and cream, scraping up the browned bits from the bottom of the pan. Simmer 3–5 minutes, stirring, until thickened enough to coat the back of a spoon. Stir in the soy sauce.
7. Return the meatballs to the gravy and simmer 2–3 minutes to warm through and coat.
8. Serve hot over mashed potatoes with a spoonful of lingonberry jam alongside.

### 2. Baked Norwegian Salmon with Dill Sauce
Simple, coastal Norwegian cooking at its best: a fillet of salmon roasted hot and fast until just flaking, topped with a cool, tangy dill sauce built on soured cream and a whisper of mustard — the herb that was born to pair with this fish.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Salmon fillet, skin-on | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Olive oil | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Lemon juice | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Sour cream or crème fraîche | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Fresh dill, chopped | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 tbsp | 4 tsp |
| Dijon mustard | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Lemon juice (for sauce) | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Garlic clove, minced (optional) | pinch | ¼ | ½ | 1 |
| Boiled potatoes, to serve | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help preheat the oven to 220°C (425°F).
2. Place the salmon fillet skin-side down in a lightly oiled baking dish. Drizzle with olive oil and lemon juice, then season with salt and pepper.
3. Bake 12–15 minutes, until the salmon flakes easily with a fork and is just opaque in the center.
4. While the salmon bakes, whisk together the sour cream, chopped dill, Dijon mustard, lemon juice, and garlic (if using) in a small bowl.
5. Let the salmon rest 2 minutes, then serve topped with a generous spoonful of the dill sauce, alongside boiled potatoes.

### 3. Swedish Yellow Pea Soup with Pork (Ärtsoppa)
Every Thursday across Sweden, this thick, golden split-pea soup — simmered slowly with a smoky cut of pork until it turns creamy — shows up on the table, a tradition that traces back to the Middle Ages. Serve with a smear of sharp mustard and crisp rye bread.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dried yellow split peas | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Water or light stock | 375ml | 750ml | 1125ml | 1500ml |
| Smoked pork hock, or thick-cut bacon/salt pork | 75g | 150g | 225g | 300g |
| Yellow onion, diced | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Fresh or dried thyme | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Dried marjoram | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Whole-grain mustard, to serve | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Rye crispbread, to serve | 1 slice | 2 slices | 3 slices | 4 slices |

**Steps:**
1. The night before, place the dried yellow split peas in a large bowl, cover with plenty of cold water, and soak at least 8 hours (or overnight).
2. Drain and rinse the peas, then add them to a large pot with the water or stock. Bring to a boil and skim off any foam that rises to the top.
3. Add the pork hock (or bacon) and diced onion. Reduce the heat to low, cover partially, and simmer 1 to 1½ hours, stirring occasionally, until the peas are completely soft and starting to break apart.
4. Ask an adult to help remove the pork from the pot, cut it into bite-sized cubes (discarding any skin or bone), and return it to the soup.
5. Stir in the thyme and marjoram, then season with salt and pepper. Simmer another 10 minutes, until thick and creamy.
6. Ladle into bowls and serve with a dollop of mustard and crispbread on the side.

### 4. Finnish Karelian Stew (Karjalanpaisti)
Finland's much-loved national dish, born in the Karelia region: cubes of beef and pork slow-braised for hours with nothing more than onion, carrot, whole allspice, and bay leaf until the meat falls apart — honest, deeply savory, and better the longer it sits.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beef chuck, cubed | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Pork shoulder, cubed | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Yellow onion, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Carrot, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Water or beef stock | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Whole allspice berries | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Bay leaf | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Boiled potatoes, to serve | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Lingonberry jam, to serve | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help preheat the oven to 150°C (300°F).
2. Layer the cubed beef and pork in a large Dutch oven or heavy oven-safe pot with the sliced onion and carrot.
3. Add the water or stock, whole allspice berries, bay leaf, salt, and pepper.
4. Cover tightly and place in the oven. Braise 2½ to 3 hours, until the meat is completely fall-apart tender.
5. Season to taste with more salt and pepper if needed.
6. Serve hot with boiled potatoes and a spoonful of lingonberry jam alongside.

### 5. Danish Meatballs (Frikadeller) with Boiled Potatoes & Gravy
Denmark's everyday national dish: oval, pan-fried pork-and-beef patties with a golden crust and a tender, almost fluffy inside, served the classic weeknight way — boiled potatoes, a simple pan gravy, and tangy pickled red cabbage.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ground pork | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Ground beef | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Yellow onion, finely grated | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Egg | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| All-purpose flour | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Whole milk | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Butter, for frying | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Butter (for gravy) | 8g | 15g | 23g | 30g |
| All-purpose flour (for gravy) | 8g | 15g | 23g | 30g |
| Beef or chicken stock | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Boiled potatoes, to serve | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Pickled red cabbage, to serve | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |

**Steps:**
1. In a large bowl, combine the ground pork, ground beef, grated onion, egg, flour, and milk. Season with salt and pepper.
2. Mix well with your hands until the mixture is smooth and slightly sticky, then let it rest in the fridge for 10 minutes to firm up.
3. Using two spoons or wet hands, shape the mixture into oval patties about 2 inches long and ½ inch thick.
4. Ask an adult to help melt the butter in a large skillet over medium heat. Fry the patties 4–5 minutes per side, until deeply golden brown and cooked through with no pink in the middle. Transfer to a plate.
5. In the same skillet, melt the gravy butter and whisk in the flour, cooking 1 minute.
6. Gradually whisk in the stock, scraping up the browned bits from the bottom of the pan. Simmer 3–4 minutes, until thickened.
7. Serve the frikadeller hot with the gravy, boiled potatoes, and pickled red cabbage.

### 6. Norwegian Fish Cakes (Fiskekaker)
Light, delicately spiced patties of minced white fish and potato, bound with egg and cream and fried until the outside turns golden — a coastal Norwegian classic with a hint of nutmeg in every bite, best served with more boiled potatoes and a tangy mustard-cream sauce.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cod or haddock fillet, skinless | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Potato, boiled and cooled, mashed | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Egg | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| All-purpose flour | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Whole milk or cream | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Yellow onion, grated | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Ground nutmeg | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| White pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Butter, for frying | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Boiled potatoes, to serve | 75g | 150g | 225g | 300g |
| Sour cream-mustard sauce, to serve | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help finely chop or pulse the fish in a food processor until roughly minced, not fully pureed — a little texture is traditional.
2. In a large bowl, mix the mashed potato with the minced fish, egg, flour, milk, grated onion, salt, nutmeg, and white pepper until well combined.
3. Cover and refrigerate the mixture 15 minutes to firm up.
4. Wet your hands and shape the mixture into small, flat patties about 2½ inches wide.
5. Ask an adult to help melt the butter in a skillet over medium heat. Fry the fish cakes 3–4 minutes per side, until golden brown and cooked through.
6. Serve hot with boiled potatoes and a dollop of sour cream-mustard sauce.

### 7. Swedish Cabbage Rolls (Kåldolmar)
Blanched cabbage leaves wrapped snugly around a warmly spiced beef-and-pork-and-rice filling, browned in butter, then simmered in broth until tender — a dish with roots in the Ottoman dolma that King Charles XII's court brought home to Sweden three centuries ago. Serve with lingonberry jam, the way it's always been.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Green cabbage, whole leaves | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Ground beef | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Ground pork | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Cooked white rice | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Yellow onion, finely chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Egg | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Whole milk | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Ground allspice | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Butter, for browning | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Beef broth | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Golden syrup (or light corn syrup) | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Lingonberry jam, to serve | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Boiled or mashed potatoes, to serve | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Core the cabbage and blanch the whole leaves 2–3 minutes, until pliable. Drain and let cool, then trim the thick center rib flat with a knife so the leaves roll easily.
2. In a bowl, mix the ground beef, ground pork, cooked rice, chopped onion, egg, milk, allspice, salt, and pepper until combined.
3. Place a spoonful of the meat mixture at the base of each cabbage leaf, fold in the sides, and roll up tightly into a neat parcel.
4. Ask an adult to help melt the butter in a large oven-safe pot or skillet over medium-high heat. Brown the cabbage rolls on all sides, 3–4 minutes total.
5. Pour in the beef broth and drizzle the syrup over the rolls. Cover and simmer gently 45–50 minutes, until the cabbage is tender and the filling is cooked through.
6. Serve hot with the pan juices spooned over, alongside boiled or mashed potatoes and a spoonful of lingonberry jam.

### 8. Swedish Cinnamon Buns (Bonus) — Kanelbullar
Soft, cardamom-scented knots of enriched dough, twisted around a buttery cinnamon-sugar filling and finished with a scatter of crunchy pearl sugar — no icing needed. The centerpiece of Swedish "fika," the daily coffee-and-a-treat ritual.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whole milk, lukewarm | 60ml | 125ml | 185ml | 250ml |
| Active dry yeast | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2¼ tsp |
| All-purpose flour | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Granulated sugar | 12g | 25g | 38g | 50g |
| Ground cardamom | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Butter, softened | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Egg, beaten (for dough) | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Butter, softened (filling) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Light brown sugar (filling) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Ground cinnamon (filling) | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Ground cardamom (filling) | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Egg, beaten (egg wash) | ⅛ | ¼ | ⅜ | ½ |
| Pearl sugar or sliced almonds | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Steps:**
1. In a small bowl, stir the lukewarm milk and yeast together and let sit 5 minutes, until foamy.
2. In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, sugar, cardamom, and salt. Add the yeast mixture, softened butter, and beaten egg. Mix and knead (by hand or with a dough hook) 8–10 minutes, until smooth and elastic.
3. Cover the bowl and let the dough rise in a warm spot 1 hour, until doubled in size.
4. Ask an adult to help roll the dough out on a floured surface into a large rectangle, about ¼ inch thick.
5. Spread the softened filling butter evenly over the dough, then sprinkle with the brown sugar, cinnamon, and cardamom.
6. Fold the dough into thirds like a letter, then cut into long, thin strips. Twist each strip and coil it into a knot to shape the classic kanelbullar knot.
7. Place the buns on a lined baking sheet, cover, and let rise 30–45 minutes, until puffy.
8. Ask an adult to help preheat the oven to 220°C (425°F). Brush the buns with the egg wash and sprinkle with pearl sugar or sliced almonds.
9. Bake 8–10 minutes, until golden brown. Cool slightly on a wire rack before serving warm.
