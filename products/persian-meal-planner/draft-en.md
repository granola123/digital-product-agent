A week that moves from smoky charcoal skewers to a slow-simmered herb stew, a glossy pomegranate braise, two jeweled rice dishes, a green herb frittata, a golden crackling rice cake, and a saffron pudding to finish — every night lands on the table gilded with saffron, ruby barberries, or a deep pomegranate glaze.

## Grocery List (by category)

**Produce & Herbs:** onions, garlic, fresh parsley, fresh cilantro, fresh dill, fresh chives (or scallions), lemons, cherry tomatoes, pomegranate (for fresh seeds as garnish)

**Protein:** chicken thighs (boneless and bone-in), lamb stew meat, eggs

**Grains, Legumes & Nuts:** basmati rice, dried kidney beans (or canned, drained), fresh or frozen fava beans (or lima beans), walnuts, dried limes (limoo amani), barberries (zereshk), pistachios, slivered almonds

**Dairy & Pantry Staples:** plain yogurt, butter, vegetable oil, olive oil, pomegranate molasses, tomato paste, saffron threads, dried fenugreek leaves

**Spices & Flavorings:** ground turmeric, ground cinnamon, ground cardamom, sugar, salt, black pepper, rosewater

*Note on specialty ingredients: dried limes (limoo amani), barberries (zereshk), pomegranate molasses, and dried fenugreek leaves are the four ingredients that make this cuisine taste unmistakably Persian — they're worth sourcing from a Middle Eastern grocer or online before starting the week, since substitutes flatten the flavor considerably.*

## Recipe Cards

All ingredient tables below are scaled for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 servings.

### 1. Joojeh Kabab (Saffron & Lemon Chicken Skewers)
Chicken thighs bathed in a saffron-gold, lemon-bright yogurt marinade, then grilled over open flame until the edges char and the meat turns tender and deeply golden — this is Iran's answer to backyard barbecue, and it perfumes the whole kitchen with saffron and citrus while it cooks.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, boneless, cubed | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Plain yogurt | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Lemon juice | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Onion, grated | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Olive oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Saffron threads, ground | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Warm water (to bloom saffron) | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Ground turmeric | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Cherry tomatoes | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Butter, melted (for basting) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| White or saffron rice, to serve | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |

**Steps:**
1. Crush the saffron threads to a powder in a small bowl (the back of a spoon works), then stir in the warm water and let it bloom for 10 minutes until it turns deep gold and smells honeyed and floral.
2. In a large bowl, whisk together the yogurt, lemon juice, grated onion, garlic, olive oil, half the bloomed saffron water, turmeric, salt, and black pepper into a glossy marinade.
3. Add the cubed chicken thighs and turn to coat every piece completely. Cover and marinate in the fridge at least 2 hours, up to 24 — the longer soak is what turns the meat tender and deeply golden all the way through.
4. Ask an adult to help thread the chicken onto skewers, alternating with the cherry tomatoes on separate skewers so the tomatoes don't fall apart before the chicken finishes.
5. Heat a grill, grill pan, or broiler to medium-high.
6. Grill the chicken skewers 10-12 minutes total, turning every few minutes, until deeply golden and lightly charred at the edges with no pink in the center. Grill the tomato skewers alongside for the last 4-5 minutes, just until blistered and soft.
7. Baste the chicken with the melted butter mixed with the remaining saffron water during the last minute of cooking for a glossy, saffron-gilded finish.
8. Serve the skewers hot over fluffy white or saffron rice with the charred tomatoes alongside.

### 2. Ghormeh Sabzi (Herb, Kidney Bean & Lamb Stew)
Iran's national dish: a mountain of fresh parsley, cilantro, and chives slow-fried until they collapse into a deep, glossy forest-green sauce, then simmered for hours with tender lamb, kidney beans, and tangy dried limes until every spoonful tastes savory, sour, and impossibly rich.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lamb stew meat, cubed | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Onion, diced | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Vegetable oil | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Fresh parsley, finely chopped | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Fresh cilantro, finely chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Fresh chives or scallion greens, chopped | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Dried fenugreek leaves | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Ground turmeric | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Dried limes (limoo amani), pierced | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Red kidney beans, cooked and drained | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Water or stock | 250ml | 500ml | 750ml | 1000ml |
| Salt | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Black pepper | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| White rice, to serve | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help warm half the vegetable oil in a heavy pot over medium heat. Add the onion and cook 5 minutes until soft, then add the garlic and lamb. Brown the lamb on all sides, about 6-8 minutes.
2. Meanwhile, in a separate wide pan, warm the rest of the oil over medium heat and add the chopped parsley, cilantro, and chives. Fry the herbs, stirring often, for 12-15 minutes until they darken to a deep, glossy forest green and smell intensely earthy and aromatic — this slow fry is what gives Ghormeh Sabzi its signature flavor, so don't rush it or skip it.
3. Stir the dried fenugreek leaves into the herbs for the last minute of frying — a little goes a long way, since fenugreek is intensely bitter-savory.
4. Add the fried herb mixture to the pot with the lamb, along with the turmeric, dried limes, kidney beans, water or stock, salt, and black pepper.
5. Bring to a gentle simmer, then cover and cook on low heat for 1.5-2 hours, stirring occasionally, until the lamb is fork-tender and the sauce has thickened into a deep, glossy green-brown.
6. Halfway through, carefully press one or two of the dried limes against the side of the pot with a spoon to release more of their tangy, sour flavor into the stew.
7. Taste and adjust the salt or sourness (squeeze a dried lime for more tang if needed) before serving.
8. Ladle the stew over fluffy white rice and serve hot.

### 3. Fesenjan (Pomegranate-Walnut Chicken Stew)
Ground walnuts simmered low and slow into a glossy pomegranate stew, deepening over an hour into a garnet-brown sauce that's tart, faintly sweet, and richer than anything else on the table — this is Persian comfort food at its most luxurious.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bone-in | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Walnuts, finely ground | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Onion, diced | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Vegetable oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Pomegranate molasses | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Water or chicken stock | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Sugar | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Ground cinnamon | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Ground turmeric | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Pomegranate seeds, for garnish | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| White rice, to serve | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help toast the ground walnuts in a dry pot over medium heat for 3-4 minutes, stirring constantly, until fragrant and just starting to release their oil — watch closely, since ground walnuts can burn fast.
2. Add the water or stock to the toasted walnuts, stir well, and simmer uncovered for 20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the mixture thickens into a loose, nutty paste.
3. Meanwhile, warm the vegetable oil in a separate pan over medium-high heat. Add the onion and cook 4-5 minutes until soft, then add the chicken thighs and brown on both sides, about 4 minutes per side.
4. Transfer the browned chicken and onion into the walnut paste. Stir in the pomegranate molasses, sugar, cinnamon, turmeric, and salt.
5. Cover and simmer on low heat for 45-60 minutes, stirring every 10 minutes or so to keep the walnuts from sticking, until the sauce turns a deep, glossy garnet-brown and a thin layer of walnut oil rises to the surface — that oil separating is the classic sign fesenjan is ready.
6. Taste and balance the sauce: add a touch more pomegranate molasses if it needs more tang, or a pinch more sugar if it's too sharp — fesenjan should taste rich, tart, and only lightly sweet.
7. Scatter fresh pomegranate seeds over the top just before serving for a burst of color and juicy crunch.
8. Serve hot over white rice, spooning plenty of the glossy sauce over each portion.

### 4. Zereshk Polo ba Morgh (Barberry Rice with Chicken)
Saffron-gilded basmati rice mounded high and scattered with ruby-red barberries that turn glossy and tart-sweet in butter — one of the most photogenic dishes in Persian cooking, and a genuine weeknight-doable one once the chicken braises while the rice steams.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs or drumsticks | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Vegetable oil (for browning chicken) | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Onion, sliced | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Ground turmeric | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Salt | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Black pepper | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Tomato paste | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Water | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Basmati rice | 75g | 150g | 225g | 300g |
| Barberries (zereshk), rinsed | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Butter | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Sugar | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Saffron threads, ground | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Warm water (to bloom saffron) | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help warm the vegetable oil in a pot over medium heat. Add the sliced onion and cook 5 minutes until soft, then add the chicken and turmeric, salt, and black pepper. Brown the chicken on all sides, about 6-8 minutes.
2. Stir in the tomato paste and water. Cover and simmer 30-35 minutes, until the chicken is fully cooked and tender and the sauce has reduced slightly.
3. While the chicken cooks, rinse the rice in a few changes of cold water until it runs clear, then soak in fresh water for 15-20 minutes.
4. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Drain the rice and boil it for 6-8 minutes, until the grains are tender on the outside but still slightly firm at the center. Drain well.
5. Bloom the saffron threads in the warm water for 10 minutes until deep gold.
6. Return the rice to the pot over low heat with a splash of water and half the butter in the bottom. Drizzle the saffron water over the top, cover with a clean kitchen towel under the lid, and steam for 25-30 minutes until fluffy and a light golden crust forms on the bottom.
7. Rinse the barberries well in cold water to remove any grit — they can carry sand. Melt the remaining butter in a small pan over low heat, add the barberries and sugar, and stir gently for just 1-2 minutes until glossy and plump. Watch closely; barberries scorch and turn bitter in seconds over higher heat.
8. Mound the saffron rice on a platter, scatter the sautéed barberries over the top, and serve the braised chicken alongside or nestled into the rice.

### 5. Kuku Sabzi (Herb & Walnut Frittata)
A dense green wall of parsley, cilantro, dill, and chives held together with just enough egg to bind it — crisp-edged and golden outside, tender and intensely herbal inside, studded with crunchy walnuts and jewel-bright barberries. Equally at home at breakfast, tucked into flatbread for lunch, or as a lighter weeknight dinner.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Eggs | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 |
| Fresh parsley, finely chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Fresh cilantro, finely chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Fresh dill, finely chopped | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Fresh chives or scallion greens, chopped | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Walnuts, chopped | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Barberries (zereshk), rinsed | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Dried fenugreek leaves | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| All-purpose flour | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Ground turmeric | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Baking powder | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Vegetable oil | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |

**Steps:**
1. In a large bowl, whisk the eggs until smooth.
2. Fold in the chopped parsley, cilantro, dill, and chives — the mixture should look more like herbs bound with a little egg than a normal omelette, so don't be shy with the greens.
3. Stir in the chopped walnuts, barberries, dried fenugreek, flour, turmeric, baking powder, salt, and black pepper until evenly combined.
4. Ask an adult to help warm the oil in a nonstick skillet over medium-low heat until shimmering.
5. Pour in the herb-egg mixture and spread it into an even layer. Cover the pan and cook 8-10 minutes, until the bottom is deep golden and the top is mostly set.
6. Carefully flip the kuku (use a large plate to help turn it, or run it under the broiler for 2-3 minutes instead if flipping feels tricky) and cook the second side 4-5 minutes, until golden and cooked all the way through.
7. Slide the kuku onto a cutting board and let it rest 5 minutes before slicing into wedges — this keeps it from crumbling.
8. Serve warm or at room temperature, with flatbread and a bowl of plain yogurt on the side.

### 6. Tahchin (Saffron Rice Cake with Chicken)
Rice bound in saffron-stained yogurt and egg, layered over tender chicken, and baked until the base turns a deep, crackling amber crust — then flipped in one dramatic motion so the golden, crisp side faces up. A showstopper that looks like it took all day but comes together with mostly hands-off baking time.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basmati rice | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Chicken thighs, boneless | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Plain yogurt | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Egg yolks | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Saffron threads, ground | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Warm water (to bloom saffron) | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Onion, diced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Ground turmeric | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Ground cinnamon | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Salt | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Butter or oil | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Barberries, rinsed, for garnish | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |

**Steps:**
1. Rinse the rice in a few changes of cold water until it runs clear. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil, add the rice, and boil 6-8 minutes until tender on the outside but still firm at the center. Drain and set aside.
2. Bloom the saffron threads in the warm water for 10 minutes until deep gold and fragrant.
3. Ask an adult to help warm a little of the butter or oil in a pan over medium heat. Add the onion and cook 5 minutes until soft, then add the chicken, turmeric, cinnamon, salt, and pepper. Cook 10-12 minutes, turning occasionally, until cooked through with no pink left. Shred or slice the chicken into bite-size pieces.
4. In a large bowl, whisk the yogurt, egg yolks, half the saffron water, and a pinch of salt until smooth.
5. Fold half of the cooked rice into the yogurt mixture until every grain is coated a rich golden yellow.
6. Grease a heavy, oven-safe pot or baking dish generously with the remaining butter or oil. Spread the saffron-yogurt rice evenly across the bottom, pressing it down gently.
7. Layer the cooked chicken over the rice, then top with the remaining plain rice, pressing down firmly and mounding it slightly in the center.
8. Drizzle the remaining saffron water over the top. Cover tightly with a lid or foil.
9. Bake at 180°C (350°F) for 45-60 minutes, until the edges look deep golden and you can smell a rich, toasty aroma — this is the crust forming on the bottom.
10. Let the tahchin rest 10 minutes, then run a knife around the edge and invert it onto a large plate in one confident motion so the crisp golden crust ends up on top.
11. Scatter with rinsed barberries and serve whole, sliced into wedges at the table.

### 7. Baghali Polo ba Morgh (Dill & Fava Bean Rice with Chicken)
Fluffy basmati rice flecked green with fresh dill and studded with tender fava beans, served alongside braised chicken in a light tomato sauce — bright, herby, and one of the easiest ways into Persian cooking, since nothing here needs more than a simmer and a steam.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Vegetable oil (for browning chicken) | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Onion, sliced | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Ground turmeric | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Salt | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Black pepper | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Tomato paste | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Water | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Basmati rice | 75g | 150g | 225g | 300g |
| Fava or lima beans, shelled (fresh or frozen) | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Fresh dill, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Butter | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Saffron threads, ground | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Warm water (to bloom saffron) | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help warm the vegetable oil in a pot over medium heat. Add the sliced onion and cook 5 minutes until soft, then add the chicken, turmeric, salt, and pepper. Brown on all sides, about 6-8 minutes.
2. Stir in the tomato paste and water. Cover and simmer 30 minutes, until the chicken is cooked through and tender.
3. Meanwhile, rinse the rice until the water runs clear, then soak 15-20 minutes. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil, drain the rice, and boil 6-7 minutes until just tender at the edges but firm in the center. Add the fava beans for the last 2 minutes of boiling, then drain everything together.
4. Bloom the saffron threads in the warm water for 10 minutes.
5. Melt the butter in a heavy pot over low heat. Layer in a third of the rice-and-fava mixture, sprinkle with a third of the chopped dill, and repeat twice more, ending with a dill layer on top. Drizzle the saffron water over everything.
6. Cover the pot with a lid wrapped in a clean kitchen towel (to trap steam without dripping) and steam over low heat for 35-40 minutes, until the rice is tender and a golden crust (tahdig) has formed on the bottom.
7. Gently fluff and mound the rice onto a platter, keeping the crispy bottom pieces to serve alongside or on top.
8. Serve with the braised chicken and its sauce spooned over or alongside.

### 8. Sholeh Zard (Bonus — Saffron Rice Pudding)
Rice cooked down for the better part of an hour until it dissolves into a glossy, saffron-gold porridge, finished with rosewater and cardamom and swept with cinnamon and slivered almonds — the fragrance alone (floral rosewater, warm saffron) is why this dessert closes out celebrations across Iran.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basmati rice | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Water | 300ml | 600ml | 900ml | 1200ml |
| Sugar | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Saffron threads, ground | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Warm water (to bloom saffron) | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Rosewater | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Ground cardamom | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Ground cinnamon, for garnish | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Butter | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Slivered almonds, for garnish | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Pistachios, chopped, for garnish | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Steps:**
1. Rinse the rice well, then combine it with the water in a heavy pot. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a low simmer.
2. Cook uncovered, stirring every so often, for 45-60 minutes, until the rice grains have completely broken down into a smooth, thick, porridge-like base — there should be no separate, chewy grains left.
3. Meanwhile, bloom the saffron threads in the warm water for 10 minutes until deep gold and fragrant.
4. Stir the sugar, bloomed saffron water, cardamom, and butter into the rice porridge. Continue simmering gently for 15-20 minutes, stirring often, until the pudding turns a rich, glossy saffron-gold and thickens further.
5. Take the pot off the heat and stir in the rosewater — adding it at the end keeps its floral scent bright instead of cooking it away.
6. Spoon the warm pudding into bowls (it's traditionally served slightly warm or at room temperature, though chilled is lovely too).
7. Garnish each bowl with a light dusting of ground cinnamon, slivered almonds, and chopped pistachios just before serving.
