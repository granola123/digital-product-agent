# Taste of Peru — 7-Day Meal Plan & Recipe Card Bundle

One of a growing set of cuisine packs in the International Food Recipe Collection launch bundle. Digital download bundle (PDF + Notion). English content for the Etsy/global market.

**A note on ingredients:** Peruvian cooking leans on a handful of chile pastes that do most of the flavor work — **ají amarillo paste** (fruity, medium-hot yellow chile — the backbone of half this week) and **ají panca paste** (smoky, mild dried red chile, used in the Pollo a la Brasa marinade). Both are increasingly stocked in the Latin/international aisle of larger supermarkets, or easy to order online (jarred, not fresh, is completely normal to use). If you truly can't find ají panca, a mix of smoked paprika and a pinch of cayenne gets you close enough for a weeknight. Seco de Res traditionally uses **chicha de jora** (fermented corn beer) — we've built the grocery list and recipe around a dark beer substitute, which is what most Peruvian home cooks outside Peru use anyway.

## Recipe Cards

All ingredient tables below are scaled for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 servings.

### 1. Lomo Saltado (Stir-Fried Beef, Tomato &amp; Fries)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beef sirloin, sliced into thin strips | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Red onion, sliced into wedges | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Tomato, cut into wedges | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Garlic clove, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ají amarillo paste | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 Tbsp | 4 tsp |
| Soy sauce | 1 Tbsp | 2 Tbsp | 3 Tbsp | 4 Tbsp |
| Red wine vinegar | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 Tbsp | 4 tsp |
| French fries (frozen, or fresh-cut) | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Vegetable oil | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Cilantro, chopped (garnish) | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 Tbsp | 4 tsp |
| Salt and black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |
| Cooked white rice, to serve | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |

**Steps:**
1. Season the beef strips with salt and pepper. If using frozen fries, cook according to package directions until crisp; keep warm.
2. Heat the oil in a wok or large skillet over very high heat until just smoking. Sear the beef in batches, 1–2 minutes, until browned outside but still pink inside. Remove to a plate.
3. Add the onion to the same pan and cook 1–2 minutes, until just softened but still a little crisp.
4. Add the garlic and ají amarillo paste, cooking 30 seconds until fragrant.
5. Add the tomato wedges, soy sauce, and vinegar, tossing 1–2 minutes until the tomato just starts to break down and release its juices.
6. Return the beef and the fries to the pan and toss everything together for 1 minute, just to combine and reheat — don't let the fries sit in the sauce long or they'll go soft.
7. Sprinkle with cilantro and serve immediately with white rice on the side.

### 2. Ají de Gallina (Creamy Shredded Chicken in Yellow-Chili Sauce)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken breast, boneless | 130g | 260g | 390g | 520g |
| White bread, crustless, torn | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Evaporated milk | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Walnuts or pecans, toasted | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Parmesan, grated | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Onion, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic clove, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ají amarillo paste | 1 Tbsp | 2 Tbsp | 3 Tbsp | 4 Tbsp |
| Chicken broth (reserved from poaching) | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Vegetable oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |
| Boiled potato, sliced, to serve | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Cooked white rice, to serve | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Hard-boiled egg, quartered (garnish) | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Black olives (garnish) | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |

**Steps:**
1. Poach the chicken breast in salted simmering water 15–18 minutes until cooked through; reserve some of the poaching liquid as broth. Once cool enough to handle, shred the chicken finely.
2. Soak the torn bread in the evaporated milk for 5 minutes until soft.
3. Blend the soaked bread (with its milk), toasted walnuts, and parmesan until smooth. Set aside.
4. Heat the oil in a pot over medium heat. Add the onion and cook 5–6 minutes until soft.
5. Add the garlic and ají amarillo paste, cooking 2 minutes until fragrant and deep gold.
6. Stir in the walnut-bread mixture and the reserved chicken broth, whisking until smooth.
7. Add the shredded chicken, stir to coat, and simmer 8–10 minutes, stirring often, until thick and creamy — it should look like a loose, glossy béchamel.
8. Season with salt to taste.
9. Spoon over sliced boiled potato and rice; garnish each plate with hard-boiled egg and black olives.

### 3. Ceviche Clásico (Citrus-Cured White Fish, No-Cook)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| White fish fillet (sea bass, tilapia, or halibut), cubed | 130g | 260g | 390g | 520g |
| Fresh lime juice | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Red onion, thinly sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Ají limo, rocoto, or serrano chile, thinly sliced | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Garlic clove, grated | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Cilantro, chopped | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 Tbsp | 4 tsp |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |
| Sweet potato, boiled, sliced, to serve | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Corn on the cob (choclo), boiled, to serve | ¼ ear | ½ ear | ¾ ear | 1 ear |

**Steps:**
1. Pat the fish cubes dry, place in a non-reactive bowl, and season generously with salt.
2. Add the garlic and half the red onion; toss to combine.
3. Pour over the lime juice, making sure all the fish is submerged. Let it cure 5–10 minutes at room temperature — just until the fish turns opaque; longer isn't better here, the fish can turn rubbery.
4. Add the sliced chile and cilantro, and toss gently.
5. Taste the citrus marinade (leche de tigre) and adjust salt — it should be bright, salty-sour, and a little spicy.
6. Pile the ceviche onto plates with plenty of the citrus liquid, and top with the remaining red onion.
7. Serve immediately alongside the boiled sweet potato and a piece of corn.

### 4. Arroz con Pollo Peruano (Green Cilantro Rice with Chicken)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bone-in | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Cilantro, roughly chopped (for blending) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Onion, chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Garlic clove, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ají amarillo paste | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 Tbsp | 4 tsp |
| Red bell pepper, diced | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Long-grain white rice | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Chicken stock | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Frozen peas | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Carrot, diced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Vegetable oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Salt and black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Blend the cilantro with a splash of the chicken stock until smooth; set aside.
2. Season the chicken with salt and pepper. Heat the oil in a pot over medium-high heat and brown the chicken 3–4 minutes per side. Remove.
3. In the same pot, add the onion, garlic, and bell pepper; cook 4–5 minutes until softened.
4. Stir in the ají amarillo paste and cook 1 minute.
5. Add the rice, stirring to coat it in the fat, then pour in the cilantro purée and the remaining chicken stock.
6. Nestle the chicken back into the pot, add the carrot, and bring to a simmer.
7. Cover and cook on low 18–20 minutes until the rice is tender and the liquid is absorbed.
8. Stir in the peas, cover, and rest off the heat for 5 minutes.
9. Fluff with a fork and serve hot.

### 5. Seco de Res (Beef &amp; Cilantro Stew)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beef stew meat (chuck), cubed | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Cilantro, roughly chopped (for blending) | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Onion, chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Garlic clove, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ají amarillo paste | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 Tbsp | 4 tsp |
| Dark beer (substitute for chicha de jora) | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Beef stock | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Carrot, sliced | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Frozen peas | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Potato, cubed | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Vegetable oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Salt and black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Blend the cilantro with a splash of beef stock until smooth; set aside.
2. Season the beef with salt and pepper. Heat the oil in a heavy pot over medium-high heat and brown the beef in batches, 3–4 minutes per side. Remove.
3. In the same pot, add the onion and garlic, cooking 5–6 minutes until soft.
4. Stir in the ají amarillo paste, cooking 1–2 minutes until fragrant.
5. Return the beef to the pot, pour in the beer, and let it bubble 1–2 minutes to cook off the raw alcohol smell.
6. Add the cilantro purée and beef stock, and bring to a simmer.
7. Cover and cook on low 35–40 minutes until the beef is nearly fork-tender.
8. Add the potato and carrot, cover, and cook 15 minutes more until the vegetables and beef are tender.
9. Stir in the peas and simmer uncovered 5 minutes to thicken slightly. Season with salt.
10. Serve hot with white rice.

### 6. Causa Rellena (Layered Chilled Potato with Chicken &amp; Avocado)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yellow or Yukon Gold potatoes, boiled and riced | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Ají amarillo paste | 1 Tbsp | 2 Tbsp | 3 Tbsp | 4 Tbsp |
| Fresh lime juice | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Vegetable oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Salt | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |
| Cooked chicken breast, shredded | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Mayonnaise | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Celery, finely diced | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Avocado, sliced | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Tomato, sliced | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Hard-boiled egg, sliced (garnish) | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Black olives (garnish) | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |

**Steps:**
1. Rice or mash the boiled potatoes while still warm until completely smooth and lump-free.
2. Mix in the ají amarillo paste, lime juice, oil, and salt until the potato is a vivid gold and holds together like a soft dough. Let it cool to room temperature.
3. Meanwhile, mix the shredded chicken with the mayonnaise and celery to make the filling.
4. Line a small loaf pan or ring mold with plastic wrap. Press in half the potato mixture in an even layer.
5. Layer the avocado slices over the potato, then spread the chicken filling on top, then the tomato slices.
6. Press in the remaining potato mixture to seal, smoothing the top.
7. Cover and refrigerate at least 1 hour, until firm and cold.
8. Unmold, slice, and garnish with the sliced hard-boiled egg and black olives just before serving.

### 7. Pollo a la Brasa with Ají Verde (Peruvian Roast Chicken, Green Sauce)
**Chicken & Marinade**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bone-in, skin-on | 180g | 360g | 540g | 720g |
| Garlic clove, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Soy sauce | 1 Tbsp | 2 Tbsp | 3 Tbsp | 4 Tbsp |
| Ají panca paste (or smoked paprika + pinch cayenne) | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 Tbsp | 4 tsp |
| Fresh lime juice | 1 Tbsp | 2 Tbsp | 3 Tbsp | 4 Tbsp |
| Ground cumin | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Dried oregano | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Vegetable oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 Tbsp | 4 tsp |
| Salt and black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |

**Ají Verde Sauce**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jalapeño, seeded and chopped | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Cilantro, roughly chopped | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Garlic clove | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Mayonnaise | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Queso fresco or feta, crumbled | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Fresh lime juice | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 Tbsp | 4 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Whisk together the marinade ingredients — garlic, soy sauce, ají panca, lime juice, cumin, oregano, oil, salt, and pepper.
2. Rub the marinade all over the chicken thighs, including under the skin. Marinate at least 30 minutes at room temperature, or up to overnight in the fridge.
3. Meanwhile, blend all the ají verde ingredients until smooth; taste and adjust salt or lime. Refrigerate until serving.
4. Heat the oven to 220°C (425°F), or heat a grill pan over medium-high.
5. Roast the chicken thighs skin-side up 30–35 minutes (or grill 6–7 minutes per side), basting once with the pan juices, until the skin is deeply burnished and a thermometer reads 74°C (165°F).
6. Rest 5 minutes, then serve with the ají verde sauce alongside.

### 8. Picarones (Fried Squash &amp; Sweet Potato Rings in Syrup) (Bonus)
*A note on this one:* traditional picarones use a full yeast rise and a proper deep-fry setup. This version keeps the same real technique but on a weeknight-friendly single rise — it's a legitimate fried dough, not a shortcut fake, it's just sized for a Tuesday instead of a street-cart batch.

**Dough**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Butternut squash, peeled and cubed | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Sweet potato, peeled and cubed | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| All-purpose flour | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Granulated sugar | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 Tbsp | 4 tsp |
| Instant yeast | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Anise seed | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Salt | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp |
| Warm water (as needed) | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Vegetable oil, for frying | as needed | as needed | as needed | as needed |

**Chancaca Syrup**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dark brown sugar, panela, or piloncillo, chopped | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Water | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Cinnamon stick | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Whole cloves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Orange peel strip | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |

**Steps:**
1. Boil the squash and sweet potato until fork-tender, about 15 minutes; drain and mash until smooth.
2. In a large bowl, mix the flour, sugar, yeast, anise seed, and salt. Stir in the mashed squash and sweet potato, adding warm water a little at a time, until you have a soft, sticky, elastic dough — about the consistency of a thick pancake batter you could still pinch into a ring.
3. Cover and let rise in a warm spot about 1 hour, until doubled and bubbly.
4. Meanwhile, make the chancaca syrup: simmer the chopped panela, water, cinnamon stick, cloves, and orange peel 10–12 minutes until syrupy. Strain and set aside.
5. Heat oil to 350°F (175°C) in a deep pot.
6. Wet your hand or a spoon, scoop a portion of dough, and shape it into a ring directly into the hot oil (or drop it as a rustic free-form dollop if shaping rings feels fiddly).
7. Fry 2–3 minutes per side until deep golden and crisp, working in batches so the oil temperature doesn't drop.
8. Drain on a rack or paper towels. Drizzle warm with the chancaca syrup and serve immediately.
