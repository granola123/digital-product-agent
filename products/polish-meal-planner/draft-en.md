**Dairy & Cultured:** twaróg (Polish farmer's cheese, or substitute dry curd cottage cheese/farmer's cheese), butter, sour cream, milk (if needed for mashing)

### 1. Pierogi Ruskie
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All-purpose flour (dough) | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Warm water | 45ml | 90ml | 135ml | 180ml |
| Egg (dough, beaten) | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Salt (dough) | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ⅜ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Neutral oil (dough) | 2ml | 4ml | 6ml | 8ml |
| Potatoes, peeled | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Twaróg (farmer's cheese) | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Onion, finely chopped (filling) | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Salt (filling) | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |
| Black pepper (filling) | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |
| Butter (filling + topping) | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Onion, sliced (topping) | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |

**Steps:**
1. Boil the potatoes in salted water until fork-tender, about 15 minutes, then drain and mash while still warm — no lumps.
2. Melt a third of the butter in a small pan over medium heat and sauté the finely chopped filling onion for 4–5 minutes, until soft and golden.
3. In a bowl, mash the twaróg into the potatoes until smooth, stir in the sautéed onion, and season with salt and black pepper. Taste and adjust — the filling should be well-seasoned since the dough itself is plain.
4. Make the dough: mix the flour and salt in a bowl, make a well, and add the warm water, beaten egg, and oil. Bring together into a smooth, slightly tacky dough, kneading 5 minutes. Cover and rest 15 minutes.
5. On a floured surface, roll the dough thin (about 2mm) and cut circles with a glass or cutter roughly 8cm across.
6. Place a spoonful of filling just off-center on each circle, fold over into a half-moon, and pinch the edges firmly to seal — no gaps, or they'll burst open in the water.
7. Bring a large pot of salted water to a gentle boil. Drop the pierogi in a few at a time so they don't stick together, and stir once gently.
8. Cook until the pierogi float to the top, then let them simmer 1–2 more minutes. Lift out with a slotted spoon and drain.
9. Melt the remaining butter in a wide pan over medium heat, add the sliced topping onion, and cook 6–8 minutes, stirring often, until deep golden brown and fragrant.
10. Add the drained pierogi to the pan and toss gently in the browned butter and onion until lightly crisped on the edges. Serve hot.

### 2. Bigos
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sauerkraut, drained | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Fresh white cabbage, shredded | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Pork shoulder, cubed | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Smoked kielbasa, sliced | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Bacon, diced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Dried wild mushrooms | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Pitted prunes, chopped | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Onion, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic, minced | 1 clove | 2 cloves | 3 cloves | 4 cloves |
| Bay leaves | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Allspice berries | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Tomato paste | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Neutral oil | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Water or stock | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Salt | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |
| Black pepper | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |

**Steps:**
1. Put the dried mushrooms in a small bowl, cover with warm water, and let them soak 20 minutes to soften. Drain, reserving the soaking liquid, and chop the mushrooms.
2. Heat the oil in a large, heavy pot over medium-high heat. Add the pork shoulder and bacon and brown on all sides, about 6–8 minutes, until no pink remains.
3. Add the onion and garlic and cook 3 minutes, stirring, until the onion softens.
4. Stir in the sauerkraut, fresh cabbage, chopped mushrooms (plus their strained soaking liquid), sliced kielbasa, prunes, bay leaves, and allspice berries.
5. Stir in the tomato paste and pour in the water or stock. Everything should be just barely covered by liquid — add a splash more if the pot looks dry.
6. Bring to a simmer, then reduce the heat to low, cover partway, and cook gently for 60–75 minutes, stirring every 15 minutes or so, until the cabbage is very soft and the flavors have melded. Add a little more water if it starts sticking.
7. Season with salt and black pepper to taste — bigos should taste rich and deeply savory, not sharp. Bigos also reheats well the next day, when many say it tastes even better.
8. Remove the bay leaves before serving. Serve hot with rye bread.

### 3. Kotlet Schabowy with Boiled Potatoes
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pork loin cutlet (schab), pounded thin | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Salt | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |
| Black pepper | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |
| All-purpose flour (dredging) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Egg, beaten | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Breadcrumbs | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Butter or neutral oil (frying) | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Potatoes, peeled | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Salt (for potato water) | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |
| Fresh dill, chopped (optional garnish) | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Place each pork cutlet between two sheets of plastic wrap and pound with a meat mallet to an even ¼-inch thickness. Season both sides with salt and black pepper.
2. Set out three shallow dishes: one with the flour, one with the beaten egg, and one with the breadcrumbs.
3. Coat each cutlet lightly in flour, shaking off the excess, then dip in the beaten egg, then press into the breadcrumbs until evenly coated on both sides.
4. Meanwhile, put the peeled potatoes in a pot, cover with cold salted water, and bring to a boil. Cook 15–18 minutes until a knife slides in easily, then drain.
5. Heat the butter or oil in a large skillet over medium heat until shimmering.
6. Fry the cutlets 3–4 minutes per side, until deep golden brown and cooked through with no pink remaining inside.
7. Drain briefly on a paper towel. Plate the cutlets alongside the boiled potatoes, and scatter fresh dill over the potatoes if using.

### 4. Żurek
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bacon or smoked pork, diced | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Onion, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Garlic, minced | 1 clove | 2 cloves | 3 cloves | 4 cloves |
| Potatoes, cubed | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Carrot, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| White or smoked kielbasa, sliced | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Chicken or vegetable stock | 300ml | 600ml | 900ml | 1200ml |
| Zakwas (fermented sour rye starter) | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Dried marjoram | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Bay leaf | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Salt | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |
| Black pepper | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |
| Hard-boiled egg | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Sour cream (optional, to finish) | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |

**Steps:**
1. In a soup pot over medium heat, render the diced bacon until the fat runs and the pieces turn golden and crisp, about 4 minutes.
2. Add the onion and garlic and cook 2–3 minutes until softened.
3. Pour in the stock, then add the potatoes, carrot, sliced kielbasa, bay leaf, and marjoram. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer and cook 15 minutes, until the potatoes are just tender.
4. Stir in the zakwas and simmer 5 more minutes — don't let it boil hard once the zakwas is in, or the tangy flavor turns flat.
5. Season with salt and black pepper to taste. The soup should taste pleasantly sour, savory, and slightly herbal from the marjoram — add a little more zakwas if it needs more tang.
6. Remove the bay leaf. Ladle into bowls, halve the hard-boiled egg(s) and nestle into each bowl, and finish with a drizzle of sour cream if using.

### 5. Gołąbki
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cabbage leaves, blanched | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Ground beef | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Ground pork | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Cooked rice | 75g | 150g | 225g | 300g |
| Onion, finely chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic, minced | 1 clove | 2 cloves | 3 cloves | 4 cloves |
| Butter or oil (for sautéing onion) | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Egg | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Dried marjoram | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Salt | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |
| Black pepper | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |
| Tomato passata or sauce | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Chicken stock | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Sugar (to balance the sauce) | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Core the cabbage and boil it whole for 3–4 minutes, then peel off the softened outer leaves one by one, returning the head to the water for another minute or two as needed until you've freed enough pliable leaves. Trim the thick center rib flat on each leaf so it rolls easily.
2. Melt the butter or oil in a small pan and sauté the finely chopped onion and garlic 3–4 minutes, until soft. Let cool slightly.
3. In a bowl, combine the ground beef, ground pork, cooked rice, sautéed onion and garlic, egg, marjoram, salt, and black pepper. Mix just until evenly combined — don't overwork it.
4. Place a portion of the meat mixture at the base of each cabbage leaf, fold the sides in over the filling, and roll up snugly into a tight parcel.
5. Arrange the rolls seam-side down in a wide pot, packing them in a single snug layer (stack a second layer if needed).
6. Stir the sugar into the tomato passata and pour it over the rolls along with the chicken stock — the liquid should come about halfway up the rolls.
7. Bring to a simmer, then cover and cook on low heat for 60–75 minutes, until the cabbage is completely tender and the filling is cooked through.
8. Spoon the sauce over the rolls when serving.

### 6. Placki Ziemniaczane
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Potatoes, grated | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Onion, grated | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Egg | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| All-purpose flour | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Salt | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |
| Black pepper | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |
| Neutral oil (for frying) | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Sour cream (for serving) | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Applesauce (for serving, optional) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |

**Steps:**
1. Grate the potatoes and onion on the fine side of a box grater (or in a food processor) directly into a colander set over a bowl.
2. Press and squeeze the grated potato and onion firmly to remove as much liquid as possible — this is what keeps the pancakes crisp instead of soggy. Let the squeezed-out liquid sit a moment, then pour off the water and keep any starch that settles at the bottom of the bowl, stirring it back into the potatoes.
3. Mix in the egg, flour, salt, and black pepper until you have a thick, cohesive batter.
4. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat until it shimmers.
5. Drop heaped spoonfuls of the potato mixture into the pan, flattening each slightly with the back of the spoon.
6. Fry 3–4 minutes per side, until deeply golden brown and crisp at the edges. Don't crowd the pan — fry in batches, adding more oil as needed.
7. Drain briefly on paper towels. Serve hot, topped with sour cream or applesauce (or both).

### 7. Kiełbasa z Kapustą
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kielbasa (smoked Polish sausage), sliced | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Sauerkraut, drained (or fresh cabbage, shredded) | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Onion, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Caraway seeds | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Bay leaf | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Apple, grated (optional) | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Neutral oil or lard | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Water or stock | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Salt | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |
| Black pepper | to taste | to taste | to taste | to taste |

**Steps:**
1. Heat the oil or lard in a wide skillet over medium heat. Add the sliced kielbasa and brown 2–3 minutes per side, until the edges crisp up. Remove to a plate.
2. In the same pan, add the onion and cook 3–4 minutes, until softened and starting to turn golden in the rendered fat.
3. Add the sauerkraut, caraway seeds, bay leaf, grated apple (if using), and water or stock. Stir to combine.
4. Cover and simmer 15–18 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the cabbage is tender and most of the liquid has cooked down.
5. Return the browned kielbasa to the pan and stir through, cooking 2–3 more minutes to heat it back up and let the flavors combine.
6. Season with salt and black pepper to taste. Remove the bay leaf before serving.

### 8. Sernik (Bonus)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All-purpose flour (crust) | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Butter, cold (crust) | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Sugar (crust) | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Egg yolk (crust) | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Salt (crust) | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Twaróg (farmer's cheese), ground | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Sugar (filling) | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Eggs (filling) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Butter, softened (filling) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Vanilla extract | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Cornstarch | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Raisins (optional) | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |

**Steps:**
1. Make the crust: in a bowl, rub the cold butter into the flour, sugar, and a pinch of salt until crumbly, then work in the egg yolk just until the dough comes together. Press it evenly into the bottom of a parchment-lined baking pan. Chill 15 minutes while the oven preheats to 160°C (325°F).
2. Bake the crust alone for 12–15 minutes, until just set and lightly golden at the edges. Let it cool slightly.
3. Make the filling: beat the twaróg, filling sugar, eggs, softened butter, vanilla extract, and cornstarch together until smooth and creamy (a stand mixer or hand mixer makes this easiest — twaróg should have no lumps left).
4. Stir in the raisins, if using, then pour the filling over the pre-baked crust and smooth the top.
5. Bake 50–55 minutes, until the top is golden and the center is just set with a slight jiggle (it firms up fully as it cools).
6. Turn off the oven and let the cheesecake cool inside with the door slightly ajar for 30 minutes, then move to a rack and cool completely to room temperature.
7. Chill at least 2 hours before slicing for the cleanest cuts — sernik tastes even better the next day.
