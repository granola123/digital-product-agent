## Grocery List (by category)

**Produce & Herbs:** onion (needed for nearly every dinner — buy in bulk), garlic, fresh ginger, carrots, potatoes, green bell pepper, yellow bell pepper, fresh tomatoes, dried apricots, raisins or sultanas, fresh chili (or chili flakes), fresh coriander/cilantro (optional garnish)

**Protein:** ground beef or lamb (for bobotie), boerewors (store-bought South African sausage), chicken thighs — a mix of bone-in skin-on and boneless (for potjie, bunny chow, Cape Malay curry, sosaties — buy in bulk), eggs

**Grains, Legumes & Starch:** mielie meal (maize meal, for pap — needed 3 times, buy a large bag), basmati or long-grain rice (for yellow rice, needed twice), 1 small crusty white bread loaf (for bunny chow), sliced white bread (for bobotie), all-purpose flour, canned baked beans

**Dairy & Pantry Staples:** milk, butter, heavy/pouring cream, curry powder, ground turmeric, ground coriander, garam masala, paprika, cinnamon stick, cardamom pods, bay leaves, apricot jam, fruit chutney (Mrs Balls-style or mango chutney), canned chopped tomatoes, Worcestershire sauce, white or white wine vinegar, apple cider vinegar (or more white vinegar), brown sugar, granulated sugar, baking soda, vanilla extract, slivered almonds (optional), chicken stock, cooking oil, salt, black pepper

## Recipe Cards

All ingredient tables below are scaled for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 servings.

### 1. Bobotie
Fragrant curried mince, sweetened with chutney and studded with raisins, baked under a golden, savory egg custard until it sets with a faint wobble — South Africa's most iconic dish, born from Cape Malay kitchens, and traditionally finished with a couple of bay leaves pressed into the custard before it bakes.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ground beef or lamb | 120g | 240g | 360g | 480g |
| Onion, finely chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Oil, for sautéing | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Curry powder | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Ground turmeric | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| White bread slice, soaked in milk | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Milk, for soaking bread | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Fruit chutney | 2 tsp | 4 tsp | 2 tbsp | 8 tsp |
| Raisins or sultanas | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Slivered almonds, optional | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Worcestershire sauce | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Vinegar | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Egg, for custard topping | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Milk, for custard topping | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Bay leaves, for topping | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Basmati or long-grain rice, to serve | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Turmeric, for yellow rice | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Preheat the oven to 170°C (340°F). Squeeze the excess milk from the soaked bread and mash it with a fork; set the bread and the reserved milk aside separately.
2. Ask an adult to help heat a little oil in a large pan and sauté the onion and garlic until soft.
3. Stir in the curry powder and turmeric and cook 1 minute until fragrant.
4. Add the mince and break it up with a spoon, cooking until no longer pink.
5. Stir in the mashed bread, chutney, raisins, almonds, Worcestershire sauce, vinegar, salt, and pepper. Simmer 5 minutes, then remove from the heat.
6. Spoon the mince mixture into a greased baking dish and press the top flat.
7. Whisk the egg with the milk for the custard and pour it evenly over the mince. Press the bay leaves into the surface.
8. Bake 30 minutes, until the custard is set and lightly golden on top.
9. While the bobotie bakes, cook the rice with a pinch of turmeric stirred into the cooking water until fluffy and golden.
10. Serve the bobotie hot with the yellow rice and extra chutney on the side.

### 2. Boerewors with Pap and Tomato-Onion Relish
Coiled, coriander-scented farmer's sausage charred over the coals, served the way every South African braai does it — alongside a mound of soft, buttery maize porridge and a sweet-sharp tomato relish spooned over the top.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boerewors (store-bought), coiled | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Mielie meal (maize meal) | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Water, for pap | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Salt, for pap | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Butter, stirred into pap | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Onion, sliced, for relish | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Tomatoes, chopped, for relish | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Brown sugar, for relish | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Vinegar, for relish | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Worcestershire sauce, for relish | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help heat a braai, grill, or grill pan to medium heat.
2. Grill the boerewors coil 20–25 minutes, turning occasionally, until browned all over and cooked through with no pink in the middle (or pan-fry if not grilling).
3. Meanwhile, make the relish: heat the oil in a pan, sauté the sliced onion until soft, then add the tomatoes, brown sugar, vinegar, and Worcestershire sauce. Simmer 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until jammy and thickened.
4. For the pap: bring the water and salt to a boil in a saucepan. Sprinkle in the mielie meal while whisking constantly to avoid lumps.
5. Reduce the heat to low, cover, and simmer 20 minutes, stirring every few minutes, until thick and smooth. Stir in the butter.
6. Slice the boerewors and serve over the pap with the tomato-onion relish spooned generously on top.

### 3. Chicken Potjie
A true potjiekos: chicken and vegetables layered — never stirred — into a cast-iron pot and left to simmer low and slow until everything is fall-apart tender and the flavors have melded into a rich, curry-scented gravy.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bone-in, skin-on, cut into pieces | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Onion, chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Curry powder | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Ground coriander | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Potatoes, chunked | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Carrots, chunked | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Green bell pepper, chunked | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Chicken stock | 80ml | 160ml | 240ml | 320ml |
| Oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help heat the oil in a potjie pot or heavy Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Brown the chicken pieces on all sides, then remove and set aside.
2. In the same pot, sauté the onion and garlic until soft. Stir in the curry powder and coriander and cook 1 minute until fragrant.
3. Return the chicken to the pot in a single layer, spice-side down.
4. Layer the potatoes, then the carrots, then the green pepper on top of the chicken, in that order — this is the golden rule of potjiekos: layer, don't stir.
5. Pour the stock gently down the side of the pot so it doesn't disturb the layers. Season with salt and pepper.
6. Cover and simmer very gently — low coals or the lowest stove setting — for 65–75 minutes without stirring, until the chicken and vegetables are tender.
7. Serve straight from the pot.

### 4. Bunny Chow
A quarter-loaf of white bread, hollowed out and filled to the brim with a thick, spiced chicken curry — Durban's most famous street food, invented by the city's Indian community and eaten with your hands, bread and all.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, boneless, cubed | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Onion, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic, minced | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Fresh ginger, grated | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Curry powder | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Ground turmeric | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Ground coriander | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Garam masala | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Canned chopped tomatoes | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Potato, diced | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Chicken stock or water | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Crusty white bread loaf, quarter, hollowed | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Sambal salad (grated carrot, chili, onion), to serve | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help heat the oil in a pot over medium heat. Sauté the onion until soft, then add the garlic and ginger and cook 1 minute.
2. Stir in the curry powder, turmeric, coriander, and garam masala, and cook 30 seconds until fragrant.
3. Add the chicken and stir to coat in the spices, cooking until sealed on all sides.
4. Add the canned tomatoes, potato, and stock. Cover and simmer 25–30 minutes, until the chicken is cooked through, the potato is tender, and the curry has thickened.
5. Season with salt and pepper.
6. Cut the bread quarters and hollow out the center of each, keeping the bread you remove for dunking.
7. Spoon the hot curry into the hollowed bread.
8. Serve with the sambal salad on the side and the reserved bread for scooping up the last of the curry.

### 5. Cape Malay Chicken Curry
A gently spiced, sweet-and-savory curry built on deeply caramelized onions, whole cinnamon and cardamom, and a handful of dried apricots that melt into the sauce — the signature style of Cape Town's Cape Malay community, milder and more fragrant than a fiery Durban curry.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bone-in | 200g | 400g | 600g | 800g |
| Onion, finely chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Garlic and ginger, grated | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Cinnamon stick | ¼ stick | ½ stick | ¾ stick | 1 stick |
| Cardamom pods | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Curry powder | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Ground turmeric | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Canned chopped tomatoes | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Dried apricots, chopped | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Potato, chunked | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Chicken stock | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Basmati or long-grain rice, to serve | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Turmeric, for yellow rice | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Cook the onion, stirring occasionally, for about 10 minutes until deeply golden and caramelized.
2. Add the garlic, ginger, cinnamon stick, and cardamom pods, and cook 1 minute until fragrant.
3. Stir in the curry powder and turmeric, cooking 30 seconds more.
4. Add the chicken pieces, turning to coat them in the spice paste, and let them brown lightly for a couple of minutes.
5. Add the canned tomatoes, dried apricots, potato, and stock.
6. Cover and simmer 45–60 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the chicken is tender and the sauce has thickened.
7. Season with salt and pepper. Fish out the cinnamon stick and cardamom pods before serving if you like.
8. Meanwhile, cook the rice with a pinch of turmeric stirred into the cooking water until fluffy and golden.
9. Serve the curry hot over the yellow rice.

### 6. Sosaties
Cubes of marinated meat threaded onto skewers with dried apricot and onion, grilled over the coals until caramelized at the edges — a Cape Malay braai classic that balances sweet apricot jam against sharp vinegar and warm curry spice.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thigh or lamb, cubed | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Dried apricots | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Onion, cut into chunks | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Green or yellow bell pepper, chunked | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Apricot jam | 1 tbsp | 2 tbsp | 3 tbsp | 4 tbsp |
| White wine vinegar | 1 tbsp | 2 tbsp | 3 tbsp | 4 tbsp |
| Brown sugar | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Garlic, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Curry powder | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Ground turmeric | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Bay leaf | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| Oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Pap or rice, to serve | 75g | 150g | 225g | 300g |

**Steps:**
1. Whisk the apricot jam, vinegar, brown sugar, garlic, curry powder, turmeric, bay leaf, and oil together in a small saucepan. Bring to a boil, then simmer 5 minutes. Let cool.
2. Thread the meat onto skewers, alternating with chunks of dried apricot, onion, and bell pepper.
3. Place the skewers in a dish, pour over the cooled marinade, cover, and refrigerate at least 2–4 hours (overnight gives the deepest flavor).
4. Ask an adult to help heat a grill or grill pan to medium-high heat.
5. Grill the skewers 10–12 minutes, turning occasionally and basting with extra marinade, until the meat is cooked through and lightly charred at the edges.
6. Serve hot over pap or rice.

### 7. Chakalaka and Pap
A punchy, chili-warmed vegetable relish built on carrots, peppers, and tomato, finished with a can of baked beans for body — spooned generously over a mound of soft maize porridge for a lighter, meat-free end to the week.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Onion, chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Garlic, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Fresh ginger, grated | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Carrots, grated or julienned | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Bell pepper, chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Tomatoes, chopped (fresh or canned) | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Curry powder | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Paprika | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Chili, chopped, or chili flakes | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Canned baked beans | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Oil | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Salt & black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Mielie meal (maize meal), for pap | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Water, for pap | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Salt, for pap | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help heat the oil in a pan and sauté the onion, garlic, and ginger until soft.
2. Add the carrots and bell pepper and cook 5 minutes until starting to soften.
3. Stir in the curry powder, paprika, and chili, and cook 30 seconds until fragrant.
4. Add the tomatoes and simmer 5–8 minutes until pulpy.
5. Stir in the baked beans, season with salt and pepper, and simmer 5 minutes more until heated through and slightly thickened.
6. Meanwhile, make the pap: bring the water and salt to a boil, whisk in the mielie meal, reduce the heat, cover, and simmer 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until thick and smooth.
7. Serve the chakalaka spooned generously over the pap.

### 8. Malva Pudding (Bonus)
A dark, spongy caramel pudding, sweetened with apricot jam and soaked through with a hot butter-cream sauce the moment it leaves the oven — impossibly moist, and the dessert on every South African family's Sunday table.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All-purpose flour | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Granulated sugar | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Baking soda | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Salt | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Egg | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Butter, melted | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Apricot jam | 1 tbsp | 2 tbsp | 3 tbsp | 4 tbsp |
| Milk | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| White or apple cider vinegar | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Vanilla extract | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Butter, for sauce | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Granulated sugar, for sauce | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Cream, for sauce | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Vanilla extract, for sauce | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Preheat the oven to 180°C (350°F). Grease a small baking dish.
2. Whisk the egg and sugar together until pale, then beat in the melted butter and apricot jam.
3. In a small cup, stir the vinegar into the milk (it will look slightly curdled — that's fine) and stir in the vanilla.
4. Sift the flour, baking soda, and salt together, then fold into the egg mixture alternately with the milk mixture until just combined.
5. Pour the batter into the prepared dish and bake 25–30 minutes, until golden brown and a skewer inserted in the center comes out clean.
6. While the pudding bakes, make the sauce: warm the butter, sugar, cream, and vanilla together in a small saucepan until the sugar dissolves and it's steaming hot (don't let it boil).
7. As soon as the pudding comes out of the oven, poke holes all over the top with a skewer and slowly pour the warm sauce over so it soaks all the way through.
8. Let it rest 10 minutes to soak up the sauce, then serve warm, with custard or ice cream if you like.
