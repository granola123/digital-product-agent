## Grocery List (by category)

**Produce & Aromatics:** onions (red and white), garlic, ginger, tomatoes, green chilies, leeks, cabbage, carrots, curry leaves (fresh, or frozen — a pantry non-negotiable for real Sri Lankan flavor), pandan leaves (rampe, fresh or frozen — substitute with extra curry leaves if unavailable), lemongrass stalks, lime

**Protein & Seafood:** chicken thighs (bone-in and boneless), chicken breast, firm white fish or tuna steaks (for ambul thiyal — traditionally tuna), eggs

**Grains, Starch & Bread:** rice flour, godamba roti or store-bought paratha/roti canai (for kottu — a reasonable home-cook swap for the hand-stretched original), white rice, all-purpose flour, cornstarch

**Coconut & Dairy:** coconut milk (thick, canned is fine), fresh or desiccated grated coconut (for pol sambol), whole milk (if substituting for coconut milk in a pinch)

**Spices & Flavorings:** roasted Sri Lankan curry powder (the dark-roasted blend — NOT the raw turmeric-forward curry powder found in most Western supermarkets; make your own by dry-roasting coriander, cumin, fennel, and curry leaves, or buy Sri Lankan-brand curry powder), chili powder, turmeric, cinnamon sticks (true Ceylon cinnamon if you can find it), cardamom pods, whole cloves, black peppercorns, mustard seeds, fenugreek seeds, dried red chilies, goraka (dried garcinia — the traditional souring agent for ambul thiyal; tamarind paste is the easiest substitute), tamarind paste, jaggery (kithul palm jaggery is the authentic choice; dark brown sugar or regular jaggery both work), nutmeg, cashews, soy sauce, tomato ketchup, salt, black pepper

## Recipe Cards

All ingredient tables below are scaled for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 servings.

### 1. Sri Lankan Chicken Curry (Kukul Mas Curry)
A deep amber-red curry built the real way — bone-in chicken marinated in dark roasted curry powder, then simmered low and slow with curry leaves, pandan, lemongrass, and warm baking spices until the sauce turns glossy and clings to the meat. Coconut milk folds in at the end for a rounded, nutty finish. This is the curry that defines a Sri Lankan Sunday table.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bone-in, cut into pieces | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Roasted curry powder | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Chili powder | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Salt (for marinade) | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Lime juice or vinegar (for marinade) | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Vegetable oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Onion, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ginger, minced | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Curry leaves | 6 | 12 | 18 | 24 |
| Pandan leaf, knotted | ¼ piece | ½ piece | ¾ piece | 1 piece |
| Lemongrass stalk, bruised | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Cinnamon stick | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Cardamom pods | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Whole cloves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Tomato, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Water | 50ml | 100ml | 150ml | 200ml |
| Coconut milk | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. In a bowl, toss the chicken with the roasted curry powder, chili powder, salt, and lime juice or vinegar. Let it marinate at room temperature for 20–30 minutes — the spices need a little time to soak in.
2. Ask an adult to help warm the oil in a heavy-bottomed pot over medium heat. Add the onion and cook 4–5 minutes, until soft and starting to turn golden at the edges.
3. Add the garlic and ginger and stir for about 30 seconds until fragrant.
4. Add the curry leaves, pandan leaf, lemongrass, cinnamon stick, cardamom pods, and cloves. Stir for 30 seconds to release their aroma — the kitchen should start to smell warm and spiced.
5. Add the marinated chicken and cook 4–5 minutes, turning the pieces, until the outside is no longer pink.
6. Stir in the chopped tomato and cook 2 minutes, until it starts to break down.
7. Pour in the water, season with the black pepper, cover, and simmer 20–25 minutes, until the chicken is tender and the sauce has thickened and turned a deep reddish-brown.
8. Stir in the coconut milk and simmer, uncovered, another 5 minutes — don't let it boil hard or the coconut milk can split.
9. Taste and adjust salt. Fish out the lemongrass, pandan, and cinnamon stick before serving. Serve hot with rice.

### 2. Kottu Roti (Chopped Roti Stir-Fry with Chicken)
The sound of kottu roti — two metal blades hammering flatbread against a hot griddle — is Sri Lanka's street-food soundtrack. Torn roti gets tossed with sautéed chicken, curry leaves, and shredded vegetables until every ribbon is coated in a smoky, curry-spiced glaze, some pieces soft, some charred and crisp at the edges.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Godamba roti or paratha (store-bought), torn into ribbons | 2 pieces | 4 pieces | 6 pieces | 8 pieces |
| Chicken breast or thigh, diced small | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Vegetable oil | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Onion, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Leek, sliced | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Cabbage, shredded | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Carrot, julienned | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ginger, minced | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Curry leaves | 6 | 12 | 18 | 24 |
| Roasted curry powder | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Chili powder | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Egg, beaten | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Soy sauce | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Lime wedges, to serve | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |

**Steps:**
1. Stack the roti and slice them into thin ribbons with a knife or kitchen scissors. Set aside.
2. Ask an adult to help heat the oil in a large flat pan or wok over medium-high heat. Add the onion, leek, garlic, ginger, and curry leaves. Cook 2–3 minutes, stirring, until fragrant and just starting to soften.
3. Add the diced chicken, curry powder, chili powder, and salt. Cook 5–6 minutes, stirring often, until the chicken is browned and cooked through with no pink left.
4. Push everything to one side of the pan. Pour the beaten egg into the empty space and scramble it for about 1 minute until just set, then mix it through the chicken.
5. Add the shredded cabbage and carrot. Toss for 2 minutes, until just starting to wilt but still a little crisp.
6. Add the torn roti ribbons and the soy sauce. Using two spatulas (this is the fun, noisy part), chop and toss everything together vigorously for 4–5 minutes, breaking up any roti clumps, until the ribbons are evenly coated and some edges look lightly charred.
7. Taste and add a little more salt or chili powder if needed.
8. Pile onto plates and serve immediately with lime wedges to squeeze over the top.

### 3. Parippu (Red Lentil Dal Curry)
Sri Lanka's everyday comfort food — bright orange-red masoor dal simmered until it collapses into a silky, spoonable curry, finished with a swirl of coconut milk. The magic happens at the very end: a sizzling tempering of mustard seeds, dried chilies, and curry leaves poured straight over the top, crackling as it hits the pot.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Red lentils (masoor dal), rinsed | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Water | 200ml | 400ml | 600ml | 800ml |
| Turmeric | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Onion, finely diced | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Green chili, slit | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Coconut milk | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Coconut oil or vegetable oil (for tempering) | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Mustard seeds | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Dried red chilies | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Curry leaves (for tempering) | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 |
| Small red onion, thinly sliced (for tempering) | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Fenugreek seeds | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Put the rinsed lentils, water, turmeric, diced onion, garlic, and green chili in a pot. Ask an adult to help bring it to a boil, then reduce to a simmer.
2. Cook, stirring occasionally and skimming off any foam, for 15–18 minutes, until the lentils are fully soft and starting to break down into a thick, porridge-like curry.
3. Stir in the coconut milk and salt. Simmer gently for 3–4 more minutes — don't let it boil hard once the coconut milk is in.
4. While the dal finishes, make the tempering: warm the oil in a small pan over medium heat. Add the mustard seeds and cover loosely — they'll pop and sputter, so stand back.
5. Once the popping slows, add the dried red chilies, curry leaves, sliced red onion, and fenugreek seeds. Fry for 1–2 minutes, until the onion is golden and the curry leaves are crisp and fragrant.
6. Pour the sizzling tempering straight over the finished dal — it should crackle audibly. Stir it in just before serving, or leave it pooled on top for looks.
7. Serve hot with rice or roti.

### 4. Fish Ambul Thiyal (Sour Sri Lankan Fish Curry)
A dry, dark, deeply savory curry from Sri Lanka's south — firm fish chunks coated in a thick paste of black pepper, garlic, and goraka (a sour dried fruit related to mangosteen) and cooked down with almost no added liquid until the sauce turns nearly black and clings to every piece. It's intensely peppery, sour, and unlike any other fish curry — traditionally cooked in a clay pot, which also helped preserve the fish for days without refrigeration.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Firm white fish or tuna, cut into chunks | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Goraka pieces, soaked (or 1 tsp tamarind paste per serving as substitute) | 2 pieces | 4 pieces | 6 pieces | 8 pieces |
| Warm water (to soak goraka) | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Garlic cloves | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Ginger, minced | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Black pepper, coarsely ground | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Chili powder | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Roasted curry powder | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Turmeric | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Curry leaves | 6 | 12 | 18 | 24 |
| Pandan leaf, torn | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Cinnamon stick | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Vegetable oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |

**Steps:**
1. Soak the goraka pieces in the warm water for 10 minutes, then blend or pound them (with the soaking water) into a rough paste. If using tamarind paste instead, just stir it with the same amount of warm water.
2. Ask an adult to help pound or blend the garlic, ginger, black pepper, chili powder, curry powder, and turmeric into a thick, fragrant paste. Combine with the goraka (or tamarind) paste.
3. Rub the spice-and-sour paste all over the fish chunks in a bowl, coating every piece.
4. Place the fish and any remaining paste into a heavy-bottomed pot (a clay pot if you have one) in a single layer. Tuck in the curry leaves, pandan leaf, and cinnamon stick, and sprinkle over the salt.
5. Drizzle the oil over the top. Do not add extra water — this curry is meant to be dry and thick, not soupy.
6. Cover and cook over low heat for 20–25 minutes, gently shaking the pot occasionally instead of stirring (stirring can break the fish apart), until the sauce has reduced to a thick, dark, clinging glaze and the fish is cooked through.
7. Taste carefully — it should be intensely peppery, sour, and salty all at once. Serve warm with plain rice; a little goes a long way.

### 5. Devilled Chicken
Sri Lanka's answer to sweet heat — crisp-fried chicken tossed through a glossy, fiery-red glaze of ketchup, soy sauce, and chili, loaded with charred onion petals, bell pepper, and tomato wedges. It's the dish that shows up at every Sri Lankan party and disappears first.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken breast or boneless thigh, cut into strips | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Cornstarch | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Soy sauce (for marinade) | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Salt (for marinade) | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Vegetable oil (for frying) | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Onion, cut into petals | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Green bell pepper, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Tomato, cut into wedges | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ginger, minced | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Curry leaves | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 |
| Green chili, sliced | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Chili flakes | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Soy sauce (for sauce) | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Tomato ketchup | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Black pepper | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Toss the chicken strips with the cornstarch, soy sauce, and a pinch of salt until evenly coated.
2. Ask an adult to help heat the oil in a wok or large pan over medium-high heat. Fry the chicken in batches for 4–5 minutes, until golden and crisp outside and cooked through with no pink inside. Drain on paper towels and set aside.
3. Pour off all but about 1 tablespoon of oil. Add the onion petals, bell pepper, garlic, ginger, and curry leaves. Stir-fry 2–3 minutes, until the vegetables are just starting to char at the edges but still crisp.
4. Add the tomato wedges, green chili, and chili flakes. Stir-fry 1 minute more.
5. Stir in the soy sauce, tomato ketchup, and black pepper to make the sauce.
6. Return the fried chicken to the pan and toss everything together for 1–2 minutes, until every piece is glossy and coated in the red-brown sauce.
7. Serve hot, straight from the pan, with rice or bread.

### 6. Egg Hoppers (Appa) with Coconut Sambol
A bowl-shaped rice-and-coconut pancake with a lacy, crisp golden edge and a soft, spongy center, cooked in a small curved pan until an egg cracked into the middle sets into a glossy sunny pool. Served with pol sambol — a sharp, coconut-and-chili relish that wakes up every bite.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rice flour | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Coconut milk | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Water | 40ml | 80ml | 120ml | 160ml |
| Instant yeast | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Sugar | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Salt (for batter) | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Eggs (for hoppers) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Salt & pepper (to season egg) | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |
| Fresh or desiccated grated coconut (for sambol) | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Red onion, finely chopped (for sambol) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Chili powder (for sambol) | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Lime juice (for sambol) | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Salt (for sambol) | pinch | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ½ tsp |

**Steps:**
1. In a bowl, whisk the rice flour, coconut milk, water, yeast, sugar, and a pinch of salt into a smooth, thin batter (about the thickness of heavy cream).
2. Cover and let the batter rest somewhere warm for 1–2 hours, until it looks a little bubbly and smells faintly fermented — this is what gives hoppers their tangy flavor and airy center (traditionalists ferment overnight, but a shorter rest still works well).
3. While the batter rests, make the sambol: mix the grated coconut, chopped red onion, chili powder, lime juice, and a pinch of salt together in a bowl, rubbing it with your fingers or the back of a spoon so the flavors blend into the coconut. Set aside.
4. Ask an adult to help heat a small, curved hopper pan (or a small nonstick wok) over medium heat. Pour in a ladle of batter and quickly swirl the pan so it coats the sides thinly, leaving a pool of batter in the center — this is what creates the lacy edge and soft middle.
5. Crack an egg into the center of the batter. Season with a little salt and pepper.
6. Cover with a lid and cook 3–4 minutes, until the edges turn golden and crisp and lift away from the pan on their own, and the egg white is set but the yolk is still soft.
7. Carefully loosen the edges with a spatula and slide the hopper out onto a plate — it should hold its bowl shape.
8. Repeat with the remaining batter and eggs. Serve hot with the coconut sambol spooned alongside.

### 7. Polos Curry (Green Jackfruit Curry)
Unripe green jackfruit, boiled until tender, simmered down in a spiced coconut milk gravy until it shreds apart with the texture of pulled meat. A beloved plant-based curry across Sri Lanka, rich with curry leaves, cinnamon, and a good hit of chili.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Young green jackfruit (canned in brine, drained, or fresh), cut into chunks | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Water (to boil jackfruit) | 200ml | 400ml | 600ml | 800ml |
| Turmeric | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Vegetable oil | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Onion, sliced | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Ginger, minced | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Green chili, slit | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Cinnamon stick | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Curry leaves | 6 | 12 | 18 | 24 |
| Roasted curry powder | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 3 tsp | 4 tsp |
| Chili powder | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Coconut milk | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Put the jackfruit chunks in a pot with the water and turmeric. Ask an adult to help bring it to a boil, then simmer 15–20 minutes, until fork-tender. Drain well.
2. Warm the oil in a pot over medium heat. Add the onion, garlic, ginger, green chili, cinnamon stick, and curry leaves. Cook 3–4 minutes, until the onion softens and everything smells fragrant.
3. Stir in the curry powder and chili powder. Cook for about 30 seconds, just until toasty-smelling.
4. Add the boiled jackfruit and stir to coat it in the spices.
5. Pour in the coconut milk and salt. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook, uncovered, for 10–12 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the curry thickens and the jackfruit has softened further.
6. Use the back of a spoon to lightly mash some of the jackfruit pieces against the side of the pot — this gives the curry its signature shredded, pulled texture while leaving some pieces whole.
7. Taste and adjust the salt or chili. Remove the cinnamon stick before serving. Serve hot with rice or roti.

### 8. Watalappan (Bonus — Coconut Jaggery Custard Pudding)
A silky, deeply caramelized custard — Sri Lanka's take on crème caramel — built from thick coconut milk, melted kithul palm jaggery, and eggs, warmed through with cardamom and nutmeg. Believed to have arrived with Sri Lankan Malay cooks generations ago, it's now the dessert of every celebration table.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kithul jaggery or dark brown sugar, grated | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Water (to melt jaggery) | 30ml | 60ml | 90ml | 120ml |
| Thick coconut milk | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Eggs | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Ground cardamom | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Ground nutmeg | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Cashews, chopped | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |

**Steps:**
1. Ask an adult to help warm the grated jaggery with the water in a small saucepan over low heat, stirring until fully melted and smooth. Let it cool slightly.
2. In a bowl, whisk the eggs until just combined (avoid whisking in too much air, or the custard will be bubbly instead of silky).
3. Whisk the coconut milk, cardamom, and nutmeg into the eggs.
4. Slowly whisk in the cooled jaggery syrup until fully combined.
5. Strain the mixture through a fine sieve into ramekins or a heatproof dish, to catch any lumps and keep the custard smooth. Sprinkle the chopped cashews over the top.
6. Set the ramekins in a steamer or a wider pan filled with an inch of hot water (a water bath). Ask an adult to help cover and steam over medium-low heat for 30–35 minutes, until the custard is set around the edges but still has a gentle wobble in the center.
7. Let the puddings cool to room temperature, then refrigerate at least 2 hours before serving chilled.
