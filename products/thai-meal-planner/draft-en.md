### 1. Chicken Pad Thai
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flat rice noodles | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Chicken breast | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Egg | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Bean sprouts | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Garlic chives | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Crushed peanuts | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Fish sauce | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Tamarind paste | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Palm sugar | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Lime wedges | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Oil (for stir-frying) | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |

**Steps:**
1. Fill a large bowl with warm (not hot) water and drop in the rice noodles. Let them soak about 10 minutes until they feel soft and bendy, like wet spaghetti — not stiff, not falling apart. Then drain them in a colander.
2. While the noodles soak, stir the fish sauce, tamarind paste, and palm sugar together in a small cup until the sugar mostly disappears. This is your sauce — set it aside for later.
3. Ask an adult to help turn on the stove. Put a splash of oil in a wok or big frying pan over medium-high heat. Wait about 30 seconds until the oil looks shiny and slides easily when you tilt the pan.
4. Add the chicken and spread the pieces out with a spatula so they aren't piled up. Let them cook without touching for 2 minutes, then flip and cook 2–3 minutes more, until there's no pink left when you cut a piece open.
5. Push all the chicken to one side of the pan. Crack the egg(s) into the empty space and scramble with your spatula, breaking them into small pieces, for about 1 minute until they turn from runny to solid and yellow.
6. Stir the chicken and scrambled egg together.
7. Add the drained noodles and pour your sauce cup over everything.
8. Using two spatulas or tongs, gently toss and stir like tossing a salad for 2–3 minutes, until every noodle turns light brown. If the pan looks dry and the noodles feel stiff, add a tiny splash of water.
9. Turn off the heat. Sprinkle in the bean sprouts and garlic chives and toss once more, just until they turn slightly soft but are still a little crunchy.
10. Scoop onto plates, sprinkle with crushed peanuts, and set a lime wedge on the side of each plate.
11. Squeeze the lime over your noodles right before eating — that bright, sour taste is what makes pad thai taste like pad thai!

### 2. Tom Yum Goong
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shrimp | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Stock or water | 300ml | 600ml | 900ml | 1200ml |
| Lemongrass (stalk) | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Galangal (slices) | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Kaffir lime leaves | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Mushrooms | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Cherry tomatoes | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Bird's eye chilies | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Fish sauce | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Lime juice | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Chili paste (nam prik pao) | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Steps:**
1. Pour the stock or water into a pot. Ask an adult to help you put it on the stove over medium-high heat.
2. While you wait, use the back of a knife or a rolling pin to gently smash the lemongrass stalk once — this cracks it open so its flavor can escape into the soup. Slice the galangal into thin coins if it isn't sliced already.
3. When small bubbles rise steadily from the bottom of the pot, that's a boil — add the lemongrass, galangal, and kaffir lime leaves. Let it boil gently for 5 minutes; your kitchen should start to smell amazing.
4. Add the mushrooms and cherry tomatoes. Let everything simmer — a gentle bubble, not a wild rolling boil — for 5 minutes, until the mushrooms look soft.
5. Add the shrimp. Shrimp cook fast, so watch closely: take the pot off the heat as soon as every shrimp turns from grey and see-through to solid pink and slightly curled, about 2–3 minutes. Overcooked shrimp go rubbery, so don't wait too long.
6. With the heat off, stir in the fish sauce, lime juice, and chili paste. Adding these off the heat keeps the lime juice tasting bright instead of turning bitter from boiling.
7. Carefully taste a spoonful (it's hot!). If it's too sour, add a little more fish sauce; if it's too salty, add a little more lime juice.
8. Ladle the soup into bowls, making sure everyone gets shrimp, mushrooms, and tomatoes. The lemongrass stalk and galangal slices are for flavor, not eating — leave them in or fish them out, like bay leaves.

### 3. Green Curry Chicken
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thigh | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Green curry paste | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Coconut milk | 100ml | 200ml | 300ml | 400ml |
| Thai eggplant | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Bamboo shoots | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Thai basil | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Fish sauce | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Palm sugar | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |

**Steps:**
1. Open the can of coconut milk without shaking it. Spoon just the thick, creamy layer from the top into your wok or pan — this is called "cracking" the coconut milk, and it should look thick like whipped cream, not runny.
2. Ask an adult to help turn the stove to medium heat. Add the curry paste to the thick coconut cream and stir for about 2 minutes. It's ready when it smells strong and toasty and looks shiny, with the color deepening and a glossy sheen forming on top.
3. Add the chicken and stir so every piece gets coated in the paste. Cook 2–3 minutes, just until the outside turns from pink to white — it doesn't need to be fully cooked yet.
4. Pour in the rest of the coconut milk and stir well until the curry turns a smooth, even green color.
5. Add the Thai eggplant and bamboo shoots. Turn the heat down so it's gently bubbling, not boiling hard, and cook about 10 minutes, stirring now and then, until the chicken is fully cooked (cut the biggest piece open — no pink inside) and the eggplant is soft when poked with a fork.
6. Stir in the fish sauce and palm sugar. Carefully taste the sauce — it should be salty, a little sweet, and a little spicy all at once.
7. Turn off the heat and tear in the Thai basil leaves with your hands, then stir once. The basil will wilt almost right away from the curry's heat.
8. Spoon the curry over a bowl of warm rice to serve.

### 4. Som Tam + Gai Yang
**Som Tam**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shredded green papaya | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Cherry tomatoes | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 |
| Green beans | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Garlic (cloves) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Chilies | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Palm sugar | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Fish sauce | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Lime juice | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Peanuts | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |

**Gai Yang**
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thigh | 120g | 240g | 360g | 480g |
| Garlic (cloves) | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Cilantro root | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| White pepper | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Fish sauce | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Oyster sauce | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |

**Steps:**
1. Gai Yang first, since it needs time to marinate: in a bowl, mix the crushed garlic, chopped cilantro root, white pepper, fish sauce, and oyster sauce to make a marinade.
2. Add the chicken thighs and rub the marinade all over every piece with your hands (or a spoon, if you don't want messy hands). Cover the bowl and let it sit at least 30 minutes — the longer it sits, even a few hours in the fridge, the tastier it gets.
3. Ask an adult to help heat a grill pan over medium heat. Lay the chicken in the pan with space between each piece so they aren't touching.
4. Cook 6–7 minutes on the first side without moving it, so it gets those brown, slightly charred marks. Flip and cook 6–7 minutes on the other side.
5. Check it's done by cutting into the thickest piece — it should be completely white inside with clear juices, no pink at all. Cook a few more minutes if needed.
6. Now the Som Tam: peel the green papaya and shred it into thin, noodle-like strips with a grater or julienne peeler (ask an adult to help — the grater is sharp).
7. In a mortar (a heavy bowl) with a pestle (a small heavy club for pounding), add the garlic and chilies first. Pound gently a few times just to bruise and break them open — you're not making a paste.
8. Add the green beans and cherry tomatoes and give a few more gentle pounds, just enough to bruise them and let their juices out.
9. Add the palm sugar, fish sauce, and lime juice, and stir it all together with a spoon.
10. Add the shredded papaya and peanuts. Switch to the spoon (not the pestle) and toss and press everything together for a minute so the papaya soaks up the dressing.
11. Taste a strand of papaya — it should be sour, salty, and a little sweet, with some heat. Add more lime juice or fish sauce if it needs it.
12. Slice the grilled chicken and arrange it on a plate next to a big pile of the papaya salad.

### 5. Pad Kra Pao (Thai Basil Chicken)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ground chicken | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Thai basil | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Garlic (cloves) | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 |
| Chilies | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Soy sauce | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Oyster sauce | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Fish sauce | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |
| Sugar | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Fried egg | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Oil (for stir-frying) | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Oil (for frying egg) | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |

**Steps:**
1. Finely chop the garlic and chilies — the smaller you chop them, the more flavor spreads through the dish. Ask an adult for help if you're still learning to use a knife.
2. Ask an adult to help heat a splash of oil in a wok or pan over medium-high heat until it looks shimmery.
3. Add the chopped garlic and chilies to the hot oil and stir constantly for about 30 seconds, just until you can smell the garlic. Don't let it turn brown or it will taste burnt and bitter.
4. Add the ground chicken. Use your spatula to break it into small crumbly pieces as it cooks, like breaking apart cookie dough. Keep stirring for 3–4 minutes until there's no pink left.
5. Pour in the soy sauce, oyster sauce, fish sauce, and sugar. Stir well so the sauce coats every piece of chicken evenly.
6. Let it cook one more minute so the sauce thickens slightly and sticks to the chicken instead of staying watery.
7. Turn off the heat. Add the Thai basil leaves and stir just until they wilt and turn a darker green — this only takes a few seconds.
8. In a separate small pan, fry an egg for each person: crack it into hot oil and cook until the white is fully set but the yolk is still soft and jiggly in the middle (cook longer if you like a firm yolk).
9. Scoop rice onto each plate, top with the basil chicken, and place a fried egg on top.

### 6. Massaman Beef Curry
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beef stew meat | 120g | 240g | 360g | 480g |
| Massaman curry paste | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Coconut milk | 120ml | 240ml | 360ml | 480ml |
| Potato | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Onion | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Roasted peanuts | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Tamarind paste | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Palm sugar | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Fish sauce | 5ml | 10ml | 15ml | 20ml |

**Steps:**
1. Open the coconut milk without shaking it and spoon the thick cream from the top into a heavy pot.
2. Ask an adult to help place the pot over medium heat. Add the curry paste to the coconut cream and stir for about 2 minutes, until it smells toasty and fragrant.
3. Add the beef and stir it in the paste for 1–2 minutes, just to coat it and seal the outside — it will turn from pink to brown on the surface.
4. Pour in the rest of the coconut milk, then add the potatoes and onion. Stir everything together.
5. Turn the heat down so the pot is just gently bubbling — a big rolling boil makes the beef tough instead of tender. Put a lid on it, leaving a small gap for steam to escape.
6. Let it cook slowly for 35–40 minutes, giving it a stir every 10 minutes or so to make sure nothing sticks to the bottom. It's ready when a fork slides into the beef easily, almost like cutting a soft baked potato.
7. Stir in the peanuts, tamarind paste, palm sugar, and fish sauce. Simmer 2 more minutes so the flavors mix in.
8. Carefully taste a spoonful — it should be rich, a little sweet, a little sour, and a little salty all together. Serve hot over rice.

### 7. Khao Soi
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Egg noodles | 80g | 160g | 240g | 320g |
| Chicken | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Khao soi curry paste | 25g | 50g | 75g | 100g |
| Coconut milk | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Fish sauce | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Pickled mustard greens | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Shallot | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Lime wedges | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |

**Steps:**
1. Spoon the thick cream off the top of the coconut milk into a pot, just like with the other curries.
2. Ask an adult to help heat the pot over medium heat. Stir the curry paste into the coconut cream for about 2 minutes, until it smells strong and toasty.
3. Add the chicken and stir to coat it in the paste, cooking 2 minutes until the outside isn't pink anymore.
4. Pour in the rest of the coconut milk and the fish sauce. Turn the heat down to a gentle simmer and cook 15–20 minutes, until the chicken is fully cooked all the way through (cut a piece to check — no pink inside). Taste and add a little more fish sauce if the broth needs more savory depth.
5. While the curry simmers, bring a separate pot of water to a boil. Cook most of the egg noodles following the package directions, until soft but still a little chewy, then drain.
6. Set aside a small handful of uncooked noodles for the crispy topping. Ask an adult to help heat a little oil in a small pan and fry this handful for 1–2 minutes, turning them, until golden and crunchy. Scoop onto a paper towel to drain.
7. Put a portion of the soft boiled noodles into each bowl and ladle the hot curry and chicken over the top.
8. Top each bowl with a pile of the crispy fried noodles, a spoonful of pickled mustard greens, some sliced shallot, and a lime wedge on the side.
9. Squeeze the lime over the soup right before eating for a fresh, tangy finish.

### 8. Mango Sticky Rice (Bonus)
| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glutinous rice | 50g | 100g | 150g | 200g |
| Ripe mango | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Coconut milk | 60ml | 120ml | 180ml | 240ml |
| Sugar | 12g | 24g | 36g | 48g |
| Salt | pinch | pinch | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Toasted sesame seeds | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Put the glutinous rice in a bowl, cover with water, and soak at least 1 hour (or overnight) — this softens the grains so they cook up soft and sticky instead of hard.
2. Drain the soaked rice well in a strainer. Ask an adult to help set up a steamer (a pot with a basket or rack above boiling water, not touching it) and line the basket with a thin cloth so the rice doesn't fall through.
3. Steam the rice 20–25 minutes, until every grain looks shiny, soft, and a little see-through, and tastes fully cooked — not chalky — when you try one.
4. While the rice steams, warm the coconut milk in a small pot over low heat with the sugar and a pinch of salt, stirring until the sugar completely disappears. Don't let it boil — just warm, like a bath.
5. Scoop out about a quarter of this warm coconut milk into a separate cup and set it aside — that's your topping for later.
6. When the rice is done, tip it into a bowl. Pour the bigger portion of warm coconut milk over the hot rice and gently fold it in with a spoon, like folding batter, until the rice has soaked up almost all the liquid. Let it sit 10 minutes to really absorb the flavor.
7. While you wait, peel the mango and slice it into long, neat pieces next to the pit.
8. Scoop the sticky rice onto plates using a spoon dipped in water (this stops the rice from sticking to the spoon), then fan the mango slices next to it.
9. Drizzle the reserved coconut milk over the rice and mango, and sprinkle toasted sesame seeds on top right before serving.
