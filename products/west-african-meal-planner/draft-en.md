## Recipe Cards

All ingredient tables below are scaled for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 servings.

### 1. Jollof Rice with Chicken
Glossy, smoky-edged grains of rice stained deep orange-red by a slow-fried tomato-pepper base, each grain separate but soaked through with flavor — this is the dish every Nigerian party is judged by, and the fight over the crispy "bottom pot" layer is half the fun.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bone-in skin-on | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Long-grain parboiled rice | 75g | 150g | 225g | 300g |
| Plum tomatoes, fresh or canned | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Red bell pepper (tatashe) | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Scotch bonnet pepper | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Onion, divided (blended + sliced) | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Vegetable oil | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Tomato paste | 10g | 20g | 30g | 40g |
| Garlic cloves, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Fresh ginger, grated | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Curry powder | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Dried thyme | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Bay leaf | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Chicken stock | 90ml | 180ml | 270ml | 360ml |
| Bouillon cube | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Season the chicken thighs with salt, a pinch of curry powder, thyme, half the garlic, and half the ginger. Set aside.
2. Blend the tomatoes, red bell pepper, scotch bonnet, and half the onion (roughly chopped) into a smooth puree.
3. Ask an adult to help heat the oil in a heavy pot over medium-high heat. Brown the chicken thighs on both sides, about 4 minutes per side, then remove and set aside (they don't need to be cooked through yet).
4. In the same pot, sauté the remaining onion (sliced) until soft, then stir in the tomato paste and fry 2 minutes.
5. Pour in the blended tomato-pepper puree. Fry over medium heat, stirring often, for 15–20 minutes until the mixture thickens noticeably, darkens, and the oil visibly separates and rises to the top — this step is what gives real jollof its depth, don't rush it.
6. Stir in the remaining curry powder, thyme, bay leaf, bouillon cube, and stock. Return the chicken to the pot and simmer 15 minutes, until the chicken is cooked through.
7. Lift the chicken out onto a plate. Stir the rice into the sauce so every grain is coated; the liquid should just barely cover the rice (top up with a splash of water or stock if it looks dry).
8. Cover tightly (a layer of foil under the lid helps seal in steam) and cook over low heat 20–25 minutes without stirring or lifting the lid, until the rice is tender and the liquid is fully absorbed. Letting the very bottom catch slightly gives the classic smoky "party jollof" flavor — keep the heat low so it doesn't burn.
9. Nestle the chicken back on top to reheat, fluff the rice gently with a fork, and serve.

### 2. Egusi Soup
A thick, savory stew built from ground melon seeds fried in red palm oil until nutty and rich, studded with tender goat or beef and wilted spinach — hearty, deeply savory, and made for scooping up with a starchy side like pounded yam or rice.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Goat meat or beef, cubed | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Ground egusi (melon seeds) | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Red palm oil | 20ml | 40ml | 60ml | 80ml |
| Onion, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Plum tomato/pepper, blended | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Scotch bonnet pepper | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Ground crayfish, optional | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Dried fish or stockfish, optional | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Stock or water | 120ml | 240ml | 360ml | 480ml |
| Fresh spinach, chopped | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Bouillon cube | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Season the meat with a little chopped onion, salt, and the bouillon cube. Ask an adult to help boil it in a pot with water to cover until tender, 25–35 minutes (goat takes longer than beef). Reserve the cooking stock.
2. In a small bowl, mix the ground egusi with a splash of water or reserved stock to form a thick, scoopable paste.
3. Heat the palm oil in a pot over medium heat, add the remaining onion, and sauté until soft.
4. Stir in the blended tomato and scotch bonnet, and fry 5 minutes until slightly thickened.
5. Drop spoonfuls of the egusi paste into the pot (this gives the classic soft lumps), or stir it in smooth if you prefer — either way, let it fry gently in the oil for 5 minutes to toast without burning, stirring occasionally.
6. Gradually pour in the reserved meat stock, stirring to loosen the egusi into a thick soup. Add the cooked meat, crayfish, and dried fish if using.
7. Simmer uncovered 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the egusi is fully cooked (no raw, gritty taste) and a layer of oil floats on top.
8. Stir in the chopped spinach and simmer 5 more minutes until just wilted. Adjust salt to taste and serve hot with rice, fufu, or pounded yam.

### 3. Suya
Thin-sliced beef, coated thick in a nutty, peppery ground-peanut spice blend called yaji and grilled hot and fast over open flame — the smoky street-food skewer that turns every Nigerian evening into an occasion, sold hot off the grill wrapped in newspaper.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beef sirloin or flank steak, thinly sliced | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Roasted peanuts, ground | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Cayenne pepper | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Paprika | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Ground ginger | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Garlic powder | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Onion powder | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Ground cloves | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Bouillon powder | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Vegetable oil, for basting | 1 tsp | 2 tsp | 1 tbsp | 4 tsp |
| Cabbage, sliced, to serve | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Onion and tomato wedges, to serve | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |

**Steps:**
1. Grind the roasted peanuts fairly fine (a spice grinder or mortar and pestle both work) — this is the base of the yaji spice.
2. Mix the ground peanuts with the cayenne, paprika, ginger, garlic powder, onion powder, cloves, bouillon powder, and salt until evenly combined.
3. Toss the sliced beef lightly with a little oil, then thread it onto skewers (soak wooden skewers in water first if using them).
4. Coat the skewers generously and evenly with the yaji spice mix, pressing it on with your fingers so it really sticks. Let rest at least 15–30 minutes (longer in the fridge, up to overnight, deepens the flavor).
5. Ask an adult to help heat a grill or grill pan to high heat.
6. Grill the skewers 2–4 minutes per side, basting lightly with oil, until charred at the edges and cooked to your liking.
7. Sprinkle a little extra yaji spice over the hot skewers right off the grill.
8. Serve with sliced cabbage, raw onion, and tomato wedges alongside.

### 4. Nigerian Groundnut (Peanut) Soup with Chicken
A silky, deep-orange soup thickened with roasted ground peanuts and red palm oil, gently spiced and simmered until the chicken falls tender — rich and nutty in a way that's completely different from Southeast Asian peanut sauces, and a true West African comfort food.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bone-in | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Raw peanuts, toasted and ground (or natural peanut butter) | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Red palm oil | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Onion, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Plum tomato/pepper, blended | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Scotch bonnet pepper | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Fresh ginger, grated | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Garlic clove, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Stock or water | 150ml | 300ml | 450ml | 600ml |
| Fresh spinach, chopped | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Bouillon cube | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Season the chicken with a little onion, salt, and the bouillon cube. Ask an adult to help boil it in water to cover until just tender, about 20 minutes. Reserve the stock.
2. If starting from raw peanuts, toast them in a dry pan over medium heat, stirring constantly, until fragrant and lightly browned, then grind to a rough paste in a food processor or with a mortar and pestle.
3. Heat the palm oil in a pot, add the onion, and sauté until soft. Stir in the blended tomato, scotch bonnet, ginger, and garlic, and fry 8–10 minutes until thickened.
4. Stir in the ground peanut paste (or peanut butter), mixing well and loosening it with a splash of the reserved stock.
5. Gradually whisk in the rest of the reserved stock, a little at a time, until smooth with no lumps.
6. Add the chicken pieces, cover partially, and simmer over low heat 25–30 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the soup thickens and a thin layer of oil rises to the surface.
7. Stir in the chopped spinach and cook 5 more minutes.
8. Adjust seasoning and serve hot over rice or with a side of fufu.

### 5. Waakye
Rice and black-eyed peas simmered together until the whole pot turns a deep reddish-brown from dried sorghum leaves, served heaped with a simple tomato stew, fried plantain, a boiled egg, and a scoop of gari — Ghana's favorite one-pot combo plate, sold from breakfast through dinner on every street corner.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dried black-eyed peas, soaked overnight | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Long-grain parboiled rice | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Dried sorghum/millet ("waakye") leaves, or pinch baking soda | small handful | small handful | 2 handfuls | 2 handfuls |
| Water | 350ml | 700ml | 1050ml | 1400ml |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Plum tomato/pepper, blended (for stew) | 40g | 80g | 120g | 160g |
| Onion, chopped (for stew) | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Vegetable oil (for stew) | 10ml | 20ml | 30ml | 40ml |
| Ripe plantain, sliced | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Oil, for frying plantain | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |
| Egg, boiled, to serve | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Gari, to serve, optional | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |

**Steps:**
1. Rinse the soaked black-eyed peas and place them in a pot with the water and the dried waakye leaves (if using).
2. Bring to a boil, then simmer 40–45 minutes until the beans are tender. Remove and discard the leaves — the cooking liquid should now look reddish-brown. (If you can't find the leaves, stir in a small pinch of baking soda instead for a similar deep color and soft texture.)
3. Rinse the rice and stir it into the pot with the beans. Check the water level comes just above the rice and beans, topping up if needed, then season with salt.
4. Cover and simmer over low heat 20–25 minutes, without stirring too much, until the rice is tender and the water is absorbed.
5. Meanwhile, make the stew: fry the blended tomato and onion in oil over medium heat 10–12 minutes, stirring occasionally, until thickened and the oil separates. Season with salt.
6. Ask an adult to help fry the plantain slices in oil over medium heat, about 2–3 minutes per side, until golden and caramelized.
7. Serve the rice and beans with a spoonful of stew, the fried plantain, a boiled egg, and a sprinkle of gari on the side.

### 6. Red Red
Black-eyed peas simmered until creamy in a red palm oil and tomato base, named for the double dose of red from the palm oil and the fried ripe plantain served alongside — sweet, savory, and spicy all in the same bite.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dried black-eyed peas, soaked (or 100g/200g/300g/400g cooked/canned) | 60g | 120g | 180g | 240g |
| Red palm oil | 20ml | 40ml | 60ml | 80ml |
| Onion, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Plum tomato, blended | 30g | 60g | 90g | 120g |
| Scotch bonnet pepper | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Fresh ginger, grated | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Garlic clove, minced | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Bouillon cube | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Ripe plantain | ½ | 1 | 1½ | 2 |
| Oil, for frying plantain | 15ml | 30ml | 45ml | 60ml |

**Steps:**
1. Cook the soaked black-eyed peas in fresh water with a pinch of salt until tender, about 35–40 minutes (or use drained canned/pre-cooked black-eyed peas to skip this step). Reserve a little of the cooking liquid.
2. Ask an adult to help heat the red palm oil in a pot over medium heat until it turns fluid and fragrant — don't let it smoke.
3. Add the chopped onion and sauté until soft.
4. Stir in the blended tomato, scotch bonnet, ginger, and garlic. Fry 8–10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until thickened and the oil starts to separate.
5. Stir in the cooked beans along with a splash of their cooking liquid. Simmer 10 minutes, seasoning with the bouillon cube and salt to taste.
6. Meanwhile, fry the plantain slices in oil 2–3 minutes per side until deeply golden and caramelized at the edges.
7. Serve the bean stew hot with the fried plantain alongside for scooping.

### 7. Nigerian Pepper Soup
A light, fiery, deeply aromatic broth built on Nigeria's signature pepper soup spice blend — warming, clean-tasting, and traditionally the first thing reached for when someone in the house has a cold, though it's easily good enough for any night of the week.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Goat meat or firm white fish, cubed | 150g | 300g | 450g | 600g |
| Onion, chopped | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Fresh ginger, grated | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Garlic clove, minced | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Scotch bonnet pepper | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Nigerian pepper soup spice mix (ground calabash nutmeg, uziza, grains of selim) | ½ tsp | 1 tsp | 1½ tsp | 2 tsp |
| Bouillon cube | ¼ | ½ | ¾ | 1 |
| Water or stock | 200ml | 400ml | 600ml | 800ml |
| Scent leaf or fresh basil, chopped | 5g | 10g | 15g | 20g |
| Salt | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |

**Steps:**
1. Season the goat meat with the onion, ginger, garlic, and salt in a pot, then add the water.
2. Ask an adult to help bring it to a boil, skimming off any foam that rises. Simmer 30–40 minutes until the goat is fork-tender (if using fish instead, skip this long simmer — fish only needs about 10 minutes total, added in step 3).
3. Stir in the scotch bonnet, pepper soup spice mix, and bouillon cube. Simmer 10 more minutes to let the spices fully infuse the broth.
4. Taste and adjust the salt and heat level to your liking.
5. Stir in the chopped scent leaf just before removing from the heat.
6. Ladle the hot broth and meat into bowls and serve as is, or with a side of steamed rice.

### 8. Chin Chin (Bonus)
Small, crunchy nuggets of nutmeg-scented fried dough, golden and shatteringly crisp all the way through — the snack that shows up in a bowl at every Nigerian party and gets eaten by the handful, no plate required.

| Ingredient | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All-purpose flour | 100g | 200g | 300g | 400g |
| Granulated sugar | 20g | 40g | 60g | 80g |
| Butter, softened | 15g | 30g | 45g | 60g |
| Milk | 20ml | 40ml | 60ml | 80ml |
| Baking powder | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Ground nutmeg | ¼ tsp | ½ tsp | ¾ tsp | 1 tsp |
| Salt | pinch | pinch | ⅛ tsp | ¼ tsp |
| Vegetable oil, for deep-frying | 500ml | 500ml | 750ml | 1000ml |

**Steps:**
1. In a bowl, whisk the flour, sugar, baking powder, nutmeg, and salt together.
2. Add the softened butter and rub it into the flour with your fingertips until the mixture looks like coarse crumbs.
3. Gradually add the milk, mixing just until a firm, non-sticky dough comes together. Knead briefly on a floured surface until smooth.
4. Roll the dough out to about ¼-inch (5mm) thickness.
5. Cut into strips, then cut crosswise into small squares or diamonds, about ¾-inch (2cm) pieces.
6. Ask an adult to help heat the oil in a deep, heavy pot to 325–350°F (165–175°C).
7. Fry the chin chin in small batches (don't crowd the pot) over medium heat, stirring occasionally, 5–8 minutes until deep golden and crunchy all the way through.
8. Remove with a slotted spoon, drain on paper towels, and let cool completely — they get crunchier as they cool.

## Recipe-Accuracy Pass Notes

Cross-referenced each dish against multiple reputable Nigerian/Ghanaian home-cooking sources before finalizing the recipes above (see final report for the one-line-per-dish summary). Key accuracy points already incorporated: red palm oil (not just vegetable oil) in Egusi Soup, Groundnut Soup, and Red Red, since it's essential to their real flavor and color, not an optional swap; genuine Nigerian pepper soup spice (calabash nutmeg/ehuru, uziza, grains of selim) rather than a generic "Italian" pepper-soup substitute; dried sorghum/millet ("waakye") leaves called out by name as the real source of Waakye's color, with baking soda noted only as a fallback substitute; yaji spice built on ground roasted peanuts (not just chili powder) for Suya; and the slow-fried, oil-separating tomato-pepper base ("frying the stew down") called out explicitly for Jollof Rice, since skipping that step is the most common way home cooks end up with mushy, under-flavored jollof.
